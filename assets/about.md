# About Mujmal al-Hikma App

This application is a reader and research environment for the *Mujmal al-Hikma* (Compendium of Wisdom), an anonymous Persian adaptation (or abridged translation) of the _Epistles of the Brethren of Purity_, based on an Arabic original. The text of the _Mujmal_ presented here is largely based on Muhammad-Taqi Danesh-pazhuh and Iraq Afshar's edition (Mujmal al-ḥikma, Tehran, 1996). However, editorial sections, such as footnotes and paragraph numbers have been removed from the text, and a working (machine) translation have been provided to assist those who do not read Persian. 

There are major differences between this work and the Arabic _Rasāʾil Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ wa Khullān al-Wafāʾ_ with many interventions on the part of the translator, such as omitting one entire epistle (_On Animals_), and combining and reframing others. The biggest liberty the translator has taken with Part IV, reducing it  to just one epistle. What remains of the epistles in this part form chapters that vaguely correspond to the original. This very curious choice reveals much about the author's intellectual commitments and taste.

 Otherwise, the general structure is the same as the _Rasāʾil Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ_, again with some confusion, especially in part IV. The author-translator is upfront about the alterations and interpolations. He claims his intention is clarifying cryptic language and removing superfluous and useless unsientific content. In some cases he quotes and adds from other philosophical texts at his disposal.

 So far my reading suggests that the original Arabic _Mujmal al-hikma_ on which this translation is based represented a faithful abridgement of the _Rasāʾil Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ_ if not a version of the _Rasāʾil_ under another title. Was the obscure title a deliberate choice for the Arabic abridgement? There were obvious reasons for dissociating the _Mujmal_, or at least the non-ideological part of it from the suspect anonymous authors. Ealiest manuscripts of the _Mujmal_ aslo diverge: some reference the _Rasāʾil_ and some do not. But it is likely, that both in the case of the original and the Persian abridgement, produced when the Nizari state of Alamut was still intact, the authors downplayed the Ismaili tenor of the epistles.
 
 Overall, the Persian Ikhwanian tradition is very rich and worth studying on its own, but it is also hoped that the study of the _Mujmal_ will contribute to our understanding of the more famous Arabic tradition.

### The App

This app was created by Aslisho Qurboniev (2025) as part of research on the _Ikhwān al-Safāʾ_ corpus. The text is presented in its original Persian with machine translation. Other features will be added in due course. All these materials are shared solely for the purpose of research and colaboration.

The application features:

- Original Persian text with English translation   
- Public Domain manuscript images for the two texts (MS. Laud Or.260  and Berlin Diez A oct. 132)
- Data visualizations of textual analysis. At the moment, there are only visualisations of the relative size of the works and their comparison.
- Full-text search capabilities

### The Text of the _Mujmal al-Ḥikma_
As mentioned the current digital text is based on I. Afshar and M.T. Danesh-pazhuh's 1996 edition. The edition, however, does not use the oldest manuscript and has some inexplicable editorial choices that are confusing. Most of the manuscripts that they consulted are not available to me, so I have used Istanbul Fatih 3178 mss (dated 645/1247) to make some corrections.

### The Working Translation
The AI translation was done using Deepseek R1, and I am well aware of the shortcomings. Only in some cases the translation has been amended or corrected by me, such as in the preface, and in the titles. I appended the machine translation to the text to provide the gist for those who are not familiar with Persian.
