### | فصل دوم 
حدّ اعتقاد ياد كرديم. اما تفسير و رسم وى آن است كه مردم طريقى به‌دست آورند كه معاش دنيا و معاد آخرت بدان آراسته توان داشت، و اگر بر شريعت پيغمبران و طريقت حكما بروند و آن فعل كنند كه فرموده‌اند در دنيا و آخرت زيانكار نباشند.
### || بيان اعتقاد حكما 
بداند كه جمله اهل حكمت متفق‌اند كه عالم نبود و پس ببود و او را صانعى هست حىّ‌ قادر حكيم عالم، و جسم نيست و جوهر نيست و عرض نيست و نتواند بود. و نخست فعلى كه از وى پديد آمد بر سبيل رحمت عقل بود و در علم او چنان بود كه اين جوهر پديد كند و علت عقل خود او بود نه چيزى ديگر. و فعل اول بارى همان بود و ديگر چيزها فعل فعل وى‌اند. و نفس فعل عقل بود. و هيولى فعل نفس بود. و آسمانها و كواكب فعل نفس و هيولى است. و در نفس آن رود كه صورت آن در عقل باشد. و در آسمانها آن رود كه صورت وى در نفس بود. و كواكب و افلاك زنده‌اند و حركت به ارادت كنند و قابل فساد نيستند. و عناصر از قوّت فلك است، و زمين و آب و هوا و آتش از حرارت و برودت و يبوست پديد آمد، و يبوست و حرارت از حركت پديد آمد، و رطوبت و برودت از سكون پديد آمد.

 و آنچه در افلاك رود «فعلى» باشد، و آنچه در زمين رود «انفعالى» باشد. و از [مركز] خاك تا محيط‍‌ افلاك جمله ممكن الوجودست و صانع اين جمله واجب الوجودست. و نفس ناطقه از عالم علوى است و آن نفس كل است و از آنجا آيد و باز آنجا شود، اگر نيك‌بخت باشد. و اعتقاد ايشان در حق انبيا آن است كه پيغمبرى ايشان واجب است و شريعت ايشان حق است و ببايد پذيرفتن، و بر هيچ پيغمبرى انكار نكنند و كتابها كه پيغمبران آورند جمله كلام خداى‌تعالى دانند، و گويند معرفت پيغمبران به موجودات همچون معرفت بينا بود به الوان گوناگون، و معرفت فيلسوف به موجودات چنان بود كه معرفت كور به‌واسطۀ لمس تا آن چيز را بداند. و چنانكه حكما به قياس و برهان حاجت دارند پيغمبران را بدان حاجت نباشد. و هرچه در عالم سفلى خواهد بودن از خير و شرّ پيغمبران در عالم ملكوت مى‌بينند، چنانكه كسى به رأى العين بيند. و نبوت يا به وحى باشد يا به الهام بود، يا به خواب، يا به آمدن رسول. و چون قوّت عظيم دارند ملك به صورت مردم بريشان پيدا شود و آنچه به كار بايدشان بياموزند، و معراج ايشان به نفس بود نه به بدن و هرگاه كه خواهند در معراج باشند. و هر كه شريعت ايشان را دست بدارد اهل حكمت او را معطل و بدبخت هر دو جهان خوانند.

### Chapter Two  
We have previously mentioned the definition of belief (*i‘tiqād*). Now, its explanation and delineation are as follows: people must adopt a path by which they may secure both worldly livelihood (*ma‘āsh*) and the hereafter (*ma‘ād*). If they follow the sacred law (*sharī‘ah*) of the prophets and the path (*ṭarīqah*) of the philosophers, and act according to their teachings, they shall suffer no loss in this world or the next.  

### || Exposition of the Philosophers’ Belief  
Know that all the people of wisdom (*ahl al-ḥikmah*) unanimously agree that the world did not exist and then came into being, and that it has a Maker—Living, Omnipotent, Wise, and All-Knowing—who is neither body, nor substance, nor accident, nor can He be described as such. The first act that emanated from Him, out of mercy, was the Intellect (*‘aql*). In His knowledge, it was decreed that this substance should come into being, and the cause of the Intellect was none other than Himself. The first act of the Creator was this, and all other things are secondary acts derived from it.  

The Soul (*nafs*) is the act of the Intellect, and Prime Matter (*hayūlā*) is the act of the Soul. The heavens and the celestial bodies are the acts of the Soul and Prime Matter. What occurs in the Soul corresponds to the forms present in the Intellect, and what occurs in the heavens corresponds to the forms present in the Soul. The stars and celestial spheres are alive, move by will, and are imperishable. The elements derive their power from the celestial sphere, while earth, water, air, and fire arise from heat, cold, and dryness. Dryness and heat arise from motion, while moisture and cold arise from rest.  

What occurs in the celestial realms is *active* (*fi‘lī*), while what occurs on earth is *passive* (*infi‘ālī*). From the center of the earth to the outermost celestial sphere, all is *contingent* (*mumkin al-wujūd*), and the Maker of all this is *Necessary Existence* (*wājib al-wujūd*). The rational soul (*nafs-i nāṭiqah*) belongs to the higher world (*‘ālam-i ‘ulwī*); it is the Universal Soul (*nafs-i kull*), from which it originates and to which it shall return—if it is fortunate.  

Their belief concerning the prophets is that prophethood is necessary, their sacred law is true and must be accepted, and no prophet should be denied. They hold that the scriptures brought by the prophets are all the speech of God, the Exalted. They say that the prophets’ knowledge of existent things is like the knowledge of a sighted man perceiving various colors, whereas the philosopher’s knowledge of existent things is like that of a blind man discerning through touch. Just as philosophers require syllogism and proof, the prophets have no need for such. Whatever is destined to occur in the lower world—be it good or evil—the prophets perceive in the realm of divine sovereignty (*‘ālam al-malakūt*), as clearly as one sees with the eye.  

Prophecy (*nubuwwah*) is either through revelation (*waḥy*), inspiration (*ilhām*), dreams, or the appearance of a messenger. Due to their immense spiritual power, an angel may appear to them in human form to teach them what they need to know. Their ascension (*mi‘rāj*) is with the soul, not the body, and they may ascend whenever they wish. Whoever abandons their sacred law is regarded by the philosophers as both idle and wretched in both worlds.  

### || On Sin  
In the view of the prophets and messengers, murder, theft, corruption, fornication, and sodomy are all sins. But in the doctrine of the philosophers, nothing is worse than harming creation—so much so that even if someone speaks falsely about the Necessary Existence but does not harm others, they may yet be saved. To them, harming others is a thousand times worse than unbelief (*kufr*). Even if a man is learned and close to the realm of divine sovereignty, harming others would still be a grave sin. Such is the belief of the philosophers—and if we have omitted anything, it may yet be inferred.
