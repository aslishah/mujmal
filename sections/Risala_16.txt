### Summary of the Second Epistle on Natural Philosophy Concerning the Heavens and the World  
In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful  

The purpose of this epistle is to discuss universal and simple bodies. Know that the world consists of the earth, animals, minerals, plants, wind, water, fire, the celestial spheres, the stars, absolute body, and the souls that sustain all these. These are divided into two categories: one is called the "Supernal World" (ʿĀlam-e ʿUlwī), also referred to as the "World of Order and Harmony" (ʿĀlam-e Nasq va Neẓām) or the "World of Divine Sovereignty" (ʿĀlam-e Malakūt). The other is what lies beneath the sphere of the moon, or the earth, which is called the "Sublunary World" (ʿĀlam-e Suflī). The sages who comprehend both these worlds call them "The Macrocosm" (al-ʿĀlam al-Insān al-Kabīr). When they observe that man is composed of both worlds, they call him "The Microcosm" (al-Insān al-ʿĀlam al-Ṣaghīr), for the heavens and the earth and all within them are body and soul, and man too is body and soul.  

Know that there is a single soul that sustains the entire world in a state of life, though each thing has its own form and shape. The soul that pertains to one form and shape may also manifest in another. Just as physicians describe the anatomy of the body, the philosophers describe the anatomy of this "Macrocosm," saying that all the bodies of the world are in reality a single body, though they appear in diverse forms.  

The forms of bodies are of two kinds: one is called the "constitutive form" (ṣūrat-e maqawwama), and the other the "complementary form" (ṣūrat-e mutammima). The constitutive form consists of length, width, and depth, which are common to all bodies. The complementary form may be circular, triangular, square, pentagonal, hexagonal, and so on. Among the complementary forms is motion in six directions, as well as circular motion in particular.  

Among the attributes of the complementary form is light, which is of two kinds: essential, like the light of the sun, and accidental, like the light of the moon, which is a reflection. Other attributes include color and transparency. Thus, we say the world is spherical, and the most excellent shape is the sphere, which is the origin of all shapes. All the stars are spherical, as we shall explain regarding the moon. The light of all stars is essential, except for the moon’s. All celestial bodies are transparent, except the earth and the moon, as we shall later mention.  

The heavens are called "samāwāt" because, wherever a person stands on the earth—whether in the east, west, north, or south—they see the dome of the sky above them and their feet directed toward the earth’s center. Hence, they are called "samāwāt" (heavens), and they are called "aflāk" (spheres) because they are circular.  

There are nine spheres: the closest to us is the sphere of the moon; above it is the sphere of Mercury; above that, the sphere of Venus; then the sphere of the Sun; then the sphere of Mars;  
-156-  
then the sphere of Jupiter; and above that, the sphere of Saturn—which our Prophet (peace be upon him) mentioned, and God the Exalted referenced in the Quran where He says, *"By the sky and the night comer—and what can make you know what the night comer is? It is the piercing star."* (Q 86:1–3). It is agreed that the "piercing star" is Saturn, whose light penetrates the seven heavens and reaches the icy moisture in our eyes, just as the image of something in water appears in our eyes.  

The eighth sphere is that of the fixed stars, and the ninth is the greatest sphere (al-falak al-aʿẓam), which encompasses all. Know that each lower sphere is the "earth" of the sphere above it, just as the sphere of Mercury is the "heaven" of the moon’s sphere, while Mercury’s sphere is the "earth" of Venus’s sphere. What we call "earth" could also be termed a "floor." Each higher sphere is the "heaven" of the one below it, as we have explained in the introduction to astronomy.  

These spheres are layered like onion skins, one above the other, without gaps. One should not imagine that there is void in the universe or that there exist bodies or spirits beyond what we observe.  

The proof that there is no void is as follows: void can only be conceived in two ways—either as a substance or as an accident. If it were a substance, it would not be void, and if it were an accident, it would depend on a substance, for an accident cannot subsist by itself. An "accident" is a state within a "substance," like whiteness in a garment or blackness in tar, whiteness in snow or camphor—it is not something separate from the substance.  

Some might argue that there is a space that is empty, but void cannot exist. Similarly, it is said that void must either be a place or not a place. If it were a place, it would be the place of a body, for there is no space without an occupant. If it were not a place, the term itself would be meaningless. If one claims that this void lies outside the universe—meaning beyond the spheres—even greater impossibilities arise, and the answer remains the same.  

Thus, we say that each star is the sovereign of its sphere, and that sphere is filled with angels of the same nature as that star. Each sphere is itself a world. The sun is the sovereign of all stars, and while there is debate over whether the sun’s sphere is nobler than the higher planets or the fixed stars, there is no doubt that the sun rules all stars and spheres.  

The reason the sun is not placed in the outermost sphere, where its station would be nobler, is that if the sun were in the fifth sphere, the existence of the world, man, animals, plants, and minerals would not endure. If it were in the third sphere, corruption would arise due to extreme cold and heat.  

In Greek astronomical texts, it is allegorically recounted that Jupiter interceded with the sun, saying, "Do not descend further, lest you burn creation with your light, nor ascend higher, lest our existence perish." We know by proof that when the sun is at its perigee, it scorches plants and animals, and when at its apogee, it brings moderation. If its perigee were in Gemini rather than Sagittarius, habitation in the northern regions would be difficult. Evidence for this is that in the southern regions, when the sun is at its perigee and Capricorn is at the zenith, there is no cultivation—the land there is barren. Similarly, some equatorial regions are uninhabited because the sun is closer there than in the north.  

Thus, it is established that the existence of all in the sublunary world depends on the sun. The spheres are like climes, the zodiac signs like cities, and the stars like their governors.

Know that the diameter of the earth is 2,164 farsakhs, and the greatest circle on the earth’s surface—that is, the equator—is 6,800 farsakhs. The diameter of the sphere of air is 53,832 farsakhs. The thickness of the moon’s sphere is several times that of the air’s sphere, Mercury’s sphere is 105 times the earth’s diameter, Venus’s sphere is 915 times, the sun’s sphere is several hundred times, and the sun’s mass is 166 and a quarter times that of the earth. Mars’s sphere is 7,656 times the earth’s diameter, Jupiter’s is 5,527 times, Saturn’s is 7,605 times, and the sphere of the fixed stars is approximately 12,000 times the earth’s diameter. These figures are recorded in astronomical texts.  

Our purpose in mentioning this is a single point—so that the wise may acknowledge it without denial—and it is this: it has been proven that every substance connected to the spheres, stars, earth, and what lies within them is also connected beyond the outermost sphere, permeating and passing through it. This is one premise.  

Another premise is that the heavens, the earth, and all that is more refined are better able to receive this power. An example is sunlight, water, glass, mirrors, and rusted iron—sunlight falls uniformly on their surfaces, yet their reception differs. The reflection of sunlight on crystal and rusted iron is not the same: iron receives fire,  
-159-  
while crystal receives light. Similarly, other substances differ.  

We give a medical analogy: Zayd is healthy, while ʿAmr is ill. Both eat the same food, yet it increases Zayd’s health and worsens ʿAmr’s sickness—the cause is the same food. This is another premise.  

Thus, we say that the reason man is receptive to the rational soul is the equilibrium of his nature and his celestial ascendant, as well as the power emanating from the spheres that connects to his balanced disposition. Through this receptivity, man attains thousands of virtues and distinctions.  

Among all bodies, those farthest from the simple substance and the outermost sphere—such as the earth and what lies upon it—are subject to decay, for nature has taken hold of them. How, then, could the spheres—which are the closest things to the simple substances, untouched by nature, and whose forms are free from materiality—be inanimate, without spirit or speech?  

The one who holds this belief is like a champion to whom a king gives great wealth for a construction project. The champion, in his pride, boasts to his family and close ones, "The king’s treasury is not as vast as mine!" Many such examples exist.  

Thus, it must be known that the sphere is called the "Fifth Nature" (al-ṭabīʿa al-khāmisa), and corruption cannot reach it in any way. The equilibrium of human nature, compared to this "Fifth Nature," is less than a speck of earth beside the outermost sphere and the simple substance.  

The powers of the prophets, messengers, and philosophers all derive from it. The more harmonious the configuration of the spheres at one’s birth, the more refined their nature and the greater their miracles.  

Everything on earth—animals and plants—lives by animal and vegetative life, but the spheres themselves are alive. The nobility of the stars over the spheres exceeds that of the spheres over the earth. Thus, it is clear that nothing in existence is nobler than the spheres and the stars. After the Necessary Being (al-Wājib al-Wujūd), the intellect, the soul, and the stars are alive, rational, and act by will—though all are governed.  

The spheres and their ranks are the exalted abode of the prophets and sages. Those referred to in religious law as the "Cherubic Angels" (al-malāʾika al-karūbiyyūn) are they, and they are called the "Bearers of the Throne" (ḥamalat al-ʿarsh).  

The purpose of this exposition is to make it known that if it is possible for man—who consumes plant seeds, is of clay, and is tainted by lust, anger, greed, arrogance, hypocrisy, malice, stubbornness, and disbelief, and is subject to nature’s command and coercion—to be alive, rational, and act by will, then the spheres and stars, which are free from such defects, are far more deserving of these attributes. This much suffices as a reminder.  

And God knows best.
