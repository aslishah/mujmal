--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION ---

### Chapter Five  
When the Necessary Existence (Wājib al-Wujūd) appoints such a person—by His grace—as the leader of creation and the vicegerent of the earth, it becomes incumbent upon him to summon the people and call them to God, the Exalted, and to establish a tradition (sunnah) and sacred law (sharīʿah) for that summons. However, since the adversary cannot find or accept miracles and signs in opposition to this claim, he denies it, saying, "This person is a sorcerer, a soothsayer, and a liar." Moreover, among them are sorcerers who, upon rejecting this person, engage in magic and deception, producing all manner of illusions, claiming, "The truth is what we say, not what the prophet says." In response to them, the sages have several clear arguments.  

-331-  
First, no prophet has ever arisen from the lineage of the lowly, nor do the lowly even possess a lineage or noble house. Second, a prophet is never ill-favored in appearance, nor does he commit acts that are repugnant to people—such as theft, deceit, bloodshed, effeminacy, lying, fornication, sodomy, or anything resembling these. Nor do such people ever emerge from the families of the prophets. Rather, a prophet is fair of face and stature, virtuous in character, noble in lineage, and magnanimous in spirit. Never has a prophet been ignorant or unjust. Whatever virtuous qualities exist among people, the prophet possesses them in greater measure. He does not seek evil, nor does he covet the wealth of others.  

A great sage among the Greeks once spoke regarding the signs of the prophets, saying: "If in my time a person appears whose lineage is noble, who is free from the blameworthy traits found in people, adorned with goodness, just in conduct, who bears his own burdens for the sake of the people and relieves them of their burdens, who is fair in face and stature, speaks with excellence, and claims prophethood—and if this person is not known to be a liar—then even without a miracle, I would accept his prophethood, for he could be nothing but a prophet. And whichever path such a person takes, that path is the path of God. Whoever denies his sacred law, their blood and wealth are lawful for him."  

Thus, know that the path to God, the Mighty and Glorious, is of two kinds: either in accordance with sacred law (sharīʿah) or in accordance with wisdom (ḥikmah). As for the path of sacred law, it is manifest and concealed from no one. But as for the path of wisdom, it too follows sacred law. For the sage, in relation to the prophet, is like the blind in relation to the sighted. The sage comprehends through analogy and the limits of certain sciences, while the prophet journeys day and night in the celestial realm with his pure soul and knows what is. If fairness is granted, and if bigotry and dishonesty are abandoned, it will be known that the one before whom a roasted lamb laced with poison is presented in discourse—compared to one who cannot distinguish poison from oil in a cup—[what merit does he possess? The truth is that the one before whom the roasted lamb is presented in discourse is the prophet, not the sorcerer, and the one who cannot distinguish poison from oil is the sage.] This proof is exceedingly clear to whoever accepts it.  

-332-  
Therefore, know that turning away from such a person is not the path of God, and denying him is neither wisdom nor philosophy.
