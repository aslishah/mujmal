--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION ---

### Chapter Five  

When the Necessary Being (Wājib al-Wujūd) appoints such a person—by His grace—as the leader of creation and the vicegerent of the earth, it is inevitable that he calls the people to faith and summons them to God, the Exalted. He establishes a tradition (Sunnah) and a divine law (Sharīʿah) upon that call. However, the adversary, unable to produce or accept miracles and signs, denies this and claims, "This man is a sorcerer, a soothsayer, and a liar!" Moreover, among them are sorcerers who, upon rejecting this person, engage in magic and deception, producing all manner of illusions, saying, "The truth is what we proclaim, not what the Prophet declares."  

In response to them, the philosophers have several clear arguments:  

**First**, no prophet has ever arisen from lowly lineage—indeed, the lowly have no true lineage or noble house.  

**Second**, a prophet is never ill-favored in appearance, nor does he commit acts repugnant to people—such as theft, trickery, bloodshed, effeminacy, lying, fornication, sodomy, or anything of that nature. Nor do such people ever emerge from the families of prophets. Rather, a prophet is fair of face, well-proportioned in stature, virtuous in character, of noble descent, and magnanimous in spirit. A prophet is never ignorant nor unjust. Every noble quality found among people is found in the prophet to an even greater degree. He does not seek evil, nor does he covet the wealth of others.  

A great sage among the Greeks once spoke regarding the signs of prophets, saying: *"If in my time a person appears whose lineage is noble, who is free from the blameworthy traits found in people, adorned with virtue, just in conduct, who bears the burdens of the people and lifts their afflictions from one another—who is fair in face, stature, and demeanor, who speaks wisely and claims prophethood—and if this person is not known to be a liar, then even without miracles, I would accept his prophethood, for he could be nothing but a prophet. Any path such a person follows is the path of God, and whoever denies his law, their blood and wealth are lawful to him."*  

Therefore, know that the path to God, the Mighty and Majestic, is of two kinds: either by way of divine law (Sharīʿah) or by way of wisdom (Ḥikmah).  

As for the path of divine law, it is evident and hidden from no one.  

As for the path of wisdom, it too follows divine law. For the philosopher in relation to the prophet is like the blind in relation to the sighted. The philosopher grasps some knowledge through analogy and deduction, but the prophet journeys day and night in the realm of the unseen with his purified soul and knows what truly is.  

If fairness is granted, and one does not resort to disbelief and dishonesty, it becomes clear that the one before whom a roasted lamb speaks in discourse is not the same as one who cannot distinguish poison from oil in a cup. [What merit is there in this? The truth is that the one before whom the roasted lamb speaks is a prophet, not a sorcerer, while the one who cannot tell poison from oil is merely a philosopher.] This is a profoundly clear proof for those who would accept it.  

Therefore, know that turning away from such a person is not the way of God, and denying him is neither wisdom nor philosophy.
