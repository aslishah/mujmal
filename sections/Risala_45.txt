بداند كه حق‌تعالى از جود خويش و عنايت تمام جوهرى بسيط‍‌ را بيافريد نورانى عالم فعال و مردم آن را به وقتى «عقل فعّال» گويند، و به وقتى «نفس» خوانند، و به وقتى «جوهر مفرد» خوانند، و به وقتى «طبيعت» خوانند و آن به افعال بگردد. پس از وجود اين نفس جوهر آمد، و آسمانها و ملكوت آمدند، و عناصر و زمين و آنچه در وى است پديد آمد. چنانكه چند جاى گفتيم. پس ابتدا [ى] حركت كه از آن طبيعت خاست از ميان نفس و هيولى آمد، و حرارت از حركت پديد آمد، و بعد از حركت سكون پديد آمد، و از سكون برودت پديد آمد، و از برودت رطوبت پديد آمد، و ازين چهار عنصر پديد آمد.و اين هرچهار از كثافت زير فلك قمر بماندند. و خاك از همه ثقيلترست به مركز آمد، و آب گرد وى درآمد، و هوا گرد آب درآمد، و آتش گرد هوا درآمد. و از تأثير هر چهار عناصر و خاصيت فلك در زمين حيوان و نبات و معادن پديد آمد. و طبيعت كه ابتدا [ى] حركت بود تدبير گرديدن گرفت، و زمانهاى بسيار بگذشت و انسان نبود. فلك به خاصيت خويش و طبيعت به لطافت نفس كلى مزاجى پديد كرد به غايت اعتدال و از آن مزاج انسان پديد آمد. پس چون مزاج او به اعتدال بود «نفس كل» و «عقل فعّال» درو تدبير كردند و نطق پديد آمد و نسل انسان پيوسته كرد، و ازيشان خاص و عام پديد آمد و قبيله‌هاى بزرگ برخاست. و از تأثيرها [ى] كواكب و خاصيت ايشان كه در قرانها و دورها پديد شد طلب

### Chapter Four: On the Nature of Prophets and Their Miracles  
Know that God the Exalted, out of His boundless generosity and perfect grace, created a simple luminous substance—the active world and its inhabitants. At times it is called the "Active Intellect," at times the "Soul," at times the "Simple Substance," and at times "Nature," for it governs all actions. From the existence of this soul-substance emerged the heavens and the celestial dominion, the elements, the earth, and all that lies within it—as we have mentioned in several places. Thus, the inception of motion, arising from nature, emerged from between the soul and prime matter. Heat arose from motion, stillness from motion, cold from stillness, and moisture from cold—and from these four, the elements were born. These four remained beneath the sphere of the moon due to their density. Earth, the heaviest of them, settled at the center; water encircled it, air encircled water, and fire encircled air. Through the interaction of these four elements and the influence of the celestial sphere upon the earth, animals, plants, and minerals came into being.  

Nature, which initiated motion, began to govern, and vast ages passed without the existence of man. The celestial sphere, by its inherent properties, and nature, through the refinement of the universal soul, produced a temperament of perfect equilibrium—and from that temperament, man emerged. Since his temperament was balanced, the "Universal Soul" and the "Active Intellect" governed within him, giving rise to speech. The lineage of man became continuous, from which both the elite and the common arose, and great tribes were established. Through the influences of the stars and their properties—manifested in cycles and epochs—they sought kingship, ruled the world, and prophets appeared, calling people to God and establishing sacred laws.  

Know, then, that no prophet has ever arisen except from a noble lineage—all were either of royal descent or the offspring of prophets. The origin of their tribe lies in a cycle or epoch when a single individual rises to power, seizes dominion, subjects the people to his rule, exacts tribute, and crushes any who defy him, leaving none to oppose him. Such a person is called the "Lord of the Cycle." His progeny then spreads, as in our current epoch with Adam (peace be upon him). Wherever his descendants settled, their lineage endured, and from among them great figures emerged until another Lord of the Cycle arose. The effect of this cycle and the secret of this epoch necessitate the appearance of a prophet—born from the noblest place and lineage, under the ascendant of the cycle, so that its influence manifests in him.  

As for why others born under the same ascendant do not attain his station, this must be understood from astrological texts. The miracles bestowed upon such a person stem from the grace of the Necessary Being, attached to him by no other cause. The tenth, seventh, and ninth houses of his ascendant, along with their ruling planets, possess immense power. This person becomes the trustee of God and the vicegerent of the Exalted Truth upon the earth. The power of his soul grows so great that he acts upon the four elements, and an angel appears to him in human form. Through him, the foundation of divine law and tradition is established; the angel speaks with him, the people of the world submit to him, they accept his religion, and he attains sovereignty. The power and properties of the celestial sphere become innate to him—his body a sovereign among bodies, his soul a sovereign among souls. His reign endures according to the ruling planet of his ascendant, his wisdom, and his felicity.  

-330-  
And until the end of that reign, whoever opposes his house or abandons him will suffer the misfortunes of the age, becoming destitute, wretched, and debased—nothing will go right for him. As long as he lives, he will remain in torment, and when he dies and his soul departs, his fate will hang in peril—as we shall discuss in the chapter on the afterlife. Therefore, if a sage or philosopher encounters such a person, he must submit to him and choose obedience.  

Once, I debated with a great figure of the age regarding the miracles of the Prophet and the validation of divine law. He replied, "Do not speak foolishly—for if a sorcerer or illusionist were to appear and the people accept him, would it not be necessary to submit to him, especially if he were a prophet, the Lord of the Cycle?" This statement contains a clear allusion. There are decisive proofs regarding the essence of prophethood and its nature, but the discourse would lengthen, and that is a matter for another place. And God knows best.
