### | خلاصۀ رسالۀ پنجم در جغرافيا و آن صور اقاليم سبعه است

 و قومى گفته‌اند كه سبب ايستادن زمين اينجا آن است كه زمين ثقيل است و به برهان درست است كه زيرزمين آن نقطه است كه ياد كرديم. پس چون زير آنجا باشد هرچه ثقيل بود ثقل او را به زير مى‌برد. پس زمين و هرچه در وى است ثقل ايشان را به زير نقطه مى‌كشد، و آب كه ثقل وى كمتر است از زمين زير مى‌كند تا پيرامن زمين درآمده است، و هوا كه از آب سبكترست گرد آب در آمده است. و دليل برين آن است كه اگر ما سنگى را بر بالا اندازيم با زير آيد به قوت خويش و طلب مركز خويش كند، و همچنين اگر چيزى چون كدوى يا مشكى يا كوزه‌اى پر باد ميان آب فرو برند قرار نگيرد و قصد كند و به مركز خويش بازگردد به ستم. و دليل ديگر آن است كه زمين كرى است از آنكه همۀ اجزاى وى قصد مركز مى‌كند، و اگر نه چنين بودى شايستى كه زمين مربع يا مسطح بودى، يا شكلى از شكلهاى ديگر. و قول چهارم آن است كه خداى‌تعالى او را بدين موضع مخصوص كرد چنانكه هر كوكبى را در فلك به‌جاى خويش مخصوص كرد. و ناقل گويد كه اگر كسى خواهد كه بداند كه زمين چرا در ميان فلك ايستاده است شيشه‌اى بكند از آبگينۀ صافى و يك مشت «ريگ مكى» در شيشه كند و آب صافى درو كند و در «خرط‍‌» نهد و ساكن مى‌گرداند تا ببيند كه آن ريگ در ميان شيشه چگونه جمع شود. پس گوئيم گرد زمين آب است الا اين ربع كه او را «ربع مسكون» گويند و بيشتر ازين ربع هم آب دارد. و بعضى از آن كوههاست و بعضى واديها و بعضى بيابانها كه معروف است، و اين ربع در جانب شمال افتاده است. و درين ربع هفت درياى بزرگ است، و در هر درياى جزير [ه] هاى بسيارست.هر جزيره از بيست فرسنگ تا صد فرسنگ تا هزار فرسنگ. و يكى از آن درياى روم است و در آن پنجاه جزيره است. ديگر درياى صقالبه است و در وى سى جزيره است. و سؤم درياى گرگان است و در وى پنج جزيره است.

### Summary of the Fifth Epistle on Geography, Concerning the Configuration of the Seven Climes  
In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.  

It is incumbent upon the wise to understand the nature of the Earth and its attributes so that they may ascend from the lower to the higher realms until certainty is attained through proof. For it is possible to proceed from the sensible to the intelligible—though this may involve dialectical reasoning. Thus, we say that the Earth stands suspended in the air, surrounded by water, which in turn is enveloped by air, and fire encircles the air. The Earth is divided into seven climes, and these seven climes lie within one quadrant of the Earth, known as the "Inhabited Quarter."  

The largest circle that divides this terrestrial sphere into two equal halves is the great circle called the Equator, corresponding to the celestial Equator. The circumference of this circle is approximately 20,400 miles or 6,800 farsangs, and its diameter is the diameter of the Earth, which is about 6,500 miles or 2,167 farsangs—these are approximate values. The center of this sphere is a single point at the midpoint of its diameter. There is no "below" the Earth in the way the ignorant imagine, supposing that water flows downward to some lower side. Indeed, in elementary instruction, such a conception may arise, but the true "below" the Earth is that point which is its center. Geometry demonstrates that a sphere has no intrinsic "above" or "below." Rather, wherever a person stands, their feet point toward the center, and their head toward the celestial sphere. Thus, if one stands in the east and another in the west, the "below" of each would align with the other.  

The half of the celestial sphere concealed from us by the Earth is called "below," and whatever inclines toward the Earth's center is "below" something else, while something else is "above" it—just as the "below" of all things is the central point we have described. The surfaces of the Earth are its "above," and the air surrounds it from all sides, with the celestial sphere encompassing the air. Therefore, the "below" of the Earth is its central point, not any other place. This is the meaning of God’s words: "Then We reduced him to the lowest of the low" (Quran 95:5). Thus, wherever a person stands on Earth, they see half of the celestial sphere, and when they move to another place, some of the sphere becomes hidden while other parts become visible.  

The compiler of this book remarks: I have consulted the works of the masters regarding how many farsangs correspond to one degree of the celestial sphere on Earth, and opinions vary. Some say twenty farsangs, others twenty-one, and still others twenty-two. In this book, nineteen farsangs are given, and I have made no alteration to it, for even if one exerts great effort, this can only be known approximately.  

The reason for the Earth’s suspension in the air is explained in four opinions:  
1. Some say the celestial sphere draws it equally from all sides, keeping it balanced in the middle.  
2. Others argue that the celestial sphere, being nobler (spiritual), cannot attract the Earth (which is base and corporeal), but rather repels it with a specific force, maintaining its central position.  
3. A third group holds that the Earth’s heaviness causes it to settle at the center, as proven by the fact that all heavy things move downward toward the center. Thus, the Earth and its contents are drawn toward the central point, while water, being less heavy, surrounds it, and air, lighter still, envelops the water. Evidence for this is that when a stone is thrown upward, it falls back down by its own force, seeking its center. Likewise, if a buoyant object like a gourd or an inflated bladder is submerged in water, it will not remain submerged but will rise back to its natural place. Another proof is that the Earth is spherical because all its parts tend toward the center; were this not so, it would be square, flat, or some other shape.  
4. The fourth opinion is that God has placed the Earth in this position just as He has assigned each star its place in the heavens.  

The compiler adds: If one wishes to understand why the Earth remains suspended in the celestial sphere, let them take a clear glass vessel, fill it with pure water, and add a handful of Makkan sand. Then let them stir it gently and observe how the sand gathers at the center of the vessel.  

We say that water surrounds the Earth except for the "Inhabited Quarter," though even this quadrant contains much water. Some parts are mountains, others valleys, and others deserts. This quadrant lies in the northern region and contains seven great seas, each with many islands ranging from twenty to a thousand farsangs in size. These seas are:  
1. The Roman Sea (Mediterranean), with fifty islands.  
2. The Sea of the Slavs, with thirty islands.  
3. The Sea of Gurgan, with five islands.  
4. The Red Sea (Qulzum), with fourteen islands.  
5. The Persian Sea, with seven islands.  
6. The Sea of India and Sind, with nearly a thousand islands.  
7. The Sea of China, with two hundred islands.  

Additionally, there are eleven smaller seas in this quadrant, each spanning twenty to a thousand farsangs. The Western Sea, the Sea of Gog and Magog, the Sea of Zanj, the Green Sea, and the Encircling Ocean are not counted among these, as they are part of the surrounding ocean.  

In the Inhabited Quarter, there are about two hundred mountains, stretching east to west or north to south, ranging from twenty to a thousand farsangs in length. Some are in seas, others in deserts or islands. There are also 240 great rivers, flowing from these mountains into the seas. This quadrant contains approximately seventeen thousand major cities and is never without a thousand kings.  

The division of the climes is not natural but conceptual, with each clime associated with a planet. Their farsang measurements vary due to the Earth’s curvature, making some climes longer and others shorter. The first clime is the largest, the seventh the smallest.  

### The Configuration of the Seven Climes  
1. **First Clime (Saturn)**: Extends east to west, 3,000 farsangs long and 150 farsangs wide. It begins at the Isle of Yaqut, passes through southern China, northern Sri Lanka, central India, Sind, the Persian Sea, Yemen, the Red Sea, central Ethiopia, the Nile, Nubia, Barbary cities, and ends at the Western Sea. Its inhabitants are mostly black.  
2. **Second Clime (Jupiter)**: 8,600 miles long, 400 miles wide. Passes through central China, Sri Lanka, northern India, Kabulistan, southern Makran, the Persian Sea, central Arabia, the Red Sea, northern Ethiopia, southern Egypt, Africa, Barbary, and Qayrawan. Its people are dark-skinned.  
3. **Third Clime (Mars)**: 9,000 miles long, 350 wide. Traverses northern China, Gog and Magog, Turkistan, Kabulistan, Kandahar, Sistan, Kerman, Persia, Iraq, northern Arabia, Syria, Egypt, Qayrawan, and Tangier. Its inhabitants are wheat-complexioned.  
4. **Fourth Clime (Sun)**: 7,800 miles long, 300 wide. Covers northern China, Gog and Magog, Turkic lands, Tokharistan, Balkh, Makran, Sijistan, Iraq, the Arabian Gulf, Syria, the Mediterranean, Cyprus, Alexandria, Qadisiyya, Qayrawan, and Tangier. Its people are brown and white, of balanced temperament. This is the clime of prophets and sages.  
5. **Fifth Clime (Venus)**: 7,400 miles long, 250 wide. Passes through Gog and Magog, Farghana, Sughd, the Oxus, Khurasan, Sijistan, Kerman, Persia, Rayy, Iraq, Azerbaijan, Armenia, the Byzantine Gulf, the Mediterranean, Greece, Andalusia. Its people are fair-skinned.  
6. **Sixth Clime (Mercury)**: 7,000 miles long, 200 wide. Covers Gog and Magog, Sistan, Khagan lands, Kimak, Sughd, Transoxiana, Khwarazm, Gurgan, Tabaristan, Gilan, Azerbaijan, Armenia, the Black Sea, Constantinople, Qaliqala, the Slavic Sea, Andalusia. Its people are ruddy and fair.  
7. **Seventh Clime (Moon)**: 6,600 miles long, 185 wide. Passes through Gog and Magog, Sijistan, the Alans, the Gurgan Sea, the Caucasus, the Caspian Gates, the Slavic Sea. Its people are mostly blond.  

It is said that a Persian king once ordered a count of notable cities, yielding seven thousand, excluding villages and small towns. These variations arise from planetary conjunctions, sometimes auspicious, sometimes inauspicious. Were it not for these celestial influences, the world’s affairs would not differ. If the causes of good and evil were not the stars and spheres, the Earth and its conditions would be uniform—no poverty or wealth, sickness or health. For all change derives from the celestial agent, while the Necessary Being is beyond change.  

The Quran attests to this: "These are the days We alternate among the people" (3:140), and "None comprehends it except the knowledgeable" (29:43). Thus, the rise and fall of nations, prosperity and ruin, stem from celestial motions, not from the immutable Divine Essence.  

Similarly, we affirm that the celestial sphere and its contents are living, volitional, rational, and articulate, devoid of base nature or color. The Earth and its contents, however, are subject to these influences—this is evident.  

No more can be added to this epistle. And God knows best.
