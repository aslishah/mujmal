بسم اللّه الرحمن الرحيم 
چند جاى گفتيم كه آنچه تحت قمر آيد چهار جنس‌اند: عناصر و معادن و نبات و حيوان. اما عناصر از جملۀ بسايط‍‌ است و سخن درين رساله در مركبات است و آن سه جنس است: معادن و نبات و حيوان، و اين اجناس انواع شوند به سبب خاصيتى كه دريشان است به «فصل ذاتى» و خاصۀ فصل ذاتى چون كوتاهى، و «خاصه» چون خنده مردم را. و گفتيم كه «نفس كلى» از وراى جملۀ كاينات پيوسته است و محيط‍‌ بر همه و مدبّر همه. پس چون عناصر به سبب حركت فلك و كواكب حركت كنند لابد بود كه در زمين از آن تولدات پديد آيد به حسب حركت آن. و در حال كه پديد آيد از «نفس كلى» قوّتى بدو پيوندد و آن را «نفس جزوى» خوانند. و اين حال كه پديد آيد يا در معادن بود يا در نبات يا در حيوان. و گفتيم كه بودن معدنيها چگونه است. اما آن وقت كه «نفس جزوى» به وى پيوندد آنگه بود كه مزاج زيبق به كبريت باشد و تا آن وقت كه آن آب در آن غار يا در آن كان قرار گرفته بود، چنانكه آب در كوزه به وقت فروريختن. و نبات يا به

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION ---

Here is the English translation of the selected epistle from the *Rasā’il Ikhwān al-Ṣafā’* (Epistles of the Brethren of Purity), maintaining its scholarly tone and philosophical nuances:

---

### **Summary of the Tenth Epistle on Natural Sciences Concerning the Conception of the Embryo**  
*In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful*  

We have previously stated that all that exists below the sphere of the Moon comprises four genera: the elements, minerals, plants, and animals. The elements are among the simple substances, whereas this epistle concerns the compound substances, which are three: minerals, plants, and animals. These genera are further divided into species due to the specific properties within them—through their *essential differentia* (like shortness) and *specific attributes* (like human laughter).  

We have also affirmed that the *Universal Soul* transcends all creation, encompassing and governing all things. Thus, when the elements are set in motion by the movement of the celestial spheres and stars, it is inevitable that corresponding generations arise on Earth in accordance with their motion. At the moment of generation, a power from the *Universal Soul* attaches to it, which is called the *Partial Soul*. This occurs whether the generation is in minerals, plants, or animals.  

We have explained the nature of mineral generation. The attachment of the *Partial Soul* to minerals occurs when quicksilver (mercury) combines with sulfur, just as water settles in a cave or a mine, akin to water poured into a vessel.  

Plants are generated either from seeds or spontaneously, while animals are generated either through reproduction or spontaneous formation (*takwīn*). Each of these has a determined moment when a specific degree of the celestial spheres necessarily rises—its ascendant (*ṭāliʿ*)—such as when a seed is cast into the earth, a brick is laid in construction, a saw is applied to wood, or semen is deposited in the mother’s womb. Thus, the occurrence of this state, the attachment of the *Partial Soul* from the *Universal Soul*, and the degree of the ascendant are all one, regardless of place.  

Plants and minerals share this temporal determination with humans—indeed, with all animals—but humans are distinguished by their balanced temperament and the actualization of their latent potentialities, which other animals lack due to partial causes beyond comprehension.  

At the moment when human semen is emitted into the womb, there is a precise ascendant degree in the celestial sphere, and the configuration of the stars and zodiacal signs at that time imparts meaning to the embryo. This configuration determines the fate of the person born from that semen. The same applies to all animals, as they are equal to humans in this regard. For instance, when a stallion mates and a foal is conceived, and then later mates again to produce another, one may be fortunate and the other unfortunate—their fortune or misfortune being well-known.  

However, throughout human life, the moment of conception carries a slight influence of auspiciousness (*saʿd*) or inauspiciousness (*naḥs*), good or evil. For example, when a man intends to unite with his mate, heat arises within him, his blood warms and surges until it produces foam, which gathers. The more abundant and complete this foam, the greater his pleasure until the moment it is ejaculated from the penis into the woman’s womb.  

At that very moment, a vegetative power from the *Universal Soul* attaches to the semen, and a precise degree of the celestial ascendant is present. The sphere, through its own essence, assumes a specific form, and powers from the sphere and the stars enter and combine with the semen.  

Once the semen settles, menstrual blood surrounds it, coloring it. The gathering of blood around the semen is like the gathering of egg white around the yolk. The heat of the semen, blood, and womb then cook it, as is the habit of nature, until the foam coagulates into a clot (*ʿalaqa*) due to the blood and semen’s heat.  

From the moment of conception until the first month passes over the clot, Saturn (*Zuhal*) holds dominion. Most scholars hold that this period is one month, beginning with Saturn because his sphere is the highest among the planetary spheres, and his soul is connected to the higher world—the abode of the archangels, the source of revelation, the seat of all blessings, the vast Throne, the foundation of the Divine Pedestal (*ḥamalat al-ʿarsh*), the cause of prophethood in prophets, and the wisdom of philosophers. Thus, beginning with Saturn is most fitting.  

During this month, the semen changes its state, taking on the color of blood, yet no movement arises within it, for though Saturn is the cause of cohesive power, he is not the cause of life. Life arises from heat and moisture, whereas Saturn is cold and dry—the cause of death. Thus, the semen remains motionless during this period, and though it takes on the color of blood, it does not mix with it, remaining still due to Saturn’s prevailing coldness.  

When the second month arrives, Jupiter (*Mushtarī*) assumes governance, his sphere lying beneath Saturn’s. In this month, his influence prevails. From Jupiter’s spiritual power, a power arises in the clot, and a moderate heat emerges within it. The two (heat and moisture) mix, producing a slight trembling or quivering movement. During this time, the pregnant woman becomes aware of the child’s presence, and this trembling continues throughout Jupiter’s governance until another month passes.  

In the third month, Mars (*Mirrīkh*) takes control. His heat intensifies the existing warmth, and the trembling becomes stronger due to the dominance of Mars’ spiritual forces. The entire mass becomes a reddish piece of flesh, and heat becomes firmly established within it until another month passes.  

In the fourth month, the Sun (*Shams*) assumes governance. The spirit (*rūḥ*) manifests within the embryo, moving by its own essence. Know that the Sun is the soul of the lower world, the heart of the higher world, the chief of the planets, and the cause of the existence of plants, animals, and minerals. The Sun is itself pure spirit, having no body—though one may say it has a body in the sense that its "body" refers to its form, which is sustained by its soul. Though the Sun’s power derives from celestial substances, it is alive by itself, not by anything else, for it is absolute spirit.  

When the Sun governs, its spiritual powers attach to the embryo, and the animal soul (*jān-i ḥayawānī*) arises within it. This applies to all animals, but humans are unique. From the moment the semen enters the woman’s womb, the Sun must necessarily be in a specific position. During this solar governance, three zodiacal signs have already passed, and in this fourth month, the Sun does not contend with another sign. These four signs correspond to the four temperaments, and the Sun’s heat is the cause of all things. The signs the Sun traverses are: one fiery, one earthy, one airy, and one watery—thus completing the four temperaments.  

When the spiritual powers attach to the embryo, the natural soul (*jān-i ṭabīʿī*) enters it, completing its formation. At this stage, it is said to possess two senses (*ḥiss*).  

When this order progresses, Venus (*Zuhrah*)—the lesser benefic—takes her turn. She is the master of forms and images. In this stage, the fetus is fully shaped, with complete features: eyes, ears, mouth, nose, tactile organs, the two excretory passages, and joints. The fetus now lies with both knees drawn to the chest, head resting on the knees, hands on the face, and arms at the sides—yet it has no sensation to perceive this discomfort. Its navel remains connected to the mother’s navel, drawing nourishment until the day of birth.  

If the child is male, its face is toward the mother’s back; if female, toward the mother’s belly.  

When governance passes to Mercury (*ʿUṭārid*), its spiritual powers attach to the fetus, causing it to stretch its limbs in the womb, sometimes moving, sometimes resting. It opens its eyes, moves its tongue, breathes, sleeps, and wakes.  

By the seventh month, the Moon (*Qamar*) assumes governance. Its spiritual powers attach to the fetus, which now grows plump, its back strengthens, and it ceases movement, aware of the confinement of its space. If the celestial configuration at conception indicates birth in this period—and if the stars are favorable—the child emerges from the mother’s womb, with the Sun in the seventh sign.  

If it does not emerge then, governance returns to Saturn in the eighth month, with the Sun in the eighth sign—the house of death for conception. In this month, the child grows heavy, movement ceases, and cold dominates. If born now, it will die; if not, its life will be short. Many are stillborn in this month.  

If birth does not occur then, Jupiter—the greater benefic—assumes governance in the ninth month, with the Sun in the ninth sign (the house of travel and transition), in trine with the conception ascendant. Two auspicious influences combine: Jupiter’s beneficence and the Sun’s favorable aspect. The child’s temperament becomes balanced, for the Sun has doubly reinforced the four powers within it.  

The reason humans rarely live beyond 120 years—though this is conventional, not demonstrative—is that the Sun has traversed 240 degrees of the celestial sphere, leaving 120. All sages consider 120 the natural lifespan, though most live only half that (60 years), and fewer reach 70. Astrological texts elaborate on this.  

We affirm that the stars exert clear influences on the sublunary world, especially on humans, though these influences vary due to:  
1. Differences in their celestial motions (ascension, declination, apogee, perigee).  
2. Their inclination and latitude (north or south).  
3. Their direct or retrograde motion.  
Many other distinctions are detailed in *Almagest* (*Kitāb al-Majisṭī*).  

Thus, if Saturn governs first and is favorably placed—free from malefic aspects—the semen is shielded from harm, and its coldness does not distress the mother. If Saturn is exalted, the embryo lies higher in the womb, safe from pain and disease. If in Jupiter’s terms, the mother is at ease, unaware of pregnancy, free from cravings. If in Mars’ terms, she is energetic, refusing others’ help. If in Venus’ terms, she is joyous, especially with her spouse. If in Mercury’s terms, she is vigilant and shrewd, knowing immediately if conception has occurred, keeping daily accounts.  

But if Saturn is afflicted, retrograde, or descending, the opposite occurs.  

In the second month, if Jupiter is exalted, direct, and favorable, the embryo is well-placed, and the mother is cheerful and healthy. If in its own terms, she devotes herself to religion, prayer, and divine inspirations. Most prophets and philosophers were conceived under Jupiter’s great beneficence, with angels conversing with them.  

If Jupiter is in Saturn’s terms, the child is contemplative, knowing untaught things, displaying signs and miracles. If in Mars’ terms (unafflicted), these miracles manifest through strength and dominance. If in Venus’ terms, the child invites people to faith, offering good counsel. If in Mercury’s terms, it is argumentative, recognizing truth but facing rejection.  

If Jupiter is debilitated, the child is dull-witted, learning only by rote, like a beast—knowing only eating and sleeping, yet managing its life well.  

In the third month, if Mars is exalted and direct, the child is bold and skilled. If in its own terms, it is combative, boasting of strength. If in Saturn’s terms, it is patient but cunning and vengeful. If in Jupiter’s terms, it acts justly. If in Venus’ terms, it is lustful and jealous. If in Mercury’s terms, it is clever but spiteful.  

If Mars is afflicted, the child is cowardly and base.  

In the fourth month, if the Sun is exalted and unafflicted, the child is regal, ambitious, fit for leadership. If in Saturn’s terms, it is lofty-minded. If in Jupiter’s terms, it reaches greatness—a king or prophet. If in Mars’ terms, it is a formidable warrior. If in Venus’ terms, it is refined but indulgent.  

In the fifth month, if Venus is exalted, the child is beautiful and pleasure-seeking. If in its own terms, it is fair, curly-haired, and sweet-tempered. If in Saturn’s terms, it is wheat-complexioned, loyal, and patient.  

In the sixth month, if Mercury (Jupiter’s younger brother) is exalted, the child is intelligent and eloquent. If in Saturn’s terms, it is deep-thinking but taciturn. If in Jupiter’s terms, it is devout. If in Mars’ terms, it is argumentative. If in Venus’ terms, it is poetic.  

In the seventh month, if the Moon is unafflicted, the child is well-mannered. If in Mercury’s terms, it is charming but fickle. If in Saturn’s terms, it is steadfast.  

If governance returns to Saturn in the eighth month and it is afflicted, the child may die at birth. If it passes to Jupiter in the ninth month, the child is born fortunate.  

Had the human form been completed in one day, it would not remain in the womb for two; if in one month, not two. In sum, the child’s fate is determined at conception, as the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: *"The fortunate is he who is fortunate in his mother’s womb, and the wretched is he who is wretched in his mother’s womb."*  

Yet, the birth ascendant (*ṭāliʿ al-mawlūd*) is also a fundamental principle, called the *canon* (*qānūn*). Since determining the conception ascendant is difficult, this canon is used for judgment.  

This suffices—and God knows best.
