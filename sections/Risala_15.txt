### Summary of the Second Epistle on Natural Philosophy Concerning the Heavens and the Cosmos  
*In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful*  

The purpose of this epistle is to expound upon universal and simple bodies. Know that the cosmos consists of the Earth, along with its animals, minerals, plants, wind, water, fire, the celestial spheres, and the stars—as well as absolute corporeality and the souls that sustain all these entities. These are divided into two realms: the first is called the *"Supernal World"* (*‘ālam ‘ulwī*), also referred to as the *"World of Order and Harmony"* (*‘ālam al-nizām*) or the *"World of Divine Sovereignty"* (*‘ālam al-malakūt*). The second is that which lies beneath the sphere of the Moon, or the Earth, termed the *"Sublunary World"* (*‘ālam al-suflā*).  

The sages who comprehend both these realms call them *"The Macrocosm"* (*al-‘ālam al-insān al-kabīr*). When observing the human individual, they note that he is composed of elements from both worlds and thus call him *"The Microcosm"* (*al-insān al-ṣaghīr*). For just as the heavens and the Earth and all within them consist of body and soul, so too does man consist of body and soul.  

Know that there is a single universal soul that sustains the entire cosmos in a state of life, though each entity possesses its own distinct form. The soul that governs one form may appear to govern another as well. Just as physicians describe the anatomy of the human body, the philosophers describe the anatomy of this *"Macrocosm."* They assert that all corporeal entities in the cosmos constitute a single body, despite their varied forms.  

Corporeal forms are of two kinds: the *"substantial form"* (*ṣūra muqawwima*), which comprises length, width, and depth—common to all bodies—and the *"complementary form"* (*ṣūra mutammima*), which includes circularity, triangularity, squareness, pentagonal and hexagonal shapes, and the like. Among the complementary forms is motion in six directions, as well as rotational motion in particular.  

Among the attributes of the complementary form is light, which is of two types: essential (such as the light of the Sun) and accidental (such as the light of the Moon, which is reflected). Other attributes include color and transparency. Thus, we affirm that the cosmos is spherical, and the most perfect shape is the sphere, which is the origin of all forms. All celestial bodies are spherical, as evidenced by the Moon. The light of all stars is intrinsic, except that of the Moon. All celestial bodies are transparent, except the Earth and the Moon, as we shall later explain.  

The heavens are called *"samāwāt"* because, wherever a person stands on Earth—whether in the east, west, north, or south—the dome of the sky appears above their head, while their feet point toward the Earth's center. Hence, they are called *"samāwāt."* They are also called *"aflāk"* (spheres) because they are perfectly circular.  

There are nine celestial spheres: the closest to us is the sphere of the Moon; above it lies the sphere of Mercury; then Venus, the Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn—which our Prophet (peace be upon him) mentioned, and which God, the Exalted, references in the Quran: *"By the sky and the night comer—and what can make you know what the night comer is? It is the piercing star."* (Q 86:1-3). Scholars agree that the *"piercing star"* is Saturn, whose light penetrates the seven heavens and reaches the icy moisture in our eyes, just as the reflection of an object appears in water. The eighth sphere is that of the fixed stars, and the ninth is the *"Greatest Sphere"* (*falak al-a‘ẓam*), which encompasses all others.  

Know that each lower sphere serves as the "earth" for the one above it, just as the sphere of Mercury is the "heaven" for the Moon, while Mercury's own "earth" is the sphere of Venus. What we call the "Earth" might more aptly be termed a "carpet." Each higher sphere is the "heaven" of the one beneath it, as we have explained in the introduction to astronomy.  

These spheres are layered like onion skins, each resting upon another without gaps. One must not entertain the notion that there is void (*khalā’*) in the cosmos or that anything beyond what we observe exists—whether bodies or spirits. The proof against the existence of void is that it cannot logically exist in two ways: either as substance (*jawhar*) or accident (*‘araḍ*). If it were substance, it would not be void; if accident, it would require a substance to inhere in, for accidents cannot subsist independently. An accident is a state within a substance—like whiteness in a garment, blackness in tar, or whiteness in snow and camphor—not an entity separate from substance.  

Some might argue that there is a space that remains unfilled, but this would render void impossible. Others say void must either be a place or not a place. If it were a place, it would be a place for bodies, for no space exists without an occupant. If it were not a place, the term itself would be meaningless. Should someone claim that void lies beyond the cosmos—meaning beyond the spheres—this proposition entails even greater absurdity than the previous ones, and the same refutation applies.  

Thus, we affirm that each celestial body is the sovereign of its sphere, and that sphere is filled with angels of the same nature as that celestial body. Each sphere is, in itself, a world. The Sun is the sovereign of all celestial bodies, though scholars dispute whether the Sun's sphere is nobler than those of the superior planets or the fixed stars. However, there is no doubt that the Sun reigns over all celestial bodies and spheres.  

One might ask why the Sun does not reside in the outermost sphere, where its station would be nobler. The reason is that if the Sun were in the fifth sphere, the existence of the cosmos, humanity, animals, plants, and minerals would be impossible. If it were in the third sphere, corruption would arise due to excessive cold or heat. The Greek astronomers allegorically state that Jupiter interceded with the Sun, saying: *"Do not descend further, lest you scorch creation with your light, nor ascend higher, lest our existence perish."*  

We know through proof that when the Sun is at its perigee, it scorches plants and animals, while at its apogee, it brings moderation. If its perigee were in Gemini rather than Sagittarius, habitation in the northern regions would be difficult. Evidence for this lies in the southern regions, where when the Sun is at its perigee and Capricorn culminates, there is no habitation—only barren land. Similarly, certain equatorial zones are uninhabited because the Sun's proximity there is greater than in the north.  

Thus, it is established that the existence of the sublunary world depends on the Sun, while the celestial spheres are like its climes, the zodiac signs its cities, and the stars their governors.  

Know that the Earth's diameter is 2,164 *farsakhs* (parasangs), and the greatest circle upon its surface—the equator—measures 6,800 *farsakhs*. The diameter of the sphere of air is 53,832 *farsakhs*. The thickness of the Moon’s sphere is a fraction of the air’s sphere, while Mercury’s is 500 times the Earth’s diameter, Venus’s 915 times, the Sun’s 100 times, and the Sun’s mass 166 and a quarter times that of Earth. Mars’s sphere is 7,656 times the Earth’s diameter, Jupiter’s 5,527 times, Saturn’s 7,605 times, and the fixed stars’ approximately 12,000 times. These measurements are recorded in astronomical texts.  

Our purpose in mentioning this is to establish a single truth—one that any rational person must acknowledge: it has been proven that every substance connected to the spheres, stars, Earth, and their contents is also linked beyond the outermost sphere, permeating and transcending it. This is the first premise.  

Second, we assert that the heavens, the Earth, and all that is more refined are better suited to receive this power. An example is sunlight shining uniformly upon water, glass, mirrors, and rusted iron—yet its effects differ. The iron absorbs fire, while crystal absorbs light, and so on.  

A medical analogy: if two men, one healthy (Zayd) and one ill (‘Amr), consume the same food, it increases the former’s health but exacerbates the latter’s sickness—yet the cause is the same food. This is another premise.  

Thus, we say that the reason man is receptive to the rational soul (*nafs nāṭiqa*) is the equilibrium of his nature and his celestial ascendant (*ṭāli‘ falakī*), along with the power emanating from the spheres that connects him to this balanced state. Through this receptivity, man attains countless virtues and distinctions.  

Among all bodies, those farthest from the simple substance (*jawhar basīṭ*) and the outermost sphere—such as the Earth and its inhabitants—are subject to decay because nature has access to them. How, then, can the spheres—closer to the simple substances and untouched by material corruption—be inanimate, soulless, and irrational?  

He who denies this is like a boastful man whom a king enriches for a service, only for him to claim before his kin that he possesses more wealth than the king himself. Such examples abound.  

Know that the celestial sphere is called the *"Fifth Nature"* (*al-ṭabī‘a al-khāmisa*), and corruption cannot reach it. Human nature’s equilibrium, compared to this *"Fifth Nature,"* is as insignificant as a speck of Earth beside the outermost sphere. The powers of prophets, messengers, and philosophers all derive from it. Whoever’s natal celestial configuration is more harmonious possesses a subtler nature and greater miraculous ability.  

All earthly beings—animals and plants—live through animal and vegetative life, but the spheres themselves are alive. The nobility of the stars surpasses that of the spheres, just as the spheres surpass the Earth. Thus, it is evident that nothing in existence is nobler than the spheres and stars—save God, the Necessary Being. After Him, the Intellect, the Soul, and the stars are alive, rational, and act by will—though all are ultimately governed.  

The spheres and their ranks are the exalted abode of prophets and sages. They are the *"Cherubic Angels"* (*al-malā’ika al-karūbiyyūn*) of scripture and the *"Bearers of the Throne"* (*ḥamalat al-‘arsh*).  

The purpose of this discourse is to demonstrate that if humans—who consume earthly plants, originate from dust, and are tainted by lust, anger, greed, arrogance, hypocrisy, malice, obstinacy, and disbelief—are alive, rational, and act by will despite being subject to nature’s tyranny, then how much more deserving are the spheres and stars—free from such impurities—of these attributes? This suffices as a reminder.  

*And God knows best.*
