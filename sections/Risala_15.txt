### | خلاصۀ رسالۀ اول از طبيعيّات و آن رسالۀ هيولى و صورت است و آن را «سمع كيان» خوانند 
بسم اللّه الرحمن الرحيم
بداند كه بزرگان حكما لفظها نهاده‌اند كه اگر ديگران خواهند كه لفظى ديگر به جاى آن بنهند يا عبارتى كه نه در خورد آن باشد به تفسير آن بياورند هيچ مژه ندارد، چنانكه لفظ‍‌ «جوهر» و «عرض» و «هيولى» و «اسطقس» و «امهات» و «اركان» و «عناصر» و «آخشيج» كه به‌جاى، اين لفظها اگر چيزى ديگر بنهند ناخوش آيد يا خود كژ آيد. و معنى قول بزرگان كه گويند «هيولى»، بدان آن خواهند كه هيولى جوهرى باشد قابل صورت، و شكلى كه در جوهر پديد آيد آن شكل را «صورت» خوانند، و اختلاف موجودات بيشتر از صورت است.چنانكه ما بيشتر چيزها مى‌بينيم كه هيولى ايشان يكى است اما صورت ايشان مختلف است. چون آهن كه از آن تيغ و كارد و اره و تبر و تيشه آيد. و هيولى يكى است ولكن صورت مختلف است، و همچنين چون چوب كه هيولى درو سرير و تخت و غير آن است.

 و هيولى از چهارگونه بود:- اول را «هيولى صناعى» گويند،- دوم را «هيولى طبيعى» گويند،- سيوم را «هيولى كلى» خوانند،- چهارم را «هيولى اولى». اما «هيولى صناعى» جسمهايى بود كه صناعان از آن صورتها كنند چون چوب درودگر را، و آهن آهنگر را، و خاك و آب بناگر را، و ريسمان جولاه را، و برين مثال مى‌داند. و «هيولى طبيعى» چون آتش و هوا و آب و زمين و هرچه از زير فلك قمر تا مركز زمين پديدار آيد ازين هيولى پديد مى‌آيد. چون معادن و نبات و حيوان و چون كون اين جمله كه ياد كرديم ازوست چون به فساد شوند و اركان و اخلاط‍‌ مستحيل شود قوّتها هم بدو باز شود: آبى به آبى و هوائى به هوائى، و آتشى به آتشى، و زمينى به زمينى. و آنچه اين فعل مى‌كند طبيعت است و آن قوّت جوهرى بسيط‍‌ است كه از حركت اول است چنانكه ياد كرديم. اما هيولى سيوم كه «هيولى كل» خوانند آن جسم مطلق است كه جملۀ عالم را نسق و نظام از آن آمد چون افلاك و كواكب، و آنچه دريشان است. اما هيولى چهارم كه «هيولى اولى» خوانند آن جوهرى بسيط‍‌ است و معقول است و حسّ‌ آن را درنتواند يافتن و آن صورت است وجود را، و آن هستى مطلق است كه كميت و كيفيت بدو راه نيابد. نخست ابداعى است كه آفريننده كرده است و آن مبدأ اول است و ابتدا [ى] همه چيزها ازوست. و بداند كه هستى و كميت و كيفيت صورتهائى باشند بسيط‍‌ كه الا عقل آن را در نتواند يافت. پس با يكديگر تركيب كنند و وجود دوم پديد آيد. بعضى از آن هيولى شود و بعضى صورت. «كيفيت» همچون صورت و «كميت» هيولى او باشد.
-148-
 و «كميت» صورتى باشد در هيولى كه جسم اول است و جسم اول هيولى او باشد و جوهر بسيط‍‌ صورت همه باشد. مثال اين از عالم سفلى و حسى:پيراهن صورتى است در جامه و جامه هيولى است پيراهن را،و جامه صورتى است در ريسمان، و ريسمان هيولى است صورت او را،و ريسمان صورتى است در پنبه ، و پنبه هيولى است او را،و پنبه صورتى است در نبات، و نبات هيولى است او را،و نبات صورتى است در اركان، و اركان هيولى است او را،و اركان صورتى است در جسم، و جسم هيولى است او را،و جسم صورتى است در جوهر، و جوهر هيولى باشد او را،و جوهر بسيط‍‌ صورت باشد همه را و شريفتر از همه.

بداند كه مكان نزديك جمله حكما هستى باشد كه هستى درو متمكن باشد، و به‌وجهى ديگر گويند مكان سطحى باشد گرد چيزى دون او درآمده. و نزديك محققان چنان است كه مكان مكيال جسم است، و قولهاى مختلف گفتند در مكان. و قومى او را «جسم» گفتند و برهان بر آن است كه مكان جسم جسم است و «عرض» نتواند بودن و اين در محسوسات باشد، و مكان جوهر بسيط‍‌ [در] «جوهر بسيط‍‌» باشد چنانكه نفس افلاطون در جاى معتاد. و بيشتر مختصران منكرند نفس جزوى را و ندانند كه اگر نفس به معنى، نه به صورت جدا نبودى خلق در دانش و افعال متساوى بودندى. بلى مبتدى چنان پندارد كه جزو نفس چنان بود كه مثلا پاره‌اى از جامه يا بعضى از جسمى. ندانند كه تجزّى جسم انفصال باشد و از يكديگر جدا شوند

چنانكه هريكى مكانى جداگانه باشد كه جسم ديگر او را بنساود. چون آب در كوزه و باد در انبان، كه كوزه آب را و انبان باد را مى‌بساود. بلى تجزّى نفس معقول باشد و از آن است كه حكما شرح جزو لا يتجزى كنند چنانكه بعد ازين در رسايل بازگوئيم و ما مثالى بياوريم اگرچه درين رساله نيست. و هركه درين كتاب نظر كند و مجمل الحكمه را بيند داند كه آنچه ما آورديم بيشتر آن است كه در آن كتاب نيست، و اگر هست مشروح نيست. گوئيم در مواضع ديگر به برهان معلوم است كه «نفس ناطقه» نه جسم است و نه قوّتى در جسم. بلى صورتى است جسم را و جسم آلت اوست. و جمله دانسته‌ها يا «محسوس» بود يا «معقول». اما «محسوس» چون بيشتر از علم و صناعتها كه هرچه به جسم متعلق است خانۀ وى خيال و حفظ‍‌ است. باز آنچه «معقول» است در خيال نيايد، بلكه در نفس عاقل بود و شك نباشد كه معقول و عاقل هر دو يكى‌اند. پس اين معقول او را نبود و عاقل آن را به استنباط‍‌ يا به تعليم به‌دست آورد و با نفس خويشتن يكى كرد. و در اول معقول نبود در نفس نبود و اكنون هست و در نفس است. پس علم نفس و جزو لا يتجزى هر دو هستند ولكن در عقل نه در حسّ‌. پس صورت نفس افلاطون در نفس كلى همچون صورت علم افلاطون بود در نفس افلاطون. پس اين تفصيل و تجزّى عقلى بود نه حسّ‌، و اين برهان است.

### Summary of the First Epistle on Natural Philosophy, Concerning Matter and Form, Known as "Samʿ Kayān"  
In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful  

Know that the great sages have established certain terms such that if others attempt to replace them with different words or employ inadequate expressions to explain them, it would be improper. Examples include the terms *jawhar* (substance), *ʿaraḍ* (accident), *hayūlā* (matter), *usṭuqus* (element), *ummahāt* (primary principles), *arkān* (pillars), *ʿanāṣir* (elements), and *ākhshīj* (constituents). If one substitutes these terms with others, it would be displeasing or incorrect.  

The meaning of the statement by the great sages when they say *hayūlā* (matter) is that matter is a substance receptive to form, and the shape that emerges in the substance is called *ṣūra* (form). The diversity of existents arises primarily from form. For instance, we observe many things whose matter is one, yet their forms differ—such as iron, from which a sword, a knife, a saw, an axe, and a chisel are made. The matter is one, but the forms vary. Similarly, wood serves as the matter for a bed, a throne, and other objects.  

-147-  
Matter is of four kinds:  
1. *Artificial Matter* (*hayūlā ṣināʿī*),  
2. *Natural Matter* (*hayūlā ṭabīʿī*),  
3. *Universal Matter* (*hayūlā kullī*),  
4. *Prime Matter* (*hayūlā ūlā*).  

*Artificial Matter* consists of bodies from which artisans derive forms, such as wood for carpenters, iron for blacksmiths, earth and water for builders, and thread for weavers—and so on.  

*Natural Matter* includes fire, air, water, and earth, as well as all that appears beneath the lunar sphere down to the center of the earth—minerals, plants, animals, and the like. When these things perish and their elements and humors dissolve, their forces return to their origins: watery to water, airy to air, fiery to fire, and earthy to earth. That which performs this action is *nature*—an essential, simple force originating from the First Motion, as previously mentioned.  

The third kind, *Universal Matter*, is absolute body, from which the order and arrangement of the entire cosmos arises—the celestial spheres, stars, and all they contain.  

The fourth kind, *Prime Matter*, is a simple, intelligible substance imperceptible to the senses. It is the form of existence, absolute being, unquantifiable and unqualifiable. It is the first creation of the Maker, the First Principle from which all things originate.  

Know that existence, quantity, and quality are simple forms that only the intellect can grasp. When they combine, a secondary existence arises—some becoming matter, others form. *Quality* is like form, while *quantity* is its matter.  

-148-  
*Quantity* is a form in matter, which is the first body, and the first body is matter for it, while simple substance is the form of all.  

An example from the lower, sensible world:  
- A shirt is a form in a garment, and the garment is its matter.  
- The garment is a form in thread, and the thread is its matter.  
- The thread is a form in cotton, and the cotton is its matter.  
- Cotton is a form in the plant, and the plant is its matter.  
- The plant is a form in the elements, and the elements are its matter.  
- The elements are a form in body, and body is their matter.  
- Body is a form in substance, and substance is its matter.  
- Simple substance is the form of all and the noblest among them.  

### || Chapter: On Place  
Know that, according to most sages, place is the existence in which existence resides. Alternatively, it is said that place is a surface encompassing a thing from below. According to the meticulous scholars, place is the measure of a body, and various opinions have been expressed about it. Some have called it a *body*, arguing that the place of a body is a body and cannot be an accident—this pertains to sensible things.  

Place in *simple substance* is like Plato’s soul in its habitual abode. Most summarizers deny partial souls, unaware that if the soul were not distinct in meaning (though not in form), all creation would be equal in knowledge and action. Beginners may imagine the partial soul as a piece of a garment or part of a body, not realizing that the division of body entails separation, where each occupies a distinct place unoccupied by another—like water in a jug or air in a bladder, where the jug contains the water and the bladder contains the air.  

-149-  
The division of the soul, however, is intelligible, which is why the sages elaborate on the indivisible part—as we shall revisit in later epistles. Here, we offer an analogy, though it is not part of this epistle. Whoever examines this book and *Mujmal al-Ḥikma* will see that much of what we present is absent there or, if present, is not detailed.  

We affirm elsewhere with proof that the *rational soul* is neither a body nor a faculty in a body. Rather, it is the form of the body, and the body is its instrument. All that is known is either *sensible* or *intelligible*. The *sensible* pertains mostly to knowledge and crafts related to the body, housed in imagination and memory. The *intelligible*, however, does not enter the imagination but resides in the rational soul. There is no doubt that the intelligible and the intellect are one. Thus, this intelligible was not originally in the soul but was acquired through deduction or instruction and united with it. Initially, it was not present in the soul, but now it is.  

Thus, the soul’s knowledge and the indivisible part both exist—but in the intellect, not the senses. Plato’s soul in the universal soul is like Plato’s knowledge in Plato’s soul. This distinction and division are intellectual, not sensory—and this is proof.  

### || Chapter on Motion  
We say motion is of six kinds:  
1. Generation and corruption,  
2. Increase and decrease,  
3. Alteration and displacement.  

*Generation* is of two kinds: from non-existence to existence, or from potentiality to actuality. *Corruption* is the opposite, as when a date palm arises from a date seed and perishes upon burning. The higher part of this tree and its body derive from water and earth. Water nourished the earth, extracting from it a paste like the date seed, which nature fashioned into a tree. Thus, the tree is from water and earth, with the form of nature within it. When the earth returns to earth, the water to water, and the form of nature to its element, this is corruption.  

That which is created by the Necessary Existence—such as the soul, intellect, and celestial spheres—never perishes, for their forms are not transient like those in the earthly realm. The form of nature in the tree is not firmly fixed; over time, form cannot remain separate from matter. Rather, it is unstable, like moisture in flesh, where the fiery element or the power of air clings to it, seeking to draw this subtle essence to its center.  

-150-  
We offer an example: the forms of all earthly things separate from their matter, whereas celestial forms do not separate from theirs. The forms in the earthly realm—attached to animals, plants, and minerals—perish with the destruction of their matter. But the forms of the higher world, like Plato’s knowledge in Plato’s soul, are united with their matter as a simple substance, imperishable—as established in books, the soul is eternal.  

We have a clear example in the lower world: gold and lead ore, or vitriol and ruby. The moisture and fluidity of gold in its earthly particles (mercury) are so firmly fixed that fire cannot separate them, nor can earth or the passage of time destroy them. Lead, however, is not so; fire annihilates it in an hour, returning its liquidity and fluidity to their center, while its mercurial parts return to earth as *mardāsang* (litharge). The same applies to ruby and vitriol.  

Thus, if it is permissible for a substance in the lower world to resist the separation of form from matter over vast ages, it is even more fitting for that which is beyond the elements of generation and corruption. An example is the burning of trees, plants, animals, and minerals—no fire burns fire itself. If fire ceases, it has returned to its center. This is a sensory example.  

*Increase* is the growth of a body due to the addition of another, like water swelling in a jug near fire, or air accumulating in a bellows, where continuous motion expands it until it bursts or snaps—a clear example without fallacy.  

*Decrease* is the reduction of a thing as its parts diminish—examples abound.  

*Alteration* is the change of an attribute in a subject, such as color, taste, smell, or other qualities.  

*Displacement* refers to forced motion, whereas rotational motion is distinct.  

-151-  
Motion manifests in many ways. Sometimes the interval between motion and rest is apparent, sometimes hidden—as discussed in a treatise on music, where a swiftness exists such that the *senses* can distinguish its motion from rest, though often it is perceived as a single, unbroken sound. Likewise, in things where the *intellect* can discern what the senses cannot.  

In some natural bodies, motion arises from themselves—like the motion of fire and the life of animals, which cease with rest, as known in medicine. Such motion in animals and fire stems from a force the soul has placed in the body. Beyond this, the body’s shape and other motions—whether forced or circular—require an external mover. These motions have six directions: above, below, right, left, front, and back.  

### || Chapter on Time  
Time is the state between past and future, measured by the celestial revolution. Though many define time, all return to this, or it is invalid. This applies everywhere on earth without doubt, for no place on earth lies beyond the celestial cycle.  

Know that time belongs to the earth, not the heavens. Though the earth’s shadow reaches Mercury’s sphere, once it passes, no darkness remains in the celestial rotation. Where there is no darkness, there is no time. Thus, time belongs to the earth.
