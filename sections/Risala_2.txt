### **Summary of the Second Epistle on Geometry**  
**In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful**  

We say that geometry is of two kinds: **intellectual** and **sensory**.  

As for the **sensory**, it is the knowledge of magnitudes and what pertains to them—geometric shapes—which may be perceived either by the sense of sight or touch. Magnitudes are of three types: **line**, **surface**, and **body**.  

As for the **intellectual**, it is the knowledge of the dimensions of these magnitudes and what pertains to them, conceived in the soul through thought, in contrast to sensory perception in apprehension and comprehension. Dimensions are also of three types: **length**, **width**, and **depth**, which are attributes of these sensory magnitudes. Contemplation of abstract dimensions is only possible through the power of thought and the discipline of the soul.  

Sensory geometry is more accessible to the learner, and whatever is grasped prior to practice is a kind of geometry. An example of sensory geometry is length, width, and depth—such as a **cube**, which has six equal sides, perceivable by the senses, with directions: right and left, front and back, above and below. This is purely sensory.  

We say that the **origin of a line is a point**, just as we stated in the first epistle that **one is the origin of number**. Here, we mean a **sensory point**, not an intellectual one.  

When sensory points are arranged in this manner, a **line** is formed, and the line is the origin of a **surface**, just as the point is the origin of the line. Likewise, the surface is the origin of a **body**.  

A line has **length** and nothing more. A surface has **length and width** but no **depth**. When a surface is doubled, the first body is formed.  

### **Section on Types of Lines**  
We say that lines are of three kinds:  
1. **Straight**, like this (—).  
2. **Curved**, like this (⌒).  
3. **Both straight and curved**, like this (⊓).  

When straight lines meet, they may be:  
- **Equal** (≡),  
- **Parallel** (‖),  
- **Converging** (> <),  
- **Tangent** (⊤),  
- **Intersecting** (×).  

Each of these gives rise to a class of sensory geometric shapes.  

When a straight line stands upon another straight line without inclining to either side, it is called a **perpendicular (عمود)**. The line upon which it falls is called the **base (قاعده)**, forming a **right angle (زاوية قائمة)**. If the angle is **less than a right angle**, it is called **acute (حادة)**; if **greater**, it is called **obtuse (منفرجة)**.  

When obtuse and acute angles lie on the same straight line, together they equal two right angles. If multiple angles lie on a straight line, all are acute, and together they equal two right angles.  

Just as arithmetic is infinite, so too are geometric shapes—such as the triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon, and so on. If someone claims a shape with a thousand sides or a body with a thousand surfaces and a thousand bases, there is no objection, for such is the nature of shapes.  

### **Section on Types of Angles**  
Angles are of two kinds:  
1. **Planar**—formed by two lines not in direct alignment.  
2. **Solid**—formed by three lines perpendicular to one another.  

Planar angles are of three types:  
- Between two straight lines,  
- Between two curved lines,  
- Between a straight and a curved line.  

Angles formed by straight lines are further classified by quality:  
- **Right**,  
- **Obtuse**,  
- **Acute**, as previously mentioned.  

### **Section on Types of Curved Lines**  
Curved lines are of four kinds:  
1. **Complete circle** (○),  
2. **Semicircle** (◡),  
3. **Greater than a semicircle** (◠),  
4. **Less than a semicircle** (◝).  

The **center (مركز)** is the midpoint of the circle. The **diameter (قطر)** is the line that divides the circle into two equal halves, passing through the center. The **chord (وتر)** is the straight line connecting the ends of an arc. The **sagitta (سهم)** is the perpendicular bisector of the chord.  

### **On Geometric Progression**  
The smallest shape is the **triangle**, composed of three parts. Then follows the **hexagon** (six parts), the **decagon** (ten parts), and so on infinitely.  

The arrangement of triangles holds profound lessons for the wise in recognizing the Necessary Existence (واجب الوجود), leading to contentment without need for anything else.  

### **On the Nature of Shapes**  
The **point** is the origin of the **line**, the line of the **surface**, and the surface of the **body**. Thus, nothing precedes the point, and nothing surpasses the body.  

### **Conclusion**  
The purpose of this epistle is to demonstrate that the foundation of all sciences—especially those pertaining to wisdom—is **arithmetic and geometry**. It is forbidden to seek knowledge, wisdom, or the recognition of God Almighty without mastery of these two disciplines.  

Whoever does not know these sciences perfectly cannot truly know God—whatever they say is error, for divine knowledge follows natural and spiritual knowledge.  

Geometry has two aspects: one in the **sensory world**, the other in the **intellectual world**. One who does not first grasp the sensory aspect can never attain the realm of spirits and intellect.  

**Arithmetic and geometry are the ladder by which one ascends to divine knowledge and abstract intelligibles.** Through disciplined study, one may abstract numbers and shapes from matter, realizing that all sensory forms can be separated from materiality—just as the essence of a cube exists beyond its sensory appearance.  

This epistle does not exhaust the sciences of arithmetic and geometry, for its purpose is to **awaken the intellect**, **stimulate inquiry**, and **guide the seeker**—not to teach geometry in full.  

The wise, upon reflection, will recognize that what is near in sensation can be abstracted, leading to the realization that the abstracting faculty—the **soul**—must itself be immaterial. Thus, they will yearn for their return to the spiritual realm, seeking deliverance from the material world—the realm of generation and corruption, the great hell—and strive for the eternal paradise of the world of souls.  

**And with God is protection and success.**
