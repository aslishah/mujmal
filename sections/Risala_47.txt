### Chapter Seven: On Governance  

Know that it is impossible for creation to attain the ultimate felicity of which we have spoken except through virtuous action, sound knowledge, a heart at ease, and the means of worldly subsistence being secured. The ease of the heart can only be achieved through the governance of the body, and unless the heart is at ease, the rational soul will not engage in knowledge and action. Therefore, the first step must be the discipline of the body so that the heart may be at ease and the soul may be engaged in its work.  

Know that the people of the world are divided into two categories:  
1. **The first group** are those whose nature does not accept [virtue] in any way, and this is due to two reasons:  
   - One is the denial of the Hereafter, and there is no remedy for this except through the grace of God Almighty.  
   - The other is [domination by] lust and anger, and this can be overcome through discipline—if the individual is receptive.  
2. **The second group** are those who do not restrain themselves from lust and anger, yet do not leave the rational soul idle.  

For the one in whom lust and anger prevail to such an extent that they prevent him from knowledge and action, it is necessary that he engage in bodily discipline and governance—otherwise, he will surely be among the ruined.  

If one wishes to undertake discipline, there are two paths before him:  
- The first is what we mentioned in the first chapter—gradually reducing food intake.  
- The second is seclusion, for these two disciplines are essential for attaining both knowledge and action.  

As for the seeker of knowledge, we have already discussed in the third chapter what must be done. Once knowledge is attained, action becomes easier. This is evidenced by the saying of our Prophet (peace be upon him): *"An hour of reflection is better than a year of worship."* There are many verses and traditions on this matter, but we seek the meaning, not the form. That meaning is that when the sage disciplines himself, casts away lust and anger, makes the mathematical sciences a ladder, and ascends to the divine, his worship becomes contemplation of the majesty and grandeur of the Necessary Being.  

When the sage is in this state, he is closer to love than to formal worship. In this state, if he falls short in performing the prescribed acts of obedience or worship as ordained by religious law, the Necessary Being will pardon him, for this is not true disobedience. Rather, if he is occupied with knowledge—and knowledge is above worship—especially if that knowledge is of divine love, then it is even more exalted.  

To impose acts of obedience and worship upon such a person would be like a beloved—who is in no need of his lovers’ services—commanding a perfect lover (one who has no beloved but Him and is with Him night and day, outwardly and inwardly) to labor in his service, saying: *"Despite my utter independence, build a palace for me and quarry all its stones with your bare nails from the mountain."* Even if the lover submits, this demand from the beloved would seem excessively harsh.  

If such an analogy is inappropriate when applied to a physical creation, how much more so when applied to the Necessary Being? The sage accepts this truth only when he is day and night in the presence of the Necessary Being, unattached to any worldly ties or pleasures, withdraws from the world into solitude, and sustains himself on knowledge and the contemplation of intelligible realities—not drowned in the world, lust, and anger like a stone in water.  

But if someone abandons worship or neglects the obligations of religious law while being immersed in the world, lust, and anger, then he is a pure disbeliever, with no share in either world. Every hair on his body would be a thousand devils.  

The proof of this is that we observe the sent prophets—their knowledge was celestial and divinely bestowed, not acquired or analogical like that of the sages. They showed no inclination toward the world and never ceased worshiping the Necessary Being for even a moment—as with Moses, Jesus, John, and the other prophets.  

If someone claims that the prophets merely performed such acts as a display for the people’s sake, let him—unless he is blind and wretched—take a lesson from the great sages. Despite their immense wisdom, they never rested from the service and worship of the Necessary Being—such as Balīnās, Luqmān, Solomon, and their like. Among the true sages, Balīnās, Luqmān, and Plato turned away from the world and devoted themselves to obedience to the Necessary Being.  

In absolute terms, whoever is preoccupied with the world, amassing wealth, lust, and anger, and neither obeys nor worships the Necessary Being, is not a sage—he is a disbeliever. He will have no share in the eternal Paradise of the heavens, will never reach the Necessary Being, and will remain forever in the everlasting Hell, never escaping the state of dogs, wolves, and serpents.  

### On Repentance  

Know that in religious law, there is repentance for every sin—be it fornication, lying, theft, or the like. But in philosophy, there is no repentance for sin. For sin is an evil act, and once an evil act takes form in the rational soul, that form can never be separated from it. Any soul that bears the imprint of an evil form will never attain the heavens.
