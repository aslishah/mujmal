### **Summary of the Ninth Epistle on the Differences in Morals**  
*In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.*  

Know that when God—Exalted and Glorified—willed to bring forth the children of Adam upon this earth and distinguish them through His wisdom and perfect power, He first created a being from clay, as the Qur’an attests. In ancient times, this being was called *Admanus*, as Idris (upon him be peace) mentioned in his book. From his essence, He created a female, so that through them, procreation arose, and the world became populated by them. He entrusted their governance, the formation of their shapes and appearances, the differences in their temperaments, languages, and colors—all of this—to the celestial sphere.  

From among these creatures, He sent prophets and raised up sages and philosophers to reveal divine law (*shari‘a*) and wisdom (*hikma*), binding divine law to revelation and wisdom to rational proof. He sent scriptures to the prophets and established arguments, declaring: *Whoever obeys Us, We shall grant eternal paradise—which is the heavens, the soul, and the intellect—and whoever disobeys Us, We shall destroy and cast into hellfire, which is the center of the earth.* As mentioned in the Book of the Israelites: *O son of Adam, We created you by Our power for eternal life, and I am the Living who never dies. Obey Me in what I have commanded you,*  

-115-  

*and beware of what I have forbidden, so that I may make you such that you never die. O son of Adam, I have the power that when I say to a thing, "Be," it is—without delay. Obey Me in what I have commanded, and turn away from what I have forbidden, so that I may empower you to say to a thing, "Be not," and it is not—without delay.*  

This statement is profoundly clear, and it is evident that the path of divine law is the best of all paths. The prophets and messengers did not command us what would lead to our ruin—rather, they sought only our guidance.  

The purpose of this epistle is to explain human character and disposition, and how, if one’s character is such that the world of spirits does not perceive it as blameworthy or discordant, they will accept it. But if it is tainted and repugnant, they will reject it. Do you not see how polished steel reflects the light of the sun, and from its reflection emerges an essence like fire? But if it is rusted, it gains nothing from the sun but a dim glow—what then of a rusted mirror or a mere stone?  

Similarly, if a king or ruler has children, upon his death, the one most adorned with virtues will inherit his father’s place—not the idle, ignorant, or unproductive. Thus, we affirm with certainty that the power of the human soul is greater than this, for the body is subject to the soul’s command, not the soul to the body’s.  

When a person wishes to adopt a habit, if they persist in it, their nature will accept it, and it will never leave them as long as they live—whether it be eating little or much, good or bad temperament, indulging or restraining desire, speaking much or little, remaining silent, and the like. Whatever habit a person cultivates, they can maintain. Thus, it is clear that morals are acquired, and human nature is like wax. If a person is learned, their nature is like warm wax, molded according to their knowledge. But if they are ignorant, their nature is like cold wax, unyielding to any command.  

-116-  

Therefore, let knowledge govern nature, and let one make oneself the sovereign of one’s nature through knowledge—moderating food, clothing, desire, and anger to the extent necessary for bodily harmony. For if one indulges every whim of nature, desire and anger will overpower them, and they will lose control.  

That which exists in the intellect as potential cannot attain actuality without felicity. The intellect is like a servant to desire, constantly scheming how to satisfy a craving or act upon anger. Thus, when it seeks the hereafter, it is hindered by these attachments. This state is like fire, oil, and a wick. The wise know that fire is the most refined of the four elements, and each element is most suited to its own center. The sole purpose and nature of earth is to incline toward its center, and its pain is when wind, water, or fire tears it from its form.  

None may claim that there exists a being without pain or comfort. The pain of earth is ruin and destruction—for as long as a tree grows and flourishes, it is in a state of being (*kawn*), but when uprooted and burned, it passes from being into corruption (*fasad*). By proof, it is known that being is superior to corruption. Thus, the comfort of earth lies in construction, cultivation, order in home and marketplace, and the soul and form—and its pain lies in ruin.  

Water’s comfort is in its center, and its pain in the opposite. Fire’s comfort is in its center, for it is the most refined; descending lower, it becomes coarse. The refined naturally inclines toward refinement, not coarseness. Thus, when fire remains in the center of air due to oil and a wick, it suffers a kind of pain—for in this state, fire is like *form*, and oil and wick like *matter*. Hence, its refined form is trapped by its coarse matter.  

Similarly, anger and desire in the human soul are like the wick and oil to fire. Just as fire is held back from its center by the wick and oil, the soul is held back from the celestial world (*malakut*) by desire and anger. Therefore, the wise must cultivate their morals and restrain desire and anger.  

-117-  

There are many other traits in humans, but all follow these two—such as greed, envy, hatred, and malice. Greed and envy follow desire, while hatred and malice follow anger.  

When a person indulges in desire, gathering all means to dominate, they resemble a pig—and in that state, their intellect is subservient to swinishness. When they quarrel over worldly matters, they resemble a dog. When they seek sustenance through theft and deceit, they resemble a wolf. When they grow arrogant in all three states, they resemble a lion. When wrath combines fully with pride and cunning, they resemble a bear. When hoarding worldly wealth becomes their obsession, always craving more, they resemble an ant. When they harbor malice without provocation, they resemble a snake. When they harm others out of weakness and fear, unprovoked, they resemble a scorpion. When their desire seeks a mate, they resemble a rooster.  

If all these traits mix with wrath and become natural habits, they become an absolute demon (*div*), leaving nothing of the angelic (*malakuti*) within them. In this state, the rational soul (*nafs-e natiqa*) is imprisoned by the demon.  

What people call "inherited disposition" (*‘aqq*) in parents is this: the soul is the mother, and the intellect the father. Thus, if the wise gradually refine themselves—first reducing food until they are content with a hundred dirhams’ worth per day—their bestial desire will weaken without doubt. Through discipline, they will cultivate dignity, improve their character, and habituate humility. In time, they will necessarily abandon these blameworthy traits.  

In this state, they will be closer to angels than to ordinary humans.  

We further assert that every bad trait in humans stems from desire and anger—and anger itself arises from desire. For in desire, no one is harmed, and though wisdom condemns it, all divine laws permit it. We cannot say that our Prophet—peace be upon him—did not know what we have mentioned, nor that he abstained from marriage. Nor can we say that Solomon—peace be upon him—was misguided, though we know he had three hundred and sixty chambers. Rather, they subjugated desire to the rule of intellect, not pursuing it out of caprice but for the sake of wisdom and the order of the world.  

-118-  

Let no one say or think that we praise desire or that any sage has ever permitted it. God forbid! The root of all corruption lies in attachment to desire—though anger is even more reprehensible.  

In this epistle, there are many exhortations, counsels, verses, and wisdom, which we have mentioned—except for certain proofs omitted here for brevity. Hence, we have avoided elaborating on them. *And God is the Grantor of Success.*
