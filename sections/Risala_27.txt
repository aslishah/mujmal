### Translation of the Fourteenth Epistle on Natural Philosophy Concerning the Essence of the Wisdom of Death  

**In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful**  

When a person reflects upon the sciences and other matters, perceives sensory objects through the senses, and grasps primary truths through the intellect—then turns to the realm of the intellect—the first thing he must do is know his own soul. In philosophical and logical texts, we have mentioned that the investigation of knowledge proceeds along nine aspects:  

1. Whether a thing exists or does not exist.  
2. Once its existence is established, what that thing is.  
3. How many things it comprises.  
4. Which specific thing it is.  
5. How it is (its qualities).  
6. Where it is.  
7. Who or what it is.  
8. Why it exists.  
9. For what purpose it exists—that is, what function it serves.  

Thus, we must first ascertain and understand the nature of the soul by addressing these nine questions.  

**1. Does the soul exist or not?**  
We affirm its existence, for none can deny it given the many wonders and effects that emanate from it.  

**2. How many is it?**  
We say it has no parts except in a conceptual sense, and its parts are not like the parts of physical bodies. When we speak of "parts," the senses understand this only in terms of division—as when something is split apart. But reason demonstrates through proof that in natural forces, there are things that may be analytically divided yet are not physically separated—such as sunlight, the flame of a lamp, the faculties of hearing and sight, and the internal faculties like attraction, digestion, repulsion, and similar powers. Each of these is a "part" in itself, yet they are not physically divisible. Likewise, the degrees of potency in medicines—when we say a certain drug is of two degrees of heat and three degrees of dryness—though these degrees exist within a single substance (whether it weighs a dirham or more or less), the potency within it is natural and indivisible. The same applies to the parts of matter. There is much proof for this, such as the heat in fire and sunlight, or the light in the stars.  

Thus, the soul is present in Zayd and ‘Amr not by being divided between them, but in another manner. If someone were to object: *"If this is so, what benefit does Zayd gain from knowledge if, after departing the body, he will merge with the ignorant essence of ‘Amr?"*—this would be an error. For if it is valid for physical bodies (such as the heat of fire or sunlight) to be separated from them without any distinction between them, then this is even more valid for spirits.  

We observe in the celestial realm that the lights of the stars all exist separately, each following its own nature, and they do not intermingle—this is evident.  

**3. What is it?**  
It is a simple, intelligible substance, as we have established elsewhere.  

**4. Which thing is it?**  
It is that which, by its unique property, transforms a genus into a species—just as it makes a mute animal speak.  

**5. How is it?**  
Its qualities are its specific attributes: luminosity, life, knowledge, the capacity to receive opposites, numerical unity, susceptibility to intellectual indication, absence of place, and lack of color, scent, weight, length, width, or depth. It resides in no physical location.  

**6. Where is it?**  
As we have stated in other epistles, place pertains to the earth and the celestial spheres. But the simple substance (the soul) is beyond the spheres. Since it is indivisible and governs the heavens and earth, the earth and heavens are within it—not it within them. For example, the earth contains the ant, not the ant the earth. Similarly, it is said that the earth is in the air, not the air in the earth.  

In the spiritual epistles, we mentioned that there is an animal in the air, surrounded by sunlight, and it lives by the sun’s heat. Without the sun’s heat, this animal would not exist, and all its innate heat derives from the sun’s power. Thus, the sun is like the soul of this animal, and sunlight envelops it. Hence, it is said that the sun is within the bird—meaning the bird is the sun’s abode, not the reverse.  

The earth’s relation to the universal soul is even less than the bird’s relation to sunlight. Therefore, the soul’s "place" must be understood in this manner.  

**7. Who or what is it?**  
We say that whenever a balanced temperament (its instrument) is formed, then it comes into being.  

**8. Why does it exist?**  
For governance, administration, and nurturing its instrument. It emanates from the Active Intellect, and from the Necessary Being, a perpetual flow of knowledge and wisdom streams toward it.  

Thus, when the Intellect knew that the Necessary Being, in His wisdom, did not permit the creation of an intellect incapable of pure goodness—and that creation itself is a great wisdom—the Intellect brought the soul into existence and continuously bestowed wisdom and goodness upon it so that the worlds might manifest through it: absolute body, the celestial spheres, stars, elements, plants, minerals, and rational animals.  

**9. For what purpose does it exist?**  
As for why it separates after union—so that the act of the Wise may be good, though death itself contains no goodness—this is because the death of a person is like the birth of a child. When a child remains in the mother’s womb for nine months and develops properly, if it does not emerge, it will perish. Its well-being lies in coming out, for the earth, fresh air, sunlight, and wholesome food are far better than the womb’s confinement and the variability of nourishment.  

Thus, its emergence is good, while remaining inside is not. Similarly, the soul’s good, comfort, and sovereignty lie in separation from the body, not in union with it. The body is its hell, while the heavens, the soul, and the intellect are its paradise. Being in paradise is better than being in hell.  

It is proven by demonstration that the birth of the soul is the death of the body, and the death of the body is the birth of the soul. Just as the fetus remains in the womb for nine months and then emerges to enjoy eating, clothing, learning, and speech—so too, when the human soul reaches perfection and separates from the body, it becomes all-seeing, beholding both worlds in their entirety, returning to its mother and father (the soul and the intellect), and benefiting from the effusion of the Almighty.  

Thus, death is wisdom, and one must understand that:  
- The souls of children are potentially wise,  
- The souls of adults are actually wise,  
- The souls of the wise are potentially knowledgeable,  
- The souls of the sages are actually knowledgeable,  
- The souls of the learned are potentially wise,  
- The souls of the sages (who are actually wise) are potentially angelic,  
- And they transition from potentiality to actuality only when they depart the body, for an angel cannot dwell in matter.  

Through these premises, the wise understand that the death of the body is the birth of the soul, the birth of the soul is the death of the body, and the life of the body is the death of the soul.  

**And God knows best and is most wise.**  

[End of Epistle]
