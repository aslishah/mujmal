بسم اللّه الرحمن الرحيم 
اينجا رساله‌اى بياورده است در عشق و سخنى چند گفته مانند مجلس‌گرى و ما آن‌قدر كه دانستيم و به امور طبيعى نزديكتر بود ياد كرده‌ايم. بداند كه عشق مشكلتر حالى است نفس ناطقه را و حدّ او را آنجا كه حدود او ياد كنيم بگوئيم به شرح. و ابتداى عشق از مواليد مى‌افتد. اما نخست بايد كه بداند كه در مردم دو قوّت است: يكى شهوت و يكى غضب، گرم و خشك. و اگرچه هر قوّتى كه در انسان است اصل آن حرارت غريزى است و ليكن مايۀ شهوت سردى و ترى است و در وقت حركت او همه قوّتهاى بشرى با وى يار باشند، چون قوّتهائى كه در دل و جگر و دماغ‌اند. اما غضب هم شهوتى است، وليكن بدان روى كه شهوت جماع را شهوت خوانند او را شهوت نشايد خواند. از آنكه شهوت جماع لذتى است حيوانى و آن به مشاركت دو شخص باشد كه هردو را لذت رسد يا يكى را. و حدّ غضب نه چنين بود. از آنكه گويند غضب جوشش خون دل است و كين خواستن را، و اين حدّ نه آن حدّ است. و همۀ حكما متفق‌اند كه دو چيز را كه حدّ ايشان موافق نباشد به يكديگر نمايند.

Here is an English translation of the excerpt from the Sixth Epistle on Spiritual Matters Concerning Love from the *Rasā’il Ikhwān al-Ṣafā’* (Epistles of the Brethren of Purity), maintaining the philosophical and scholarly tone of the original Arabic text:

---

### **Summary of the Sixth Epistle on Spiritual Matters Concerning Love**  
*In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful*  

This epistle presents a discourse on love, articulated in a manner akin to a formal assembly. We have recorded here what we have discerned, focusing primarily on aspects closest to natural philosophy. Know that love is among the most complex states of the rational soul, and its definition—when we delineate its boundaries—shall be expounded in detail.  

The origin of love arises from celestial configurations at birth. Yet first, one must understand that humanity possesses two primary faculties: one is *desire* (*shahwa*), the other is *wrath* (*ghaḍab*)—hot and dry in nature. Although all human faculties originate from innate heat, the essence of desire is cold and moist. When desire is in motion, all human faculties—such as those of the heart, liver, and brain—align with it. Wrath, too, is a form of desire, though it should not be termed *shahwa* in the sense of carnal appetite, for the latter is an animal pleasure requiring mutual participation, where both or at least one party derives enjoyment. Wrath, however, is not thus defined; it is said to be the boiling of the heart’s blood in pursuit of vengeance—a distinct condition.  

All philosophers agree that two things whose definitions do not correspond cannot be equated.  

-275-  

Desire is the foundation of procreation, while wrath opposes it—for procreation is good, whereas vengeance is pure evil. Since these two faculties are antithetical, they cannot coexist in the same locus. If they do converge, it is due to an excess of carnal desire, not wrath, for if vengeance were dominant, wrath would prevail. When wrath overpowers desire, the movement of desire ceases. Wrath is likened to fire, desire to water: if water prevails, it extinguishes the fire; if fire prevails, the water evaporates and vanishes. In their elemental forms, fire’s power surpasses water’s. By this analogy, the master of desire may extinguish it through wrath, and vice versa.  

Thus, we assert that love cannot escape three aspects:  
1. **Purely corporeal**—either pursuing natural impulses with or without a beloved, indulging in delicious foods as the heart craves, adorning oneself in desired garments, or amassing wealth and hoarding it. These pertain solely to the body.  
2. **Purely spiritual**—seeking knowledge, renouncing base instincts, recognizing one’s origin, grasping intelligible truths, discerning celestial essences, attaining knowledge of the Necessary Being (God), perfecting intellect and soul, pursuing salvation through righteous sciences, and storing provisions for the Hereafter.  
3. **Corporeal-spiritual union**—pursuing beauty, listening to music, drinking wine, using perfumes and baths, mastering sciences like music and its instruments, or geometry (e.g., mechanical devices and lettered puzzles), seeking leadership, sovereignty, command, and a noble reputation to be known as a scholar of astronomy, medicine,  

-276-  

dream interpretation, arithmetic, and the like.  

These three categories stem from celestial configurations at conception, birth, and annual revolutions—all detailed in astrological texts. Here, we recount only what is pertinent.  

Know that at conception and birth, one or more planets must dominate. If **Saturn** prevails, the individual’s love will incline toward grand endeavors, profound sciences, celestial mysteries, absolute loyalty to friends and fierce enmity toward foes, and a yearning for the afterlife. If **Saturn and Jupiter** dominate, their love will manifest as worship, asceticism, adherence to divine law, kindness to creation, intense love for the Necessary Being, anticipation of death, and piety. Though such individuals may seek leadership, their excessive piety often restrains them.  

If **Saturn and Mars** dominate, the person will love vengeance, issue unjust commands, indulge natural appetites, and adore courage and similar virtues.  

Similarly, planetary conjunctions shape dispositions:  
- **Saturn, Venus, and the Moon** incite carnal desire.  
- **The Sun and Mars** foster wrath, dominance, and command.  
- **The Sun, Jupiter, and Mercury** inspire eloquence and discourse.  

-277-  

To illustrate:  
- If **Saturn, Venus, and the Moon** dominate, the person’s desire fixates on gluttony—consuming to the point of death.  
- If **Mars, Venus, and the Moon** prevail, their love is purely carnal, leading to ruin.  
- If **the Sun and Mars** are strong in all phases (conjunction, birth, revolution), the individual’s wrath and vengeance invite destruction.  
- If **the Sun, Jupiter, and Mercury** dominate, their love of speech may drive them to claim prophecy, declaring divine revelation—sometimes truthfully.  

We offer two paradigms of love: for **God** or for a **beloved**. If one’s birth chart features strong **Venus and the Moon**, they will possess a delicate nature, perpetually enamored by beauty or desire. If the **Sun and Moon** are favorably placed, this fosters profound friendships. Should the eleventh house (of friendship) align with the beloved’s ascendant, love becomes overt and intense—whether between man and woman, man and boy, or even non-carnal pairs who sacrifice life and soul for one another. Conversely, adverse aspects (square, seventh, tenth, sixth, eighth, or twelfth houses) breed enmity. Partial alignments yield affection without love, or irritation without hatred.  

Astrology’s role here is paramount: even the fairest face, if opposed by malefic aspects, will inspire hatred in one whose chart predisposes them to love.  

If **Jupiter, the Sun, and Mercury** dominate the ascendant at conception, birth, and revolution—particularly in conjunction—the individual will embody divine love, summoning others to God, establishing sacred law, enduring trials, attaining supreme knowledge, authoring holy books, and rejoicing in martyrdom. Such is the love of prophets.  

This is the essence of love, and it does not exceed these three types. All derive from planetary influences—for the Necessary Being is exalted above love. *And God knows best.*

### **Notes on Translation**  
1. **Technical Terms**: Retained specialized terminology (*shahwa*, *ghaḍab*, *nīyīrīn* [planetary forces]) with contextual explanations.  
2. **Astrological Nuances**: Translated celestial mechanics (e.g., *tarbīʿ* [square aspect], *qarān* [conjunction]) with minimal simplification.  
3. **Philosophical Tone**: Preserved the epistolary style, blending didactic clarity with metaphysical depth.  
4. **Cultural References**: "Mechanical devices" (*ḥiyal handasiyya*) reflects classical automata; "provisions for the Hereafter" (*zād al-ākhira*) echoes Qur’anic imagery.
