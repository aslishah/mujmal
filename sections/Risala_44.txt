### Chapter Four: On the Nature of Prophets and Their Miracles  

Know that the Almighty, out of His boundless generosity and perfect grace, created a simple, luminous substance—an active, governing intellect of the universe. At times, it is called the "Active Intellect," at others the "Universal Soul," sometimes the "Simple Substance," and at other times "Nature," for it manifests through actions. From the existence of this soul-substance came forth the heavens and the celestial dominion, the elements, the earth, and all that lies within it—as we have mentioned in several places.  

The first movement arose from the interaction between the Universal Soul and prime matter (hyle). From movement came heat, from heat came stillness, from stillness came cold, and from cold came moisture. Thus, the four elements emerged. These four remained below the sphere of the moon due to their density. Earth, the heaviest, settled at the center; water gathered around it; air encircled water; and fire enveloped air. Through the interaction of these four elements and the influence of the celestial spheres, animals, plants, and minerals came into being.  

Nature, which initiated movement, began to govern, and vast ages passed without the existence of man. Then, through the unique properties of the heavens and the subtlety of the Universal Soul, a perfectly balanced temperament was formed, and from it, humanity emerged. Since man’s temperament was in perfect equilibrium, the "Universal Soul" and the "Active Intellect" governed within him, giving rise to speech and the perpetuation of his lineage. From them came both the elite and the common folk, and great tribes arose.  

Through the influences of the stars and their unique properties—manifested in great cycles and epochs—people sought kingship, established dominion over the world, and prophets appeared. They called mankind to God, established divine laws, and guided creation. Know, then, that no prophet has ever arisen except from a noble lineage—all were either princes or descendants of prophets. The origin of their tribe lies in a great cycle (qarn) or epoch (dawr), wherein a mighty figure rises, seizes power, subjects the people to his rule, exacts tribute, and crushes all who oppose him, leaving none to challenge his authority. Such a man is called the "Lord of the Cycle" (Ṣāḥib al-Qarn).  

The descendants of this figure spread widely—just as, in our current epoch, it was Adam (peace be upon him). Wherever his progeny settled, their lineage endured, and from among them great leaders emerged until another "Lord of the Cycle" arose. The hidden decree of this cycle and the secret of this age necessitated the appearance of a prophet—one born of the noblest origin and lineage, under the ascendant of the cycle, so that its influence would manifest in him.  

As for why others born under the same celestial configuration do not attain his stature—this must be understood through the books of astrology. The miracles (karāmāt) granted to such a person stem from the necessary grace of the Divine, attached to him alone, not from any other cause. The tenth, seventh, and ninth celestial houses, along with their ruling planets, possess immense power.  

This individual becomes God’s trusted representative and the vicegerent of the Almighty on earth. His soul attains such power that he acts upon the four elements, and an angel in human form appears to him. Through him, divine law and tradition are established; angels speak with him; the creatures of the world submit to him; they accept his religion, and he attains sovereignty. The power and properties of the celestial spheres become innate to him—his body a king among bodies, his soul a king among souls. His reign endures according to the decree of his celestial ascendant, his wisdom, and his felicity.  

For the duration of that dominion, whoever opposes his house or abandons him will suffer the misfortunes of time—reduced to poverty, humiliation, and misery. Nothing will go right for him; he will live in torment, and after death, his soul will face peril—as we shall discuss in the chapter on the afterlife.  

Thus, if a sage or philosopher encounters such a person, he must submit to him and choose obedience. Once, I debated a great scholar of the age regarding the miracles of the Prophet and the validation of divine law. He replied, "Do not speak foolishly! If a sorcerer or illusionist were to appear and the people accepted him, would it be necessary to submit to him? Especially when it comes to a prophet, the Lord of the Cycle?" This statement contains a clear allusion.  

There are decisive proofs regarding the essence of prophethood and its nature, but to elaborate would prolong the discussion, and that is a matter for another place.  

And God knows best.

3. **Prophetic Authority**: The cyclical view of history ("قران" and "دور") reflects the Ikhwan’s belief in astrological and metaphysical determinism.  
4. **Scholarly Tone**: The translation mirrors the original’s didactic style, blending theology, cosmology, and political theory.
