### Summary of the Sixth Epistle on Natural Philosophy Concerning the Essence of Nature  
In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.  

Know that Nature, according to some of the ancients, is a power emanating from the Universal Soul in all celestial and terrestrial bodies, from the outermost sphere (*falak al-muhit*) to the center of the Earth. The logicians say that Nature is the primary principle of motion, which first arises from the First Intention—the act of the Necessary Being, which is pure good devoid of any evil. For the act of the Necessary Being is the Intellect, and the Soul is not the direct act of the Necessary Being but rather the act of Its act. Similarly, prime matter (*hyle*) is the act of Its act, and so on, down to the center of the Earth. These two descriptions of Nature are closely related and do not contradict one another. Hence, the sages say: *"The Intellect, the Soul, Nature, and the Sphere are as one."*  

Thus, it must be understood that the entire cosmos consists of eleven spherical layers, with the Sun at their center: five above and five below. On the upper side are the spheres of Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, the fixed stars, and the outermost sphere (*falak al-muhit*). On the lower side—referred to as the "sublunary" realm—are the spheres of Venus, Mercury, the Moon, the sphere of air, and the Earth.  

-179-  

All that we have mentioned constitutes a single body, with the Universal Soul as its life, and the Intellect as the life of that life. This totality is a rational, discerning, and volitional animal. The Earth within this great animal is like a small, round pellet in its intestines, and the animals upon the Earth are like creatures within that pellet—insignificant in comparison. All that exists on Earth is but a part of this great animal, which knows no death, for its life is essential, whereas the life of earthly beings is accidental—like the humors within an animal.  

The seven planets correspond to its seven organs: the heart, liver, brain, stomach, spleen, lungs, and kidneys. Everything on Earth receives its form from them, and whatever is destined to exist first manifests in the celestial sphere, then in the elements, then in animals, then in plants, and finally in minerals. The more refined a thing is, the more swiftly and profoundly it receives influence. Each planet governs a specific region on Earth: Saturn rules over desolate valleys, harsh deserts, and mountains; broad roads, streams, and rivers—great and small—are under the dominion of Capricorn and Aquarius. Jupiter, Sagittarius, and Pisces govern mosques, temples, churches, schools, places of Quranic recitation, courts, noble assemblies, festival grounds, pulpits, monasteries, and Quranic schools. Mars, Aries, and Scorpio rule fire temples, places of slaughter, battlefields, thieves' dens, and wild beasts. Venus, Libra, and Taurus preside over gardens, places of leisure, gatherings of joy, wine, pleasure, companionship, and laughter. Mercury, Gemini, and Virgo govern marketplaces, artisans, debate halls of knowledge,  

-180-  

administrative offices, and the scribal arts. When these planets are in favorable positions, all these affairs prosper accordingly.  

The proof that the cosmos is a single animal lies in observing ourselves: we possess a body, a soul, and faculties within that body—such as hearing, sight, smell, taste, touch, imagination, thought, memory, estimation, common sense, the heart, liver, lungs, stomach, spleen, gallbladder, and brain—as well as seven limbs: the head, feet, hands, back, belly, and the male and female reproductive organs. There are also seven other powers in the body: attraction, retention, digestion, expulsion, nourishment, growth, and reproduction. The soul is the sovereign, and the entire body obeys its command. Likewise, upon examining the celestial spheres, we find that they possess all these aspects—indeed, they are a more perfect animal, and man is but a microcosm of it.  

If one reflects deeply upon this epistle, no doubt or difficulty shall remain in the path of wisdom and divine law—save that which is resolved. We reiterate here that from the sphere of the Sun, a spiritual power permeates the entire cosmos—through the spheres, stars, elements, and all corporeal beings, universal and particular. The order of the world depends upon it, and the perfection of existence lies with it—just as the innate heat of the heart sustains the entire body. The Sun is the heart of this great animal, and its life derives from it. The philosophers call this solar power *"the spiritualities of the Sun,"* while the divine law names it a mighty angel with armies and attendants—and Israfil is one of its powers.  

Similarly, from the body of Saturn, a spiritual power extends throughout the cosmos, from the outermost sphere to the center. Through this power, prime matter receives all forms, and it is called the retentive power of this great animal. Just as the melancholic humor from the spleen permeates the entire body—and the retentive power of the body depends upon it—this planet is the spleen of the great animal. The philosophers call this power *"the spiritualities of Saturn,"* and the divine law names it an angel with vast armies and countless helpers—and the Angel of Death is one of its powers.  

Likewise, from Mars, a spiritual power pervades the cosmos, universal and particular. The pursuit of ends and the quest for nobility arise from it—just as the choleric humor from the gallbladder spreads throughout the body. The philosophers call this *"the spiritualities of Mars,"* and the divine law names it an angel commanding innumerable hosts—and Gabriel is one of its powers. This planet is the gallbladder of the great animal.  

From Jupiter, spiritual powers infuse the entire cosmos, universal and particular. The equilibrium of temperaments and the cause of all good depend upon it—and by it, the human body thrives. This planet is the liver of the great animal. The philosophers call this *"the spiritualities of Jupiter,"* and the divine law names it an angel with vast armies—and Ridwan, the keeper of Paradise, is one of its powers.  

From Venus, a spiritual power adorns the cosmos, bestowing beauty and splendor upon existence—just as nourishment from the stomach reaches the liver and the entire body, and all pleasure, delight, and joy arise from it. The philosophers call this *"the spiritualities of Venus,"* and the divine law names it an angel with vast hosts—and the houris of Paradise derive from this power. This planet is the stomach of the great animal.  

From Mercury, a universal power connects with all corporeal beings. Knowledge, intuition, inspiration, revelation, and rare sciences arise from it—just as thought, discernment, and rational proofs emerge from the brain. The philosophers call this *"the spiritualities of Mercury,"* and the divine law names it an angel with armies and attendants—and the noble scribes (*Kiram al-Katibin*) are among its powers. This planet is the brain of the great animal.  

From the Moon, a power permeates the cosmos, universal and particular—a mediating force between the two worlds, just as the lungs sustain the entire body through respiration and innate heat. The philosophers call this *"the spiritualities of the Moon,"* and the divine law names it an angel from whom blessings, sustenance, and prosperity flow—and Mika'il is among its powers. It is said that the knowledge of mankind is carried to the heavens by these angels.  

From each of the fixed stars, a power connects with the cosmos, enabling specific actions.  

-182-  

Thus, if one contemplates the truth of what we have described, they will realize that there is no injustice in the cosmos. Saturn and Mars are not malefic, death is not tyranny, and killing is not oppression. Let them judge these matters as they would judge themselves, setting aside bias and passion. Let them examine human anatomy and reflect: Were it not for Venus (as the gallbladder), by what humor would the stomach digest food? For unless bile from Venus is secreted into the stomach, food would not ferment—remaining raw or producing corrupt humors. Likewise, consider Saturn and the spleen: Were it not for Saturn, the spleen would not draw dry melancholy from the liver, and the liver would be overwhelmed by dry melancholy, leading to ruin. Without Saturn, there would be no death after old age—which would be a great burden.  

If any of these planets were absent, existence would be disordered and deficient, as detailed in astronomical texts. Whoever studies this epistle with due attention—understanding the purpose behind the creation of the spheres and stars—will encounter no insoluble difficulty and need not seek excessive knowledge. Further elaboration will come in another epistle. Here, this much suffices.  

And God knows best what is right—to Him is the return and the final destination.
