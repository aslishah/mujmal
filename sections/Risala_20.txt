### **Summary of the Seventh Epistle on Natural Sciences Concerning the Genera of Plants**  
*In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful*  

Know that God the Exalted, by His grace, created the four elements beneath the sphere of the moon. The matter of these three kinds—animals, plants, and minerals—derives from these four elements, and their regulator is Nature, which is the principle of primary motion. The celestial spheres and stars are the agents that continuously bestow the power of governance upon these four elements.  

Nature is like a carpenter, the celestial spheres are like his hands, axe, and saw, and the elements are like wood and iron from which beds, doors, and thrones are made. Animals, plants, and minerals are like those crafted beds and thrones. Every plant in the world arises from its own kind, just as animals do, and remains true to its origin—just as an animal comes from semen, a bird from an egg, and crops from seeds. In summary, no plant emerges from a seed of another kind, just as nothing but a human is born from human semen, and no beast arises from it.  

From wood, neither a shirt nor linen is produced; from mercury, no ruby is formed; from a ruby, no gold is derived; and from minerals, no plant arises. Just as among minerals, gypsum is the basest and ruby the noblest, among animals, scorpions and insects are the lowliest while humans are the most excellent. Among plants, wild growths are the least, while the date palm is the finest—for though it appears as a plant, in essence, it resembles an animal: if its head is cut off, it dies; if a nail is driven into its heart, it perishes; and if a saw is placed upon its trunk when it bears no fruit, and it is threatened, *"If you bear no fruit next year, we will cut you down,"* it will yield fruit. Similarly, just as animals are bred by placing the male’s seed in the female’s mouth to induce pregnancy, the date palm, though not an animal in form, possesses a soul and grows.  

Though its seed is its cause, the greatest cause of the plant is water—for even if the seed, earth, and sun (all three being causes of plants, just as other causes exist for animals and minerals) are present, without water, neither plant nor animal would exist. We observe that in saline lands, where water exists, plants like *ushnān* grow. Animals dwell beneath the earth without sunlight, yet without water, no creature or plant could endure anywhere.  

No sage denies the vegetative soul, and the nourishment of plants resembles that of animals. The examples we have given concerning the liver apply equally to plants, for the liver possesses seven faculties: attraction, retention, digestion, expulsion, nutrition, generation, and growth.  
- *Attraction* draws water to itself,  
- *retention* holds it,  
- *digestion* breaks it down,  
- *expulsion* distributes it to the branches,  
- *nutrition* converts it into chyme (the essence of what it needs),  
- *generation* produces new growth from the branches,  
- and *growth* sustains its development.  

The term "plant" encompasses all vegetation, as logicians say, being a genus. In this epistle, the names of plants and trees—such as apple, quince, cucumber, and melon—along with their varied forms, have been mentioned. Yet there is no knowledge here that logicians might use in demonstrative reasoning.  

We say: The final rank of plants is connected to the first rank of animals, the final rank of animals to the first rank of humans, and the final rank of humans to the first rank of angels. After animals, the noblest thing is the plant, just as after angels, the noblest thing is the human. This much suffices.  

Thereafter, the epistle on animals was presented, but it contained no knowledge fit for mention in demonstrative proof, so we omitted it. *And God grants success.*  

***[The epistle on animals was originally the twentieth but was omitted.]***

### **Notes on Translation:**  
1. **Philosophical Nuances:** The text blends Aristotelian natural philosophy with Neoplatonic hierarchy (minerals → plants → animals → humans → angels).  
2. **Technical Terms:**  
   - *Nature (ṭabīʿa)* as the governing principle aligns with classical Islamic physics.  
   - The "vegetative soul" (*nafs nabātī*) reflects the Aristotelian tripartite soul (vegetative, animal, rational).  
3. **Metaphors:** The carpenter analogy (Nature as craftsman, elements as materials) underscores the structured cosmology.  
4. **Omissions:** The original occasionally references omitted sections (e.g., the animal epistle), preserved here for fidelity.  

This translation aims to balance readability with scholarly precision, retaining the interwoven metaphysical and empirical reasoning characteristic of the *Ikhwān al-Ṣafā*.
