### **Summary of the Sixth Epistle on Natural Philosophy Concerning the Essence of Nature**  
*In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful*  

Know that Nature, according to some of the ancients, is a power emanating from the Universal Soul in all celestial and terrestrial bodies—from the outermost celestial sphere to the center of the Earth. The logicians say that Nature is the primary principle of motion, which first arises from the Prime Intention, which is the act of the Necessary Being (God). That act is pure good, containing no evil, for the act of the Necessary Being is the Intellect, while the Soul is not the act of the Necessary Being but rather the act of Its act. Likewise, prime matter (*hylē*) is the act of His act, and so on, descending to the center of the Earth. These two definitions of Nature are closely related and do not differ significantly. Hence, the sages say: *"The Intellect, the Soul, Nature, and the celestial sphere are as one."*  

Therefore, it must be understood that the entire cosmos consists of eleven concentric spheres, with the Sun at their center—five above and five below. On the upper side are the spheres of Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, the fixed stars, and the outermost sphere. On the lower side are the spheres of Venus, Mercury, the Moon, the sphere of air, and the Earth.  

All these together form a single body, with the Universal Soul as its life, and the Intellect as the life of that life. This whole is a rational, discerning, and volitional animal. The Earth within this great animal is like a small, round pellet in its bowels, and the creatures upon it are like animals within that pellet, occupying no significant space. Everything upon the Earth exists in relation to that great animal, which knows no death, for its life is essential, whereas the life of earthly beings is accidental—like the humors within an animal.  

The seven planets correspond to its seven organs: the heart, liver, brain, stomach, spleen, lungs, and kidneys. Everything on Earth receives its form from them, and whatever comes into existence originates first in the celestial sphere, then in the elements, then in animals, plants, and minerals. The more refined a thing is, the more swiftly and perfectly it receives influence. Each planet governs a specific region on Earth:  

- **Saturn** rules over desolate valleys, harsh deserts, mountains, highways, rivers, and canals—this is the domain of Capricorn and Aquarius.  
- **Jupiter, Sagittarius, and Pisces** govern mosques, temples, churches, schools, places of Qur’anic recitation, courts, noble assemblies, festivals, pulpits, and monasteries.  
- **Mars, Aries, and Scorpio** rule fire temples, slaughterhouses, battlefields, thieves' dens, and wild beasts' lairs.  
- **Venus, Libra, and Taurus** preside over gardens, pleasure grounds, wine gatherings, merriment, companionship, and laughter.  
- **Mercury, Gemini, and Virgo** govern marketplaces, artisans, scholarly debates, administrative offices, and the writing of knowledge.  

When these planets are in favorable positions, their influences bring harmony and fulfillment.  

The proof that the cosmos is a single animal lies in observing ourselves: we possess a body, a soul, and faculties such as hearing, sight, smell, taste, touch, imagination, thought, memory, estimation, and common sense—along with organs like the heart, liver, lungs, stomach, spleen, gallbladder, and brain. Similarly, the celestial spheres possess all these in a more perfect form, and man is but a microcosm of this great being.  

Whoever reflects deeply on this epistle will find no doubt or ambiguity in the path of wisdom and divine law, for all shall be resolved. Know that from the sphere of the Sun, a spiritual power extends throughout the cosmos—through the spheres, planets, elements, and all bodies, universal and particular. The order of the world depends upon it, just as the innate heat of the heart sustains the body. The Sun is the heart of this great animal, and its life flows from it. The philosophers call this solar power the *"Spiritual Influences of the Sun,"* while the religious tradition names it a mighty angel with vast armies—of which Isrāfīl is one manifestation.  

Likewise, from Saturn’s mass extends a spiritual force that binds the cosmos from the outermost sphere to the center. Prime matter receives forms through this power, which the sages call the *"Spiritual Influences of Saturn."* Religion designates it as an angel with innumerable hosts—of which the Angel of Death is one function.  

Similarly, Mars emits a spiritual force that drives ambition and nobility, just as yellow bile spreads through the body. The philosophers term this the *"Spiritual Influences of Mars,"* and religion calls it a martial angel—with Gabriel as one of its powers.  

Jupiter’s spiritual forces bring equilibrium and blessings, nurturing humanity. It corresponds to the liver of the cosmic animal, and its angelic counterpart is Riḍwān, keeper of Paradise.  

Venus bestows beauty and delight upon the world, akin to the stomach distributing nourishment. Its angelic host includes the *ḥūr al-‘ayn* (celestial maidens).  

Mercury governs knowledge, inspiration, and revelation, functioning as the brain of the great animal. Its angels include the *kirām al-kātibīn* (noble scribes).  

The Moon mediates between the two worlds, sustaining life as the lungs do. Its angel is Mikā’īl, through whom blessings and sustenance flow.  

The fixed stars each exert their own influences. Thus, when one contemplates these truths, they will see that there is no injustice in the cosmos: Saturn and Mars are not malefic, death is not tyranny, and killing is not oppression. Let the seeker reflect on human anatomy—if Venus did not supply bile, how would the stomach digest food? Without Saturn, the spleen would not draw excess black bile from the liver, leading to imbalance. Without Saturn, there would be no death after old age, which would be a greater burden.  

Whoever studies this epistle with due attention and understands the purpose behind the creation of the spheres and planets will encounter no insoluble difficulty nor need excessive learning. Further elaboration will come in another epistle. Here, this much suffices.  

*And God knows best what is correct—to Him is the return and the final destination.*
