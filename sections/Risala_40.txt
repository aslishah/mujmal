--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION ---

### Summary of the Ninth Epistle on Spiritual Matters Concerning Causes and Effects  
In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.  

Know that the endeavor of all people of knowledge, who are attributed to the cognizance of God, is the recognition of realities and the nature of things as they truly are—distinguishing between genera and species, the specific properties of each kind, and the questions and answers pertaining to them. These are none other than: *Is it? What is it? How much is it? Which is it? How is it? Where is it? When is it? Why is it? Who is it?*  

-291-  

Each of these questions has a distinct answer, none resembling the other, and whenever a difficulty arises concerning any existing thing, these nine questions are posed about it. If the answer is given in accordance with reality, speaking of the essential differentia without sophistry, then what is known will be truth and proof. But if it is not the essential differentia, it will be like a mere narration—either false or merely persuasive, which is called "satisfactory."  

First, we say: Does the world exist or not? By the term "world," we mean everything other than the Necessary Existent (God), for it may be called the world, or each of its parts may individually be called a world—such as the world of intellect, the world of soul, the world of the celestial realm and spirits, the world of the spheres, the higher world, the lower world, the world of generation and corruption, the world of plants, the world of animals, and the world of humans. Likewise, a genus along with its species may metaphorically be called a world, and from this arises the saying that there are eighteen thousand worlds. Thus, if one speaks of the world of ants or the world of particles, it is permissible.  

Now, with the eye of observation, we see that the world exists. If by this existence one means the world of spirits, we know without doubt that bodies, insofar as they are bodies, do not move on their own; plants do not grow; the celestial spheres do not rotate by themselves without a soul to move them; and if the animal’s body were not aided by the soul, it would never move. The proof of this is the death of an animal, for death is nothing but the separation of the soul from the body. Thus, by this proof, it is established that both the world of spirits and the world of bodies exist—sometimes separate and sometimes united. There are also things whose soul and body are never separated, such as the higher world, the celestial spheres, and the stars.  

Thus, the answer to *"Is it?"* has been given by proof.  

As for *"What is it?"*, the answer is that, in summary, it is a single soul connected to a single body, governing it through wisdom and providence—as is evident to the eye of observation, such as the rotation of the sphere, the heat of the sun, and the actions of the stars, as mentioned in the epistle *"Man as the Macrocosm."* Thus, the answer to *"What is it?"* is established.  

The condition is that the answer to *"What is it?"* must be given from the nearest genus, and when the world is mentioned in its entirety, there is no other genus to which it belongs as a species. Thus, the answer is correct.  

As for *"How much is it?"*—that is, how many things constitute the world?—we say it is a soul and a body. The details of this soul have been discussed in the epistles on *Generation and Corruption*, *Heaven and the World*, and others.  

As for *"Which is it?"*, it is evident to the senses and the intellect that it extends from beyond the outermost sphere to the center of the earth, comprising in detail the soul, the intellect, the spheres, the stars, the elements, minerals, plants, and animals. All of this answers *"How much?"*, while *"Which?"* is answered by *"It."*  

As for *"How is it?"*, it is according to the nature attached to it—qualities such as color, smell, and taste, which are three genera, arise from simple or composite natures, such as heat and cold, dryness and moisture, heat and dryness, cold and moisture, heat and moisture, cold and dryness, and the balanced state, as well as the fifth nature, which pertains to the spheres and stars.  

As for *"Where is it?"*, it stands within the Universal Soul, like a child in its mother’s womb.  

As for *"When is it?"*—that is, how long will it last?—it will last forever, because the agent of this act and the intender of this intention is not a nature that decays. Every agent acts either by command, by nature, or by mercy. Necessarily, the world is the act of an agent, since it is not the Necessary Existent. We have said that the act of the Necessary Existent is pure mercy, and His act is compassion upon contingent beings. Thus, if the Universal Soul acts by the command of the Necessary Existent, it will never cease, for mercy and compassion cannot rightfully cease from the Necessary Existent. Since compassion is from the Necessary Existent, and the Necessary Existent is never without generosity, it will never cease.  

If we say it acts by nature, we have already stated that there is no nature in the soul, the spheres, or the stars—thus, it must be mercy. The mercy of the soul comes from the intellect, the mercy of the intellect upon the soul comes from the Necessary Existent, and the mercy of the Necessary Existent is from the Necessary Existent and will never cease. Thus, it will endure forever.  

As for *"Why is it?"*—that is, for what reason?—it is so that the effect of the Wise One’s wisdom may appear, and this meaning requires no further argument.  

As for *"Who is it?"*—that is, for whose sake?—we shall return to the discussion of causes to avoid repetition.  

Similarly, every statement and question should inquire into the essence of the thing with brevity. We say, as the philosophers have said: *"Nature does nothing in vain"*—meaning nature performs no act that is corruption. If something appears corrupt to us, it is due to our ignorance; otherwise, it is for some greater good. Thus, the truth is that there is no corruption in the world. What is called "corruption" pertains to accidents, not to substance or body, and no corruption is more apparent than the killing of a human body. But if seen truthfully, this is not corruption—rather, it is the enlivening of the soul, and the enlivening of the soul is pure good. This discourse is best left for its proper place.  

Now, concerning causes: Whatever exists by itself, while the existence of another thing depends upon it, the first is called the *cause* and the second the *effect*. Among existing things, some have one cause, such as the intellect and the soul; some have two causes, such as the spheres and the stars; and some have four causes, such as minerals and plants.  

The first cause of all existents is the wisdom, mercy, and compassion of the Necessary Existent. The Active Intellect is His effect, the intellect is the cause of the soul, and the soul is its effect. The soul is the cause of prime matter, and prime matter is its effect. Prime matter is the cause of absolute body, and absolute body is its effect. Absolute body is the cause of the spheres and stars, and the spheres and stars are its effects. The spheres and stars are the causes of the elements, and the elements are their effects. The elements are the causes of plants, minerals, and animals, and plants, minerals, and animals are their effects.  

We have said that no analogy is as evident for deducing the unseen as number. The *"one"* is the first of all numbers because it is the cause of two, and two is the cause of three—and so on in this natural order. It cannot be said that one is the cause of three without the mediation of two, which is the middle term. Similarly, the human form in the womb arises through intermediary causes. The Necessary Existent does not inscribe the human form directly in menstrual blood without the mediation of time and space, for if there were no time and space, there would be no generated beings in the world.  

Each generated thing has four causes:  
1. Material,  
2. Formal,  
3. Efficient,  
4. Final.  

For example, if there were no builder, there would be no house; if there were no earth (the material), there would be no house; if there were no form of the house, it would not be called a house; and if it were not for the purpose of dwelling in it, there would be no house. For Zayd builds the house to sit in it, whether for himself or for another. Thus, when Zayd completes the house, he is the cause of the house in the sense that he assembles the wood, bricks, and stones in a specific form—but Zayd is not the cause of the house’s existence, only the cause of its construction.  

If a passerby lodges in that house, or a caravan, or some animals, and the house collapses due to a cause such as rain, an earthquake, or the wear of time, killing those inside, Zayd is not the cause of their death. The cause of their death was the weakness of the wall and the rain, not Zayd. Thus, the collapse of the house, its decay, and the rain are from nature, not from Zayd’s action.  

Similarly, one who builds a ship does so for the comfort of creation—this much suffices, even if he himself has no need for it.  

And God knows best what is right.
