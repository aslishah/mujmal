### Summary of the First Epistle on Spiritual Matters Concerning Intellectual Principles  
In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.  

Know that the principles of a thing are that which serve as its origin and cause in knowledge, and these have been established in many sciences. Yet one must acknowledge that it is not incumbent upon the speaker to encompass all sciences for a single individual. These principles are called such because they consist of a verbal premise in syllogistic reasoning, and none may deny them. For Pythagoras, the sage, was the first in this age to compose a book on the science of arithmetic, compiling its calculations and properties. He stated that all existents conform to numbers, and whoever comprehends numbers—their genera, species, and properties—understands the genera and species of existents, their quantities, and their causes. This is because every science is illuminated through mathematical exemplars. Thus, he said: "The Almighty, Exalted is He, created all things in pairs, triads, quartets, quintets, and the like."  

As for "pairs," they include: matter and form, substance and accident, cause and effect, simple and composite, subtle and dense, transparent and opaque, light and darkness, motion and rest, hot and cold, moist and dry, heavy and light, beneficial and harmful, good and evil, correct and erroneous, truth and falsehood, male and female—*"And of all things We created in pairs."* (Quran 51:49).  

As for "triads," they include: length, width, and depth; line, surface, and body; past, present, and future; necessary, possible, and impossible; mathematical, natural, and divine.  

As for "quartets," they include: the four natures—heat, cold, moisture, and dryness; fire, air, water, and earth; blood, phlegm, yellow bile, and black bile; spring, autumn, summer, and winter. And so on, extending to higher units and tens.  

They say: "One is the origin of all numbers, just as God, the Exalted, is the Creator of all existents." And just as "one" has no limit because it has no genus, so too does God, the Exalted, have no limit because He has no genus. And just as "one" is present in all numbers, God, the Exalted, is present with all existents. And just as all numbers perish while "one" remains, all existents perish while God, the Exalted, remains. And just as numbers arise from the repetition of "one," the existence of all beings arises from the creation of God.  

Two is the first numerical pair and the foundation of calculation, just as the First Intellect is the foundation of all other things. And just as three follows two, the soul follows the intellect. The fourth is like prime matter, the fifth like nature, the sixth like absolute body, the seventh like the celestial spheres, the eighth like the elements, and the ninth like generated beings. And just as nine is the last of the units, generated beings are the last of universal existents. Minerals correspond to the tens, plants to the hundreds, and animals to the thousands.  

Numbers may be even or odd, whole or fractional. Among the ranks of existence, that which belongs to the realm of spirits is better suited by odd nature, while that which belongs to the corporeal world is better suited by even nature. That which pertains to the celestial spheres is better suited by whole numbers, while that which pertains to generation and corruption is better suited by fractions.  

Know that body is one of the existents—a composite substance made of two simple substances: one is matter, and the other is form. Matter is the substance that receives form, and form is the substance. Matter is like iron, and form is like the shape of a knife. The first form that matter accepts is length, width, and depth. Then, through the medium of body, it accepts all other forms, such as the triangle, square, pentagon, and the like.  

Prime matter is an effect of the soul, and the First Intellect is a substance brought into existence by the Necessary Being. It is a spiritual substance—eternal, complete, and perfect. The cause of its completion and perfection is the emanation from the Necessary Being, the cause of its permanence is its connection to the Divine Emanation, the cause of its completion is its acceptance of this emanation, and the cause of its perfection is that which is above it—the Necessary Being. Thus, the cause of the soul’s existence is the intellect.  

This is the opinion of Pythagoras the Sage on this matter. And God knows best.
