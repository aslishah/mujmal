### Summary of the Fourteenth Epistle on Natural Philosophy Concerning the Essence of Death as Wisdom  
*In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful*  

When a person reflects upon the sciences and other matters, perceives sensory objects through the senses, and grasps primary principles through the intellect—thereby ascending to the intellectual realm—the first thing they must do is know their own soul. In philosophical and logical texts, we have mentioned that the examination of the sciences proceeds along nine aspects:  
1. Whether a thing exists or does not exist.  
2. Once its existence is established, determining what that thing is.  
3. How many things it consists of.  
4. Which specific thing it is.  
5. How it is (its qualities).  
6. Where it is.  
7. Who or what it is.  
-229-  
8. Why it exists.  
9. For what purpose it exists—that is, what function it serves.  

Thus, we must first understand and address the state of the soul through these nine inquiries. We say: Does the soul exist or not? Its existence cannot be denied, given the numerous effects and wonders that emanate from it.  

As for *how many* it is, we say: It has no parts, except in a conceptual sense, and its parts are not like the parts of physical bodies. When we speak of "parts," the senses understand nothing by this term except division—as when things are torn apart and separated from one another. However, reason demonstrates through proof that within the natural faculties of things, there are aspects that may be considered divisible yet are not actually separated—such as the light of the sun, the flame of a lamp, the faculties of hearing and sight, and the internal faculties like attraction, digestion, repulsion, and similar powers. Each of these is a part in itself, yet it is not divisible. Likewise, consider the degrees of potency in medicines: we say that a certain drug is hot in the second degree and dry in the third degree. Though these degrees exist within a single portion of the drug—weighing a *dram* or more or less—the potency within it is natural and cannot be divided. The same applies to the parts of the celestial spheres. There are many proofs for this, such as the heat in fire and sunlight, or the light in the stars.  

Thus, the soul is present in Zayd and ‘Amr not by being divided between them but in another manner. If someone were to think: "If this is so, what benefit does Zayd gain from knowledge if, after departing from the body, he will merge with the ignorant essence of ‘Amr?"—this would be an error. For if it is permissible for bodies—such as the heat of fire or the warmth of the sun—to be separated from them without any distinction between them, then they do not intermingle. For example, when wood is burned in fire, its moisture and oiliness burn away and  
-230-  
separate from one another without mixing.  

If this meaning holds true for bodies, it is all the more valid for spirits. We ourselves observe in the higher world that the lights of the stars all exist separately, each descending from the fifth nature (the celestial realm) to a particular nature, without ever intermingling—and this is evident.  

As for *what* it is, it is a simple, intelligible substance, as we have established elsewhere.  

Regarding *which* thing it is, it is that which, by its specific property, transforms a genus into a species—as it renders a non-speaking animal into a speaking one.  

As for *how* it is, its qualities are its specific attributes—such as luminosity, life, and knowledge—and its capacity to accept opposing attributes. It is singular in number, can be indicated by intellectual reference, has no location, and possesses no color, scent, weight, length, width, or depth. It resides in no place.  

Concerning *where* it is, we have said in other epistles that place belongs to the earth and the heavens, whereas the simple substance is beyond the heavens. Since it is indivisible and governs the heavens and the earth, the earth and heavens are within it—not it within them. For example, the earth and an ant: the ant resides within the earth, not the earth within the ant. Similarly, it is said that the earth resides in the air, not the air in the earth. In the spiritual epistles, we mentioned that there is an animal in the air, enveloped by sunlight, and it lives by the heat of the sun. Were it not for the sun’s heat, this animal would not exist, and every innate heat within it derives from the sun’s power. Thus, the sun holds the station of the soul for this animal, and sunlight surrounds it. Hence, it is said that the sun resides within the bird—meaning the bird is the sun’s abode.  

Compared to the Universal Soul, the earth is even less significant than the bird is to sunlight. Therefore, the abode of the soul must be conceived in this manner.  

As for *who or what* it is, we say: Whenever a balanced temperament (its instrument) comes into being, then it exists.  

Regarding *why* it exists, it is for governance, administration, and the cultivation of its instrument. It emerges from the Active Intellect, and from the Necessary Being, a perpetual effusion of knowledge and wisdom flows to it. Thus, when the Intellect knew that the Necessary Being, in His wisdom, did not permit the creation of an intellect incapable of pure goodness—and that in creation lies great wisdom—the Intellect, too, brought the soul into existence and endowed it with a continuous effusion of wisdom and goodness, so that the worlds might manifest through it: absolute body, the heavens, the stars, the elements, plants, minerals, and rational animals. Thus, it exists for this purpose.  

-231-  

As for why it separates after union—so that the act of the Wise may be good, while in death there is no good—this is because the death of a person is like the birth of a child. When an infant completes nine months in the mother’s womb and develops properly, if it does not emerge, its corruption ensues; its well-being lies in coming forth, for the earth, the gentle air, the sun’s rays, and pleasant foods are far better than the womb’s confines and the goodness or unpleasantness of its nourishment. Thus, its emergence is good, whereas remaining there is not.  

Similarly, the state of the human soul is such that its goodness, comfort, and sovereignty lie in separation from the body, not in union with it. For the body is its hell, while the heavens, the soul, and the intellect are its paradise. Thus, being in paradise is better than being in hell.  

By proof, it is known that the birth of the soul is the death of the body, and the death of the body is the birth of the soul. Just as the fetus remains in the mother’s womb for nine months and then emerges to enjoy food, clothing, learning, and speech, so too, when the human soul reaches perfection and separates from the body, it becomes all-seeing, beholds both worlds in their entirety, and returns to its mother and father—the soul and the intellect—receiving benefit from the effusion of the Almighty.  

Thus, death is wisdom, and one must know that:  
- The souls of children are potentially wise,  
- The souls of adults are actually wise,  
- The souls of the wise are potentially knowledgeable,  
- The souls of the sages are actually knowledgeable,  
- The souls of the learned are potentially wise,  
- The souls of the sages who are actually wise are potentially angelic.  

They transition from potentiality to actuality only when they separate from the body, for an angel cannot reside in matter.  

Thus, through such premises, the wise understand that:  
- The death of the body is the birth of the soul,  
- The birth of the soul is the death of the body,  
- The life of the body is the death of the soul.  

*And God knows best and is most wise.*
