### Summary of the Sixteenth Epistle on Natural Philosophy Concerning the Nature of Pains and Pleasures  
In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful  

Many among the wise harbor an aversion to the death of their bodies, despite knowing that eternal comfort and everlasting life lie elsewhere, and that there is no true benefit in remaining bound to the animal state. For the wise, this aversion stems from the weakness of the soul. The state of such a rational being may be likened to that of a child whose father entrusts him to a teacher, instructing him: *"Over a period of time, you must teach this child all that befits a servant of the king—impart to him intellectual and bodily disciplines until he becomes a master."*  

Thus, the child, desiring only play and amusement and unwilling to endure the toil of learning, daily seeks to appease the teacher—secretly bringing sweets from home, or procuring beautiful lovers for the teacher, knowing his inclinations. When the child sees the teacher constantly indulging in these pleasures, he too becomes engrossed in eating, coupling, and play, growing accustomed to them. Time passes in this manner until, from excessive indulgence, the teacher falls ill and nears death. The child, though aware that his true refuge lies with his parents, fears his father’s reproach and thus wishes the teacher would not die, lest he be exposed to the shame of ignorance and paternal disgrace.  

In this analogy:  
- The teacher represents the five senses.  
- The child is the rational soul (*nafs natiqa*).  
- The parents symbolize universal intellect (*‘aql*) and soul (*nafs kulli*).  
- The sweets and pleasures signify worldly delights.  

The rational soul, which ought to ascend through the senses and common perception (its teacher) to the realm of intelligibles, performing good deeds and worship to prepare for the hereafter, instead does the opposite. Hence, it inevitably feels aversion to death.  

When the rational soul becomes habituated to lust and fails to purify itself, even if it possesses the strength to ascend to the higher world after leaving the body, it will shrink in shame from facing its true parents—the universal soul and intellect.  

We thus assert that everything in the universe, from the center of the earth to the celestial spheres, experiences pleasure and pain according to its nature. Since pleasure and pain are concepts conveyed through language, we begin with animals, stating that the being in whom pain and pleasure are most evident is man—the noblest of animals, endowed with the subtlest perception. Man is composed of soul and body, and his soul experiences pleasures, while their opposites are pains.  

The root pleasures are of four kinds:  
1. **Carnal** – Natural pleasure derived from agreeable foods.  
2. **Animal** – Sensory pleasure of two types:  
   - Copulation when desired.  
   - Wrath, whether justified or not, in seeking retribution.  
3. **Human** – Intellectual pleasure attained through grasping intelligibles, which is the essence of true knowledge.  
4. **Spiritual/Angelic** – Pleasure the soul attains after parting from the body.  

Lust and wrath are shared by humans and animals, while sensory and intellectual pleasures are shared by humans and angels. The spiritual is exclusive to souls that have separated from the body.  

As for plants, scripture speaks of their "pleasure" in verses such as *"And they are in awe of Him"* (Quran 21:28), though they feel no pain. Non-rational animals, however, experience both pain and pleasure—all bodily. Man experiences both bodily and spiritual pleasures.  

Human pleasures are of two kinds:  
1. **Mediated through the body** (seven types):  
   - Visual (beauty of colors, forms, and natural patterns).  
   - Auditory (melodious sounds, song, praise).  
   - Gustatory (agreeable foods).  
   - Tactile (softness, roughness).  
   - Olfactory (fragrances pleasing to temperament).  
   - Sexual (union with the desired).  
   - Vengeful (retribution against enemies).  

These pleasures reach the soul through the body and are twofold:  
   - Immediate upon attainment.  
   - In recollection (e.g., remembering a beautiful face).  
   - This duality is the present reality of Paradise and Hell for them—fulfillment being Paradise, deprivation Hell.  

True pleasure for the soul lies in ascending to the celestial spheres—eternal Paradise, the ultimate fulfillment. Hell is remaining bound to the earthly realm—the ultimate deprivation. Hence, sages condemn wrath and harming others more than lust, for the latter’s discourse involves unspeakable matters.  

2. **Spiritual pleasures** (exclusive to the soul, four types):  
   - Grasping intelligibles (profound joy, sometimes found in sensory things, like a geometer marveling at a shape).  
   - Attaining sound belief, preparing for the hereafter.  
   - Seeing in oneself a praiseworthy character approved by intellect.  
   - Being praised for noble knowledge, deeds, or traits (e.g., a prophet for prophecy, a poet for eloquence).  

Pleasure and pain in the universe are twofold:  
- **Bodily**: Food, drink, clothing, scent (though some pleasures are superficial, tied to blood or impurity), and sound (transient, leaving aversion upon cessation). Visual pleasure is worse—love if present, longing if absent.  
- **Soul’s pleasure**:  
  - Worldly (arts like medicine, geometry, astronomy, virtues like generosity, courage).  
  - Otherworldly (ascending to heavens, witnessing the Necessary Being, eternal sovereignty like the angels).  

Pain of body and soul is the opposite of the above.  

And God knows best what is correct.
