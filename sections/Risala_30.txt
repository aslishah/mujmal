### **Summary of the Seventeenth Epistle on Natural Sciences: Concerning the Differences in Languages**  
*In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful*  

Know that this epistle presents a discourse on the differences in languages, and in this treatise, there is no discipline that might burden the mind. Thus, we have conveyed only that which is closer to sensory perception and natural disposition.  

First, we say that from the Universal Soul (*al-Nafs al-Kulliyya*), two powers extend to the entire world: one is called the *knowing power* (*al-ʿĀlima*), and the other the *active power* (*al-ʿĀmila*). The connection of these two powers is akin to the connection of sunlight to the sphere of air that lies within the celestial sphere.  

The knowing power is oriented toward the world of the celestial dominion (*al-Malakūt*), deriving knowledge from the Universal Soul and the celestial soul (*al-Nafs al-Malakiyya*), then delivering it to the active power. Thus, that which is intelligible (*maʿqūl*) and becomes known to people arises through the knowing power. The active power, meanwhile, is oriented toward the body. These two powers are what scripture refers to as the *Honorable Scribes* (*Kirām al-Kātibīn*).  

When these two powers are linked to a person, knowledge and action manifest within them. Each of these powers has its instruments, without which their functions cannot be carried out. These instruments are specific to the human soul, and it is for this reason that when we define the soul, we say: *The soul is the first perfection of a natural body*, meaning that this body is its specific instrument.  

When knowledge and action begin—since for potentiality (*quwwa*) to transition into actuality (*fiʿl*), it must have a specific instrument—the tongue serves as an interpreter in expressing certain kinds of sciences. For whatever the intellect (*ʿaql*) comprehends through demonstration (*burhān*), the tongue can articulate. Were it not so, there would be no doubt in the world, for the intellect never errs or lies. However, through this tongue, this language, and these letters, more can be explained than what the intellect alone knows. Thus, this interpretation is called *language* (*lugha*), and there is no doubt that everything in existence—to which the name "being" applies—derives from the Necessary Existence (*al-Wājib al-Wujūd*), for He is the cause of all.  

Yet, the order of creation must be preserved, for the act of God—Exalted is He—is distinct from the act of His creation. Accordingly:  
- The cause of the Intellect (*al-ʿAql*) is the Necessary Existence,  
- The cause of the Soul (*al-Nafs*) is the Intellect,  
- The cause of Prime Matter (*al-Hayūlā*) is the Soul,  
- The cause of the celestial spheres (*al-Aflāk*) is Prime Matter,  
- The cause of Nature (*al-Ṭabīʿa*) is the Soul,  
- The cause of the celestial elements (*al-Asṭukhsāt*) is the spheres,  
- The cause of the terrestrial elements (*al-Arākīn*) is the spheres,  
- The cause of the Earth (*al-Arḍ*) is the elements,  
- The cause of the planets (*al-Kawākib*) is the Sun,  
- The cause of generated beings (*al-Mutawallidāt*) is the elements.  

Were we to enumerate all the species and genera, the discourse would lengthen. Thus, from the center of the Earth to the outermost planets, all are effects of the Intellect. The planets are living, volitional, and articulate (*ḥayy, murīd, nāṭiq*), though their speech is not like that of humans. Human speech arises from the cavities of the head, the larynx, the tongue, breath, and letters, whereas their speech has no such cause—for we have established in the science of astronomy (*ʿilm al-hayʾa*) that there are no cavities within the celestial sphere. Thus, their speech is angelic (*malakī*), not corporeal (*jismī*).  

We have said that everything on Earth is a manifestation (*namūdhaj*) of the higher world. Thus, speech and language are manifestations, as are the climes and cities, each of which must correspond to a planet, a sphere, or a zodiacal sign. Though some skeptics may claim that astrological judgments are invalid, we possess demonstrative proofs in the universal principles of astrology and astronomy—as seen in the minor, intermediate, major, and supreme cycles concerning the revolutions of states and nations, the emergence of prophets and kings. If we neglect particular cases to avoid disputation with the ignorant, the universal truth remains luminous as the sun. And since this is so, the particular is also validated by this proof, for there is no universal that does not encompass particulars.  

Thus, the differences in languages arise from the planets. Some divine sages hold that certain sciences and languages descended through celestial revelation (*waḥy*), as it is universally acknowledged that the science of medicine descended to Asclepius (*Asqulābiyūs*) and the science of astronomy to Idris (*Idrīs*). Every language must have its cause in the planets, and the reasons for this are numerous, for the connections of the planets are manifold.  

If the governing planet of a language is Saturn (*Zuhal*), particularly in the first clime, there will be utmost equilibrium, for Saturn is greatly auspicious there. In regions where languages are heavy, the letters conform to the gravity of the language. If Jupiter (*al-Mushtarī*) shares influence, though the language may still be weighty, it will not be so to the same degree. Some attribute the differences in languages to natural dispositions—such as balanced, cold, moist, or dry temperaments—but all of these, too, stem from celestial influence.  

*And God knows best.*
