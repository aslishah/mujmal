### Summary of the Sixteenth Epistle on Natural Sciences Concerning the Diversity of Languages  
In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.  
Know that this epistle concerns the diversity of languages, and in this epistle, there is no science that might weary the mind. Thus, we have presented only that which is closer to sensory satisfaction and nature.  

First, we say that from the Universal Soul, two powers extend to the entire world. One is called the "knowing" power, and the other the "acting" power. The connection of these two powers is like the connection of sunlight to the sphere of air that lies within the celestial sphere. The knowing power is oriented toward the world of the divine realm (*malakūt*), deriving knowledge from the Universal Soul and the celestial soul (*nafs-i malakī*), then delivering it to the acting power. Thus, that which is intelligible and known to people arises through the knowing power. The acting power, meanwhile, is oriented toward the body. These two powers are what scripture refers to as the "noble scribes" (*kirām al-kātibīn*).  

When these two powers are joined in a person, knowledge and action manifest within them. Each of these powers has its own instruments, without which their functions cannot be performed.  

-241-  

These instruments belong exclusively to the human soul, and it is for this reason that when we define the soul, we say: *The soul is the first perfection of the natural body*, meaning that this body is its specific instrument. Thus, when knowledge and action begin—so that potentiality may become actuality—it must have a specific instrument. The tongue serves as an interpreter in the expression of certain sciences, for whatever the intellect comprehends through demonstration, the tongue can articulate. Were it not so, there would be no doubt in the world, since the intellect never errs or speaks falsehood. However, through this tongue, this language, and these letters, more can be explained than the intellect alone can grasp. Thus, this interpretation is called "language," and there is no doubt that everything in the world that bears the name of existence derives from the Necessary Being (*wājib al-wujūd*), for It is the cause of all things.  

Yet, the order of creation must be maintained, for the act of the Almighty is distinct, and the act of His act is likewise distinct. Thus, by this principle:  
- The cause of the Intellect is the Necessary Being,  
- The cause of the Soul is the Intellect,  
- The cause of Prime Matter (*hayūlā*) is the Soul,  
- The cause of the celestial spheres is the Soul,  
- The cause of Nature is the Soul,  
- The cause of the celestial elements is the spheres,  
- The cause of the terrestrial elements is the celestial spheres,  
- The cause of the planets is the Sun,  
- The cause of generated beings is the elements.  

Were we to enumerate all the species and genera, it would become lengthy. Thus, from the center of the earth to the outermost sphere of the planets, all are effects of the Intellect.  

The planets are living, volitional, and articulate beings—though their speech is not like human speech. For human speech arises from the hollow of the head, the larynx, the tongue, breath, and letters, whereas their speech has no such cause. As we have established in the science of astronomy (*‘ilm al-hay’a*), there is no hollow within the celestial sphere. Thus, their speech is angelic (*malakī*), not corporeal.  

We have said that everything on earth is a representation of the higher world (*‘ālam-i ‘ulwī*). Thus, speech and language are representations, as are the climes and cities, each of which must correspond to a planet, a sphere, or a zodiacal sign.  

Though some skeptics may claim that astrological judgments are unfounded, we possess demonstrative proofs in the general principles of astronomy and astrology—just as in the minor, intermediate, major, and supreme cycles concerning the revolutions of states and nations, the emergence of prophets and kings. Thus, if we neglect particulars to avoid disputation with the ignorant, the universal truth remains as clear as the sun. And since this is so, the particulars are also validated by this proof, for there is no universal without particulars beneath it.  

Thus, the diversity of languages stems from the planets. Some divine sages hold that certain sciences and languages descended through celestial revelation (*waḥy*), as it is universally acknowledged that the science of medicine descended to Asclepius and the science of astronomy to Idris.  

Every language must have its cause in the planets, and the reasons for this are numerous, for the connections of the planets are manifold. Thus, if Saturn governs a language—especially in the first clime, where moderation is utmost, since Saturn is greatly auspicious there—then in places where languages are weighty, their letters will suit that language. If Jupiter shares influence, though the language remains weighty, it will not be so to the same degree.  

Some attribute the differences in languages to nature—such as balanced, cold, moist, or dry temperaments—yet all of these are also effects of the celestial spheres.  

And God knows best.
