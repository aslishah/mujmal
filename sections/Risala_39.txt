### | خلاصۀ رسالۀ هشتم از نفسانيّات در كميت اجناس حركات
بسم اللّه الرحمن الرحيم 
بداند كه حكما متفق‌اند كه حركت جان راست نه تن را، و اگر در تن حركتى پديد آيد از جان باشد نه از تن، و حركت تن را عاريتى بود و جان را ذاتى باشد نه عرضى، و هرچه ذاتى چيز بود تا بقاى آن چيز بود آن صفت با وى بود. مثلا حيوان آن بود كه در حدّ وى گويند حيوان نامى حسّاس است. پس حسّاسى ذاتى حيوان است و مادام تا حيوان بود حسّ‌ با وى بود، و همچنين خاصّه كه تا آن چيز بود هرگز از آن چيز برنگردد و زايل نشود. چون به دو پاى رفتن مرغ را و مردم را، و خنده و گريه مردم را، و صهيل و شهيق اسب و خر را. پس [حركت] ذاتى جان باشد و تن را به عرض بود. از آنكه بعد از مفارقت جان آن از وى زايل مى‌شود و در حال بى‌حركت بماند. پس حركت جان يك حركت است، ولكن به محل مختلف شود. چنانكه نفس و بدن عالم كه جمله عالم يك نفس و يك بدن است.

 و اين جمله حركتها كه در عالم علوى و سفلى مى‌باشد از يك حيات و يك حيوان است، و گاهى به جملۀ او محيط‍‌ باشد، و گاهى در كره‌هاى فلكى باشد، و گاهى در اجسام جملۀ زمين. و همۀ حركتها از آن حركت [است] و امثال اين در محسوسات بسيارست. مثلا شخصى به پاى بايستد ، يا به‌دست چرخ آسيا بگرداند. آن گردانيدن يك حركت است و حركتهاى بسيار در وى بسته باشد: يكى حركت [چرخ] آسيا، دؤم حركت ستون آسيا، سوم حركت سنگ آسيا، چهارم حركت گندم و اين هر يكى به چهار قسم شود و هر قسمى ديگر بار به قسمها شود، پنجم حركت آرد، ششم حركت مرد. و اين جمله عرضى است و اصل همه حركت حيات نفس با آن مرد يا آن شخص است كه سبب اين جمله شده است. پس حركت حيات شخص ذاتى بود و اين جمله عرضى بود و بسيارگاه بود كه آسيا را چيزى ديگر گرداند چون باد و چون آب، و اين جمله عرضى باشد، و سبب ايشان چيزهاى دور باشد. پس حركت همه چنين است، آنچه در عالم سفلى است. اما آنچه در عالم علوى است به سبب آنكه مادّت وى از صورت وى جدا نخواهد شد حركت او ذاتى او شده است، از آنكه سبب جدا شدن صورت از مادّت آن است كه مادّت متغير شود و كهنه گردد و طبيعت او را فرسوده كند و در افلاك و كواكب [تغيّر] طبع و فرسودگى نيست و تغيّر و فساد نيست و آنچه او حركت كند آن نه اين تغيّر است كه در كون و فساد است.بلى تغيّر آن بود كه گرد خويشتن مى‌گردد و كواكب گاهى درين برج باشند و گاهى جايى ديگر، و اين جمله به برهان دانسته شد كه نه تغيّر باشد. و اين شرح اينجا راست نيايد از بسيارى. پس هر حركتى كه در عالم سفلى است به عرض است، و سبب اين حركتها نفس كل عالم علوى است.

### Summary of the Eighth Epistle on Psychic Matters Concerning the Quantity and Types of Movements  
In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.  

Know that the sages unanimously agree that movement belongs to the soul, not the body. If movement appears in the body, it is from the soul, not the body itself. The movement of the body is borrowed, whereas for the soul, it is essential, not accidental. Whatever is essential to a thing remains with it as long as that thing endures. For example, an animal is defined as a sentient, living being. Thus, sentience is intrinsic to the animal, and as long as the animal exists, sensation remains with it. Similarly, certain attributes never depart from a thing as long as that thing exists and do not perish—such as the bipedal motion of birds and humans, the laughter and tears of humans, or the neighing and braying of horses and donkeys. Therefore, movement is intrinsic to the soul, while for the body, it is accidental, since upon the soul’s departure, movement ceases, and the body becomes motionless.  

Thus, the movement of the soul is one, though it manifests in different substrates. Just as the soul and body of the universe are one—for the entire cosmos is a single soul and a single body—so too are all the movements in the higher and lower worlds derived from one life and one living being. At times, it encompasses the whole, at others, it resides in the celestial spheres, and yet other times, it is in the earthly bodies. All movements stem from that [single] movement, and many examples of this are found in the perceptible world. For instance, when a person stands on their feet or turns a millstone with their hand, that turning is one movement, yet many movements are contained within it: the movement of the millstone, the movement of the mill’s axle, the movement of the grinding stone, the movement of the wheat—each of which can be further divided into four types, and each type into further subdivisions—then the movement of the flour, and finally, the movement of the man. All these are accidental, while the root of them all is the movement of the living soul of that man or person who is the cause of all this. Thus, the movement of the person’s life is essential, while these [other movements] are accidental.  

Often, the mill may be turned by something else, such as wind or water, and these too are accidental, their causes lying in distant things. Thus, all movement in the lower world is of this nature. As for the higher world, because its matter is inseparable from its form, its movement is essential to it. The reason form separates from matter is that matter undergoes change, decays, and its nature wears out. But in the celestial spheres and stars, there is no change of nature, no decay, no alteration or corruption. Their movement is not the kind of change associated with generation and corruption. Rather, their change consists in revolving around themselves, with the stars sometimes in one constellation and at other times in another. It has been proven by demonstration that this is not true change. To elaborate further here would be excessive.  

Thus, every movement in the lower world is accidental, and the cause of these movements is the universal soul of the higher world.

What we intend to discuss in this epistle concerning movement and its primary cause is better addressed in the context of cause and effect, as we have done in other epistles—such as those on generation and corruption, the heavens and the cosmos, quiddity and nature, the introduction to astronomy, and cycles and epochs. The movement that arises from another movement has the first as the cause of the subsequent movement, and the second movement is caused by the first. The cause is the "agent," and the effect is the "acted upon." Thus, in this epistle, we shall discuss other matters, for there is much to say on this subject. And God knows best.
