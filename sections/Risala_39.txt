### **Summary of the Ninth Epistle on Psychical Matters Concerning Causes and Effects**  
*In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful*  

Know that the endeavor of all seekers of knowledge who are devoted to the recognition of God is the understanding of realities and the true nature of things as they are—distinguishing between genera, species, and the specific properties of each kind. Questions and answers concerning them follow this framework, which consists of nine inquiries: *Is it? What is it? How much is it? Which is it? How is it? Where is it? When is it? Why is it?* and *From whom is it?* Each of these questions has a distinct answer, none of which resemble one another, and whenever something among existent beings presents difficulty, these nine questions are applied to it.  

If one answers regarding the reality [of a thing] and speaks of its essential differentia without fallacious reasoning, then what is known is truth and proof. But if it is not an essential differentia, it is like a mere report—either false or merely persuasive, which is called "rhetorical."  

We first ask: *Does the world exist or not?* By "world," we mean everything other than the Necessary Existent (God). It may be called the "world" collectively, or each part may be called a "world" individually—such as the world of intellect, the world of soul, the world of the celestial realm and spirits, the world of the spheres, the higher world, the lower world, the world of generation and corruption, the world of plants, the world of animals, and the world of man. Likewise, a genus along with its species may metaphorically be called a "world." Hence, when some speak of "eighteen thousand worlds," it is permissible—even to speak of "the world of ants" or "the world of particles," for we observe with our own eyes that the world exists.  

If one speaks of the world of spirits, we know without doubt that a body, insofar as it is a body, does not move on its own, plants do not grow, and the celestial spheres do not rotate by themselves without a soul as their mover. If the animal’s body were not aided by the soul, it would never move. The proof of this is the death of an animal, for death is nothing but the separation of the soul from the body. Thus, by this proof, it is established that both the world of spirits and the world of bodies exist—sometimes separate from one another and sometimes connected. There are also things whose soul and body are never separated, such as the higher world, the celestial spheres, and the stars.  

Thus, the answer to *"Is it?"* has been given by proof.  

As for *"What is it?"*, the answer is that, in summary, it is a single soul connected to a body, governing it through wisdom and providence—as is evident to the eye, such as the rotation of the sphere, the heat of the sun, and the actions of the stars, as mentioned in the *Epistle on "Man as a Microcosm."* Thus, the answer to *"What is it?"* is established.  

The condition for answering *"What is it?"* is that the answer must be given from the nearest genus. Since the world has already been mentioned collectively, there is no other genus for it to belong to, so the answer is correct.  

As for *"How much is it?"*—meaning, *How many things is the world?*—we say it is a soul and a body. The details of this soul have been discussed in the *Epistle on Generation and Corruption*, the *Epistle on the Heavens and the World*, and others.  

As for *"Which is it?"*—meaning, *What thing is it?*—it is known through observation, perception, and reason that it extends from beyond the outermost sphere to the center of the earth, comprising in detail the soul, intellect, spheres, stars, elements, minerals, plants, and animals. All this answers *"How much?"*, while *"It"* answers *"Which is it?"*  

As for *"How is it?"*—meaning, *In what state is it?*—it is according to the nature attached to it, including color, smell, and taste, which are three genera of quality. These are based on simple and compound natures, such as heat and cold, dryness and moisture, heat and dryness, cold and moisture, heat and moisture, cold and dryness, and the balanced state. There is also the fifth nature—that which belongs to the spheres and stars.  

As for *"Where is it?"*—it stands within the Universal Soul, like a child in its mother’s womb.  

As for *"When is it?"*—meaning, *How long will it last?*—it will last forever, because the agent of this action and the intender of this intention is not a nature that decays. Every agent acts either by command, by nature, or by mercy. Necessarily, the world’s action has an agent, for it is not the Necessary Existent. We have said that the action of the Necessary Existent is pure mercy, and His action is compassion upon contingent beings. Thus, if the Universal Soul acts by the command of the Necessary Existent, it will never cease, for the cessation of mercy and compassion from the Necessary Existent is impossible—since compassion comes from the Necessary Existent, and the Necessary Existent is never without generosity.  

If we say it acts by nature, we have already stated that there is no nature in the soul, the spheres, or the stars—so it must be mercy. The mercy of the soul comes from the intellect, the mercy of the intellect upon the soul comes from the Necessary Existent, and the mercy of the Necessary Existent is from the Necessary Existent and will never cease. Thus, it will endure forever.  

As for *"Why is it?"*—meaning, *For what purpose?*—it is so that the effect of the Wise One’s wisdom may appear. This meaning requires no further proof.  

As for *"From whom is it?"*—meaning, *For whose sake?*—we shall return to the discussion of causes to avoid repetition.  

Similarly, every statement and question must be asked with a glimpse of the reality of the thing. As the sages have said: *"Nature does nothing in vain."* That is, nature does no act that is corruption. If something appears to us as corruption, it is due to our ignorance—otherwise, it is for some greater good. Thus, the truth is that there is no corruption in the world. What is called "corruption" pertains to accidents, not to substance or body. No corruption is more apparent than the killing of a human body, yet if seen truthfully, it is not corruption but the enlivening of the soul—and the enlivening of the soul is pure good. This matter is best discussed in its proper place.  

Now, concerning causes: Whatever has existence in itself and gives existence to another is called the "cause," and the thing that receives existence is called the "effect." Among existents, some have one cause (such as intellect and soul), some have two causes (such as the spheres and stars), and some have four causes (such as minerals and plants).  

The first cause of all existents is the wisdom, mercy, and compassion of the Necessary Existent. The Active Intellect is His effect, the intellect is the cause of the soul, and the soul is its effect. The soul is the cause of prime matter, and prime matter is its effect. Prime matter is the cause of absolute body, and absolute body is its effect. Absolute body is the cause of the spheres and stars, and the spheres and stars are its effects. The spheres and stars are the causes of the elements, and the elements are their effects. The elements are the causes of plants, minerals, and animals, and plants, minerals, and animals are their effects.  

We have said that no analogy is as evident from the seen to the unseen as number. "One" is the first of all numbers because it is the cause of "two," and "two" is the cause of "three." This natural order must be followed. One cannot say that "one" is the cause of "three" without the mediation of "two," which is the middle term. Similarly, the human form in the womb appears through the mediation of other causes—not because the Necessary Existent inscribed the human form directly in menstrual blood without the mediation of time and place. Without time and place, the generated beings of the world would not exist.  

Each generated thing has four causes:  
1. **Material cause**  
2. **Formal cause**  
3. **Efficient cause**  
4. **Final cause**  

For example, if there were no builder, there would be no house; if there were no earth (the material), there would be no house; if there were no form of the house, it would not be called a house; and if it were not for the purpose of dwelling, there would be no house. For Zayd builds the house to sit in it, whether for himself or for another. Thus, when Zayd finishes the house, he is the cause of the house because he assembled wood, brick, and stone in a specific form—but Zayd is not the cause of the house’s existence, only the cause of its construction.  

Now, if a passerby lodges in that house, or a caravan, or some animals, and the house collapses due to a cause such as rain, an earthquake, or the wear of time, killing those inside—Zayd is not the cause of their death. The cause of their death was the weakness of the wall and the rain, not Zayd. Thus, the collapse of the house, its decay, and the rain are from nature, not from Zayd’s action.  

Similarly, one who builds a ship does so for the comfort of people—this much suffices, even if he himself has no need for it.  

*And God knows best what is correct.*
