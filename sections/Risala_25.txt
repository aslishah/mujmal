بسم اللّه الرحمن الرحيم 
چند جاى گفتيم كه آنچه تحت قمر آيد چهار جنس‌اند: عناصر و معادن و نبات و حيوان. اما عناصر از جملۀ بسايط‍‌ است و سخن درين رساله در مركبات است و آن سه جنس است: معادن و نبات و حيوان، و اين اجناس انواع شوند به سبب خاصيتى كه دريشان است به «فصل ذاتى» و خاصۀ فصل ذاتى چون كوتاهى، و «خاصه» چون خنده مردم را. و گفتيم كه «نفس كلى» از وراى جملۀ كاينات پيوسته است و محيط‍‌ بر همه و مدبّر همه. پس چون عناصر به سبب حركت فلك و كواكب حركت كنند لابد بود كه در زمين از آن تولدات پديد آيد به حسب حركت آن. و در حال كه پديد آيد از «نفس كلى» قوّتى بدو پيوندد و آن را «نفس جزوى» خوانند. و اين حال كه پديد آيد يا در معادن بود يا در نبات يا در حيوان. و گفتيم كه بودن معدنيها چگونه است. اما آن وقت كه «نفس جزوى» به وى پيوندد آنگه بود كه مزاج زيبق به كبريت باشد و تا آن وقت كه آن آب در آن غار يا در آن كان قرار گرفته بود، چنانكه آب در كوزه به وقت فروريختن. و نبات يا به

### Summary of the Tenth Epistle on Natural Sciences Concerning the Casting of the Seed  

In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.  

We have previously mentioned that what lies beneath the sphere of the moon consists of four genera: the elements, minerals, plants, and animals. However, the elements are among the simple substances, and the discussion in this epistle concerns the compounds, which are three genera: minerals, plants, and animals. These genera are further divided into species due to the specific properties within them—through an "essential differentia" (such as shortness) and a "property" (such as human laughter).  

We have stated that the "Universal Soul" is perpetually connected beyond all creation, encompassing and governing everything. Thus, when the elements move due to the motion of the celestial sphere and the stars, it is inevitable that various generations arise on Earth according to their motion. At the moment of their emergence, a power from the Universal Soul attaches to them, which is called the "Partial Soul." This emergence occurs either in minerals, plants, or animals.  

We have explained the nature of mineral existence. However, when the Partial Soul attaches to it, it is at the moment when the temperament of mercury combines with sulfur, and until that time, the water remains settled in its cave or mine, just as water remains in a vessel before being poured out.  

Plants either grow from seeds or spontaneously, and animals either through procreation or spontaneous generation. For all of these, there is a specific time when a particular degree of the celestial spheres necessarily rises, and the ascendant of that moment determines their fate—such as the casting of seed into the earth, the laying of bricks in construction, the cutting of a tree, the falling of semen into the mother’s womb, and similar events.  

Thus, the occurrence of this state, the attachment of the Partial Soul from the Universal Soul, and the degree of the ascendant are all one, regardless of location. However, plants and minerals, like all animals, share this attribute with humans, though humans are distinguished by their balanced temperament and the actualization of their latent potential—qualities other animals lack due to partial causes that cannot be fully discerned.  

At the moment when human semen enters the womb from the penis, there is a precise instant of the celestial ascendant, and the configuration of the stars and zodiac at that time determines its meaning. The fate of the person born from that seed will unfold according to that ascendant. Similarly, all animals share this equality with humans—such as when a stallion mates and a foal is born, and at another time, another mating produces a different offspring, one unfortunate and the other fortunate, their misfortune and fortune being well known.  

Yet, throughout a person’s life, the moment of the casting of the seed contains a trace of fortune and misfortune, good and evil. For instance, when a person intends to unite with their mate, heat arises within them, their blood warms and boils until it produces froth and gathers. The more abundant and complete this froth, the greater their pleasure until the moment it spurts from the tip of the penis and enters the woman’s womb.  

At that very moment, a power from the vegetative soul (which derives from the Universal Soul) attaches to it, and at that instant, a precise degree of the celestial ascendant exists. The celestial sphere, through its own essence, assumes a particular form and configuration. A power from the sphere and powers from the stars also attach to that seed.  

When this seed settles, menstrual blood surrounds it and imbues it with its color. The gathering of blood around the seed is like the gathering of egg white around the yolk. Then, the heat of the seed, the blood, and the womb interact in the process of concoction, as is the habit of nature, until the froth coagulates and solidifies due to the blood and the heat of the seed.  

From the moment it settles until the passage of one month over this clot, Saturn holds dominion. Most scholars hold that this period is one month, and they begin with Saturn because his sphere is the highest among the celestial spheres, and his soul is connected to the higher world. It is the abode of the near angels, the source of revelation, the place of all blessings, the vast throne, the mine of the bearers of the Throne, the cause of the prophethood of the prophets, and the wisdom of the philosophers. Thus, it is most fitting to begin with it.  

During this month, the seed changes from its initial state and takes on the color of blood, yet no movement appears within it, for although Saturn is the cause of binding power, he is not the cause of life. Life arises from heat and moisture, whereas Saturn is cold and dry—the cause of death. Thus, the seed remains motionless during this period, and though it takes on the color of blood, it does not mix with the blood and remains still due to the dominance of Saturn’s coldness.  

When the second month arrives, Jupiter assumes governance, his sphere lying beneath Saturn’s. In this month, his influence prevails. From Jupiter’s spiritual power, a power arises within this clot, and a moderate heat appears within it. The two mix together, and a movement emerges—like a trembling or shuddering. During this period, the pregnant woman becomes aware of the child’s existence, and as long as Jupiter’s governance lasts, this trembling continues until another month passes.  

In the third month, Mars takes over governance. The heat increases beyond what it was, and the trembling intensifies due to the dominance of Mars’ spiritual forces. The entire mass becomes a piece of red flesh, and the heat becomes firmly established until another month passes.  

In the fourth month, the Sun assumes governance and holds dominion. The spirit appears within it and moves by its own essence, knowing that the Sun is the soul of the lower world, the heart of the upper world, the chief of the stars, and the cause of the existence of plants, animals, and minerals. The Sun itself is spirit and has no body—though one might say it has a body in the sense that what is called "body" refers to form, and form is sustained by the soul.  

Though the Sun’s power derives from celestial substances, it is alive by itself, not by anything else, for it is absolute spirit. Thus, when the Sun governs, its spiritual forces attach to the fetus, and the animal soul appears within it. This occurs for all animals under the Sun’s governance.  

However, humans are unique. From the moment the seed enters the woman’s womb, the Sun must necessarily be in a particular position. By the time governance falls to the Sun, three zodiac signs have already passed, and in this one month of its governance, it does not contend with another sign. In total, four signs have passed, and these four signs correspond to the four temperaments. The Sun’s heat is the cause of all things, and the signs it has passed through are one fiery, one earthy, one airy, and one watery—thus completing the four temperaments.  

When spiritual forces attach to the fetus, the natural soul enters it, and its formation is completed. At this stage, it is said to possess two senses.  

When this order passes, the turn of Venus arrives—the lesser benefic, the lord of forms and images. At this time, the fetus is fully shaped and given complete features: eyes, ears, mouth, nose, the organs of touch, the two excretory passages, and joints. In this state, the fetus lies with both knees drawn to the chest, the head resting on the knees, both hands open on the chest, and the arms spread to the sides. Yet, it has no sensation to perceive this discomfort, and its navel remains connected to the mother’s navel, drawing nourishment from her until the day of birth.  

If the child is male, its face is toward the mother’s back; if female, its face is toward the mother’s belly.  

When governance passes to Mercury, its spiritual forces attach to the fetus, causing it to stretch its limbs in the womb, sometimes moving and sometimes resting. It opens its eyes, moves its tongue in its mouth, breathes, sometimes sleeps, and sometimes awakens.  

By the time the Moon’s turn arrives in the seventh month, its spiritual forces attach to the fetus. In this month, the fetus grows plump, its back hardens, and it ceases movement, becoming aware of the confinement of its space and intending to emerge—if the configuration of the stars at the time of the casting of the seed indicated that it would come forth in this period and if the stars were all favorable.  

In this state, the Sun is in the seventh sign. If the child does not emerge in this month, governance returns to Saturn in the eighth month, and the Sun is in the eighth sign—the house of death for the casting of the seed. In this month, the child becomes heavy, ceases movement, and cold dominates it. If born in this month, it will die; if not, its life will be short. It is also common for it to be stillborn.  

If it does not emerge in this month, governance passes to Jupiter in the ninth month—the greater benefic. The Sun is then in the ninth sign, the house of travel and transition, and in trine with the ascendant of the casting of the seed. Two felicities coincide: one from Jupiter’s beneficence and the other from the Sun’s aspect. Thus, the child’s temperament becomes balanced, for the Sun has doubly bestowed upon it the fourfold power.  

Two hundred and forty degrees of the celestial sphere have passed, leaving one hundred and twenty. The reason humans do not live beyond one hundred and twenty years is this—though this is an approximation, not a demonstration. Yet all sages call this the natural lifespan, and most people live half of it—sixty years. Few reach seventy, and the science of astrology elaborates extensively on this.  

We say that the stars have evident influences in the lower world, especially upon humans. However, their effects vary—sometimes due to differences in their celestial motions (ascension, declination, apogee, and perigee), sometimes due to their latitude (north or south), and sometimes due to their stations (direct, retrograde, etc.). There are many such distinctions, as mentioned in the *Almagest*, though it is not fitting to elaborate here.  

When governance first belongs to Saturn, and Saturn is well-placed and free from malefic aspects, the seed is free from all harm, and its coldness does not distress the mother. If Saturn is ascending in its sphere and epicycle, the seed is placed higher in the womb, safe from pains and ailments. If in Jupiter’s terms, the mother is at ease, unaware of the pregnancy, and free from cravings. If in Mars’ terms, the mother is energetic and unwilling to delegate tasks. If in Venus’ terms, she grows happier each day, especially with her spouse. If in Mercury’s terms, she is vigilant and shrewd, knowing when the seed reaches her womb and keeping daily accounts.  

But if Saturn is afflicted, descending, or retrograde, the opposite occurs.  

In the second month, governance passes to Jupiter. If ascending in its sphere and epicycle, direct and well-placed, the seed is better situated, and the mother is joyful and healthy. If in its own terms, she devotes herself to religion, prayer, and worship, receiving divine inspirations. Most prophets and philosophers were such—during this month, due to Jupiter’s greatness and its effect on the seed, angels spoke to them or appeared visibly. The same applies to the child.  

If Jupiter is in Saturn’s terms, the mother is contemplative, knowing untaught things, and the child bears signs and miracles. If in Mars’ terms (and Mars is unafflicted), these signs manifest with strength and dominance. If in Venus’ terms, the child calls people to religion, offering good counsel. If in Mercury’s terms, the child is argumentative, stubborn, a debater who recognizes truth but is unheeded.  

If Jupiter is favorably received by its house lord or term lord, all these traits are amplified. If not, its words are ignored, and its actions are mostly deceitful. If descending or retrograde, the child is dull-witted, learning only by rote, like a beast—knowing only eating and sleeping, though managing its livelihood well.  

In the third month, governance passes to Mars. If ascending in both spheres, direct and well-placed, the child is bold and quick-handed, excelling in its deeds. If in its own terms, the child is combative, proud of its strength and victories. If in Saturn’s terms, the initial temperaments mix—what we described occurs, but with patience, cunning, and hidden anger. Such a child turns away from no one.  

If Mars is in Jupiter’s terms, its actions blend with Jupiter’s—acting justly, avenging fairly, and forgiving when able. If in Venus’ terms, the child is lustful, jealous of its family, and proud of its zeal. If in Mercury’s terms, it is shrewder than all, vengeful, fickle, and achieves its ends through cunning.  

If Mars is descending, retrograde, or afflicted, the child is cowardly, lowly, fearful, and content with humiliation.  

When governance passes to the Sun in the fourth month, if ascending and free from affliction, the child is destined for kingship and leadership, with grand ambitions, skilled governance, and association with great rulers. If in its exaltation or own house, these traits are doubled.  

If in Saturn’s terms, the child is magnanimous, ambitious, conceiving great plans and achieving most. If in Jupiter’s terms, it reaches the utmost—either kingship or prophethood (or both), attaining all virtues effortlessly, with a royal nature and divine knowledge. If born under a conjunction or with two conjunctions in governance, it is a sent prophet—though the nature of its miracles, mission, and laws must be seen from the seventh and ninth houses and the ascendant of the conjunction (as detailed in books on conjunctions).  

If the Sun is in Mars’ terms, the child is brave and mighty, commanding vast armies feared by kings. If in Venus’ terms, it is noble but preoccupied with women, music, and pleasure. If in the Sun’s own terms, the opposite occurs—though not severely blameworthy. If descending or afflicted, all is reversed.  

In the fifth month, governance passes to Venus. If ascending and direct, the child is beautiful, pleasurable, and lustful, amassing wealth. If in its own house, exaltation, or terms, it is fair, curly-haired, handsome, sweet-lipped, plump-faced and -legged, cheerful, and good-natured.  

If in Saturn’s terms, it is wheat-complexioned, sweet-lipped, large-eyed, curly-haired, with overlapping teeth and a commanding presence—loving deeply, trustworthy, patient, and never treacherous.  

If in Jupiter’s terms, it is balanced, sweet-statured, handsome, large-eyed, with small pupils, thick hair, a curly beard, and a cheerful, truthful, and pure-hearted nature.  

If descending or afflicted, these traits diminish according to Venus’ debility.  

In the sixth month, governance passes to Mercury—Jupiter’s younger brother (as Venus is Mars’ sister, the Moon Saturn’s sister, and the Sun their father). If ascending, direct, and well-placed in its own terms and sign, the child is lively, sharp-minded, and intelligent.  

If in Saturn’s terms, it is narrow-minded, meddlesome, deeply thoughtful but slow-spoken. If in Jupiter’s terms, it is devout, pious, enjoining good and forbidding evil, always speaking of the hereafter and truth.  

If in Mars’ terms, it is argumentative, quarrelsome, harmful, fond of debate and war, quick-witted, often erring and recanting—possibly a poet, orator, or judge. If in Venus’ terms, it praises worldly pleasures, sings, and excels in poetry and music.  

If afflicted, descending, retrograde, or combust, the child is mute, dull, or senseless.  

In the seventh month, governance passes to the Moon. If free from affliction or in its own house, exaltation, or triplicity, the child is adorned with all good qualities.  

If in Mercury’s terms, it is handsome, good-natured, and wise but fickle, easily bored, and sociable. If in Saturn’s terms, it is wise, steadfast, and finishes what it starts.  

If in Venus’ terms, a male child has a feminine appearance and nature inwardly, and vice versa for a female. If in Mars’ terms, it is virtuous. If in Jupiter’s terms, it is balanced in worldly and spiritual matters.  

If governance returns to Saturn and it is afflicted, the child’s state is bad; if born, it may die or live briefly. If well-placed and governance passes to Jupiter in the ninth month, the child is born fortunate.  

Had the foundation and form of a human been completed in one day, it would never remain in the womb for two. If completed in one month, it would not stay for two. In sum, what befalls the child was determined at the casting of the seed—as our Prophet (peace be upon him) said: *"The fortunate is he who was fortunate in his mother’s womb, and the wretched is he who was wretched in his mother’s womb."*  

Yet, taking the child’s birth ascendant is also a complete principle—called the *canon*—because determining the moment of the casting of the seed is difficult, so this canon is used for judgment.  

This much suffices. And God knows best.
