### | خلاصۀ رسالۀ ششم از نفسانيّات در عشق 
بسم اللّه الرحمن الرحيم 
اينجا رساله‌اى بياورده است در عشق و سخنى چند گفته مانند مجلس‌گرى و ما آن‌قدر كه دانستيم و به امور طبيعى نزديكتر بود ياد كرده‌ايم. بداند كه عشق مشكلتر حالى است نفس ناطقه را و حدّ او را آنجا كه حدود او ياد كنيم بگوئيم به شرح. و ابتداى عشق از مواليد مى‌افتد. اما نخست بايد كه بداند كه در مردم دو قوّت است: يكى شهوت و يكى غضب، گرم و خشك. و اگرچه هر قوّتى كه در انسان است اصل آن حرارت غريزى است و ليكن مايۀ شهوت سردى و ترى است و در وقت حركت او همه قوّتهاى بشرى با وى يار باشند، چون قوّتهائى كه در دل و جگر و دماغ‌اند. اما غضب هم شهوتى است، وليكن بدان روى كه شهوت جماع را شهوت خوانند او را شهوت نشايد خواند. از آنكه شهوت جماع لذتى است حيوانى و آن به مشاركت دو شخص باشد كه هردو را لذت رسد يا يكى را. و حدّ غضب نه چنين بود. از آنكه گويند غضب جوشش خون دل است و كين خواستن را، و اين حدّ نه آن حدّ است. و همۀ حكما متفق‌اند كه دو چيز را كه حدّ ايشان موافق نباشد به يكديگر نمايند.

 و نهاد شهوت مردم سبب تناسل است و غضب بر ضد اين است. از آنكه تناسل خير است و انتقام شرّ محض است. چون اين هردو قوّت ضدّاند لابد باشد كه به يكجا جمع نشوند و اگر اتفاق [افتد] كه جايى به يكديگر جمع شوند سبب آن كثرت شهوت جماع بوده باشد نه غضب، كه اگر انتقام بودى غضب زيادت بودى. و چون غضب زيادت از شهوت آيد حركت شهوت نيست كند و مثال غضب آتش است و مثال شهوت آب. اگر غلبت آب را بود آتش را بكشد و اگر غلبت آتش را بود آب به بخار برود و بنماند، و اگر در قوّت مفرد سخن گفته آيد معلوم است كه قوّت آتش بيشترست از قوّت آب. و بدين مثال كه ما گفتيم خداوند شهوت تواند بود كه قوّت شهوت را بكشد به قوّت غضب و قوّت غضب را تواند كه بكشد به قوّت شهوت. پس گوييم كه عشق از سه وجه خالى نباشد: يا مفرد تن را باشد، يا مفرد جان را، يا به مشاركت جان و تن بود. اما آنچه مفرد تن را باشد اول راندن مرادست بر وفق طبع با معشوق يا با غير معشوق، و يا خوردن طعامهاى لذيذ از هر آنچه دل به وى ميل كند، يا پوشيدن انواع لباس كه دل آن را خواهد، يا اندوختن سيم بود، و ذخيره نهادن، و اين جمله به تن تعلق دارد. و اما آنچه به جان تعلق دارد استنباط‍‌ علمها باشد، و پيشه از خويشتن بنهادن، و مبدأ خويشتن بدانستن، و معقولات را دريافتن، و جواهر ملكوت بشناختن، و معرفت واجب الوجود به‌دست آوردن، و در عقل و نفس رسيدن، و نجات خويشتن اندر علمهاى صالحه طلب كردن، و زاد آخرت و نيكى اندوختن. اما آنچه به مشاركت تن و جان است طلب روى نيكو كردن، و سماع شنيدن، و شراب خوردن، و بوى خوش و گرمابه به‌كار داشتن و نهادن علمها چون موسيقى و آلات آن، و علمهاى هندسى چون حروفها و صندوقها كه آن را حيل هندسى خوانند، و طلب رياست و سلطنت و مهترى كردن، و فرمان دادن، و نام نيكو اندوختن تا بدانند كه او دانا است به علوم مانند نجوم و طب

### Summary of the Sixth Epistle on the Soul Concerning Love  
In the Name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful  

Herein is presented an epistle on love, wherein several discourses are given, akin to a symposium. We have recorded as much as we have understood, focusing on that which is closer to natural matters. Know that love is among the most complex states of the rational soul, and its definition—where we shall delineate its boundaries—will be expounded in detail.  

The origin of love arises from natural dispositions. But first, one must understand that within man reside two faculties: one is desire (*shahwat*), and the other is wrath (*ghadab*), which are hot and dry in nature. Although every faculty in man originates from innate heat, the essence of desire is coldness and moisture. When desire is in motion, all human faculties—such as those in the heart, liver, and brain—assist it. Wrath, too, is a form of desire, yet it should not be called *shahwat* in the same way as the desire for copulation, for the latter is an animal pleasure involving two individuals who both derive enjoyment, or at least one does. The definition of wrath is not thus, for it is said that wrath is the boiling of the heart’s blood and the seeking of vengeance—and this definition is not the same as the other. All sages agree that two things whose definitions do not correspond should not be conflated.  

-275-  

The innate disposition of desire in man is the cause of procreation, while wrath opposes it. For procreation is good, whereas vengeance is pure evil. Since these two faculties are opposites, they cannot coexist in the same place. If it happens that they do converge somewhere, the cause is an excess of carnal desire rather than wrath, for if vengeance were dominant, wrath would prevail. When wrath exceeds desire, it inhibits the movement of desire. Wrath is likened to fire, and desire to water. If water prevails, it extinguishes the fire, but if fire prevails, the water evaporates and vanishes. If we speak of these faculties in isolation, it is evident that the power of fire surpasses that of water. By this analogy, we say that the master of desire can extinguish the power of desire with the power of wrath, and the master of wrath can extinguish the power of wrath with the power of desire.  

Thus, we say that love is never devoid of three aspects: it may pertain solely to the body, solely to the soul, or to the partnership of body and soul.  

That which pertains solely to the body is the pursuit of pleasure in accordance with nature, whether with the beloved or another, or the consumption of delicious foods of whatever kind the heart desires, or the wearing of fine garments as the heart wishes, or the amassing of silver and storing it away—all these relate to the body.  

That which pertains to the soul is the acquisition of knowledge, the abandonment of base traits, the recognition of one’s origin, the comprehension of intelligibles, the discernment of celestial essences, the attainment of knowledge of the Necessary Being, the perfection of intellect and soul, the pursuit of salvation through righteous sciences, and the accumulation of provisions for the Hereafter and virtuous deeds.  

That which pertains to the partnership of body and soul is the pursuit of beauty, the enjoyment of music, the drinking of wine, the use of perfumes and baths, the cultivation of sciences such as music and its instruments, the mathematical sciences like geometry and mechanical devices (known as *hiyal-i handasi*), the quest for leadership, sovereignty, and mastery, the issuing of commands, and the accumulation of a good name so that one is known for mastery of sciences like astrology, medicine,  

-276-  

dream interpretation, arithmetic, and the like.  

These three aspects we have mentioned arise from the celestial configuration at the time of conception, birth, and the annual revolution—all of which are detailed in astrological texts. Here, we shall recount only what is relevant.  

Know that at the time of conception and birth, one or two planets—or more—must be dominant. If Saturn is dominant, all of that individual’s love will be directed toward great deeds, contemplation of subtle sciences, the uncovering of celestial secrets and their truths, perfect honesty and friendship with friends, fierce enmity with enemies, and a yearning for the Hereafter.  

If Saturn and Jupiter are dominant, all of his love will be for worship, asceticism, and adherence to divine law, kindness toward God’s creation, extreme devotion to the Necessary Being, anticipation of death, and the practice of piety and abstinence. If such a person seeks leadership, he may attain it, but his excessive piety will render it unsuitable for him.  

If Saturn and Mars are dominant, this person will forever love vengeance, issue unjust commands, pursue carnal desires naturally, and adore manliness and courage, and the like.  

Similarly, the temperaments of the planets must blend. For instance, if Saturn, Venus, and the Moon are dominant in a nativity, all of his love will be for copulation, the pursuit of its pleasure, and the means to attain it.  

If the Sun and Mars are dominant, all of his love will be for wrath, dominance, and command.  

If the Sun, Jupiter, and Mercury are dominant, all of his love will be for speech, holding assemblies, and mastering rhetorical techniques.  

-277-  

Let us provide an example for clarity. If Saturn, Venus, and the Moon are dominant, his desire will be entirely for the stomach. Thus, if Venus or the Moon is in its cycle, this person will experience no revolution, for at the time of his revolution, these three planets will be in full strength—essential, cyclical, and revolutionary. His love for the stomach will be so complete that he may eat to the point of death or be killed for his gluttony.  

As we said, if Mars, Venus, and the Moon are dominant, all of his love will be for copulation, such that these three planets are strong in their cycles, nativity, and revolution, positioned in places that incite such acts. This person will be consumed by carnal love and may ultimately perish because of it.  

As we said, if the Sun and Mars are dominant—and if these two are strong in their cycles, conjunction, conception, nativity, and revolution—this person will be so consumed by anger, vengeance, and hatred that he may commit an act for which he is executed.  

If the Sun, Jupiter, and Mercury are dominant—as mentioned earlier—and these three are strong, this person will be so enamored with speech that he may claim prophethood, saying, "God Almighty has spoken to me," and often he may speak truthfully.  

Let us provide two examples for two scenarios to clarify both. We say love is either for the sake of God or for the beloved. First, if a person’s nativity has Venus and the Moon strong in conception, birth, revolution, and conjunction, the native will have a delicate disposition and will constantly fall in love with someone for their beauty or for carnal desire. Moreover, this is fundamentally a cause of affection in nativities. In this native’s chart, the Sun and Moon must necessarily be in favorable positions. Thus, if a man gazes upon someone or befriends another whose nativity has the two luminaries strong, and if in his own nativity they are in trine or sextile, their friendship will be profound.  

If one is a man and the other a woman or a youth, a great love will arise. If the eleventh house of the lover’s chart is the beloved’s ascendant, the love will become manifest and intense. Similarly,  

-278-  

if both are women or both are youths—even if there is no carnal aspect—their friendship will be immense, to the point that they would spare neither head nor soul for one another.  

But if the nativity opposes this—being in square, or the seventh, tenth, sixth, eighth, or twelfth house—these two will be bitter enemies.  

Regarding the state of love, if some conditions are met but not all, there will be affection and friendship, but not love. Likewise, in enmity, if some conditions are met but not all, there will be harm but not hatred.  

This is of immense importance in astrology, to the extent that even if there were a face so beautiful that none in that age could rival it, and a person whose nativity dictated perpetual love, yet if the two luminaries were in square in their charts, they would inevitably despise one another.  

If Jupiter, the Sun, and Mercury dominate the ascendant of conception, birth, conjunction, cycle, and revolution, and if this person’s nativity has the ascendant in conjunction, the love of divine knowledge and virtue will necessarily arise in him. He will call others to faith, attain great prophethood, manifest kingship, and be so overwhelmed by divine love that he will establish religious law and tradition, summon creation to God, fear no calamity, attain the highest ranks of knowledge, compose divine scriptures, and, if killed for the love of God, will rejoice and perform great miracles.  

This is love, and it does not exceed these three types. All of this stems from the influence of the stars, for the Necessary Being is exalted above love. And God knows best.
