### [Translation Preface]  
*In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.*  

Praise and gratitude be to that God whose existence is necessary, while all else besides Him exists contingently. Whatever exists contingently is His creation. For every thing, He separately ordained a cause among genera and species, leaving nothing without a cause. Whatever bears the name of existence is His effect, and He is the cause of all beings—whether formal, spiritual, sensible, or intelligible. All these exist through His orderly act and subsist by Him, while He subsists by Himself. He is exalted beyond all limits, definitions, positions, natures, and directions—for whatever possesses these five attributes is contingent, whereas He is the Necessary Existence.  

He is the Living, and His life is none other than Himself; the Knowing, and His knowledge is none other than Himself; the Powerful, and His power is none other than Himself. He encompasses all beings, and His encompassment is His knowledge. When He willed to bring the world into existence from nothingness, through His grace—without motion, rest, or change derived from His own essence, and not by compulsion from another—He brought forth a substance. From that substance, He produced motion, and from motion, rest. Thus, motion became the agent of heat, and rest the agent of cold. Then heat produced dryness, and cold produced moisture. Through His generosity, He created the four elements from these four principles: from heat, fire; from moisture, air; from cold, water; and from dryness, earth.  

He bestowed emanation upon the primal substance, and from that emanation produced another substance, from which He fashioned absolute body. From absolute body, He formed the heavens and the stars. He arranged fire, air, water, and earth in order as the center of the heavens, making the heavens the realm of spirits and the earth the realm of bodies. The heavens became the abode of angels, and the earth the abode of plants, minerals, and animals. In these two realms, He created countless wonders, subordinating the lower world to the higher, and both worlds to the primal substance.  

From among the many genera and species, He chose humankind, adorning them with intellect, discernment, and reason, and combining within them the powers of both worlds. From among them, He sent prophets and dispatched angels to those prophets. May God’s blessings be upon them, for they guided us to the straight path, revealed God’s law plainly, and sought our felicity through it. They spoke to each according to their capacity—to the elite, they unveiled wisdom plainly, while to the common folk, they spoke in symbols. Through both ways, they led creation to God the Exalted, purging the world of disbelief and corruption. May God’s blessings be upon them, especially upon our Prophet, the Seal of the Prophets and the Master of the Messengers, and upon his family and companions. And after them, may God’s blessings be upon the philosophers and the God-knowing sages, the masters of reasoning, the solvers of difficulties, and the guides to the straight path. Blessings be upon all of them, and upon the souls of the pure and the leaders.  
  

Know then that there are many books, most of them in the Arabic tongue and few in Persian. There is no delight in these books - among them *The Song of the Stars*, *The Book of the Name of the Lord*, *Marzuban-nama*, and the like . We have found no book encompassing all branches of philosophy—mathematics, logic, physics, and metaphysics—save for the *Encyclopedia of Knowledge* (*Danishnama*), though its language is exceedingly obscure, filled mostly with allusions and symbols. The (*Mujmal al-Hikma*) is a compendium but it is likewise cryptic and burdened with superfluities. We have seen this book translated into Persian in one or two places, yet the symbols remained unresolved and the redundancies retained.

Thus, it happened that this humble was commanded to render the book into Dari Persian, removing all superfluities, clarifying its symbols, and elevating its allusions to explicit speech. I proceeded according to this command, seeking success in obedience. This is the first epistle, serving as an introduction to the book of *Arithmetic*.
