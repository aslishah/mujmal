بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم 
سپاس و ستايش آن خداى را كه واجب الوجودست. و هرچه جز وى است ممكن الوجودست، و هرچه ممكن الوجودست كردۀ اوست. و هر چيزى را جدايگانه از اجناس و انواع علتى پديد كرد و هيچ چيز بى‌علتى نيافريد. و هرچه نام هستى بر وى افتاد معلول وى است، و او علت همۀ موجودات است، صورتى و معنوى و حسّى و عقلى. و اين همه به ترتيب فعل وى است و به وى قايم، و او به خود قايم. و از هرچه حدّ و رسم و وضع و طبع و جهت است منزه است، و هرچه اين پنج صفت وى را باشد آن چيز ممكن الوجود بود، و وى واجب الوجودست. و حىّ‌ است و حيات او جز وى نيست، و عالم است و علم او جز وى نيست، و قادرست و قدرت او جز وى نيست، و محيط‍‌ است به موجودات، و احاطت او علم وى است. و چون خواست كه عالم را از ناچيز پديد آورد به عنايت خويش بى‌حركتى و سكونى و تغيّرى از وجود خويش، نه از الزام ديگرى، جوهرى پديد كرد، و از آن جوهر حركت پيدا آورد، ] و از حركت سكون، تا حركت فاعل
-27-
 حرارت شد، و سكون فاعل برودت. و باز حرارت فاعل يبوست شد، و برودت فاعل رطوبت. و به جود خويش ازين چهار اصل چهار عناصر آفريد: از حرارت آتش آفريد، و از رطوبت هوا، و از برودت آب، و از يبوست خاك. و جوهر اصل را فيض داد، و از آن فيض جوهرى ديگر كرد، و از آن جوهر جسم مطلق كرد، و از جسم مطلق سماوات و كواكب ساخت. و آتش و هوا و آب و زمين را به ترتيب مركز سماوات كرد، و سماوات را عالم ارواح ساخت، و زمين را عالم اجساد، و سماوات را محل ملايكه كرد، و زمين را محل نبات و معدن و حيوان. و چندين هزار عجايب درين هر دو عالم پديد آورد، و عالم سفلى را در حكم عالم علوى كرد، و هر دو عالم را در حكم جوهر اصلى. و از چندين اجناس و انواع بشر را برگزيد، و وى را به عقل و راى و تمييز بياراست، و از قوت هر دو عالم در وى تركيب كرد. و ازيشان پيغمبران فرستاد و به پيغمبران ملايكه فرستاد. درود خداى بريشان باد كه ما را راه راست نمودند و شريعت خداى آشكارا كردند و بدان بهروزى ما خواستند، و با هركس سخن در خور وى گفتند. با خاصّه حكمت آشكارا كردند و با عامّه رمز گفتند و بدين هر دو راه به خداى تعالى نمودند، و عالم را از كفر و بدى پاك كردند. درود خداى بريشان باد، خاصه بر پيغمبر ما كه خاتم النبيين و سيد المرسلين است، و بر خاندان و ياران وى. و بعد ازيشان درود خداى بر فيلسوفان و حكيمان خداى‌شناس باد كه خداوندان قياس‌اند و حل‌كنندۀ مشكلها و نمايندۀ راه راست. درود بر همگنان باد و بر جان پاكان و پيشوايان.

اما بعد بدانيد كه كتابها بسيارست و بيشتر به لغت تازى است و اندكى به لغت پارسى. و در آن كتابها هيچ حظى نيست مانند سرود اختران و كتاب نام بارخداى و مرزبان نامه و آنچه بدين ماند. و ما هيچ كتاب نيافتيم از آنچه در حكمت به‌كار آيد كه از رياضى و منطقى و طبيعى و الهى جمله در وى باشد مگر دانش‌نامه، و آن به لفظى سخت مشكل است و بيشتر اشارات است و رموز. و كتاب مجمل الحكمة مجموع است، ولكن همچنين مرموز است، و در آن حشو بسيار. و ما يك دو جاى ديديم كه اين كتاب را به پارسى نقل كرده بودند و همچنان مرموز فرو گذاشته و حشو به‌جاى مانده. پس چنين اتفاق افتاد كه اين ضعيف را فرمودند كه اين كتاب را به پارسى درى نقل كند و هرچه حشوست از آن دور كند و هرچه مرموز است آشكار گرداند و از حد رمز به تصريح رساند. بر موجب فرمان پيش گرفت تا فرمان‌بردارى توفيق بار آورد. و اين اول رسالتى است كه همچون مدخلى است در كتاب ارثماطيقى.

### | [Translator’s Preface]  
In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful

Praise and gratitude be to that God who is the Necessary Existent, while all besides Him are contingent existents, and all contingent existents are His creations. For each thing, He separately fashioned a cause from among genera and species, and He created nothing without a cause. Whatever bears the name of existence is His effect, and He is the cause of all beings—whether formal, spiritual, sensible, or intelligible. All of these exist by His orderly act and subsist through Him, while He subsists through Himself. He is exalted above all limits, definitions, positions, natures, and directions, for anything possessing these five attributes is a contingent existent—whereas He is the Necessary Existent.  

He is the Living, and His life is none other than Himself; He is the Knowing, and His knowledge is none other than Himself; He is the Powerful, and His power is none other than Himself. He encompasses all existents, and His encompassing is His knowledge. When He willed to bring the world into existence from nothingness, through His own grace—without motion, rest, or change from His essence, and not by the compulsion of another—He produced a substance. From that substance, He brought forth motion, and from motion, rest. Thus, motion became the agent of heat, and rest the agent of cold. Then heat became the agent of dryness, and cold the agent of moisture. Through His generosity, He fashioned the four elements from these four principles: from heat, He created fire; from moisture, air; from cold, water; and from dryness, earth.  

He bestowed emanation upon the primal substance, and from that emanation, He produced another substance. From that substance, He formed absolute body, and from absolute body, He fashioned the heavens and the stars. He arranged fire, air, water, and earth in order as the center of the heavens, and He made the heavens the realm of spirits and the earth the realm of bodies. The heavens became the abode of angels, and the earth the abode of plants, minerals, and animals. He created countless wonders in both these worlds, subordinating the lower world to the higher, and both worlds to the primal substance.  

From among the many genera and species, He chose humankind, adorning them with intellect, discernment, and reason, and combining within them the powers of both worlds. He sent prophets among them and dispatched angels to those prophets. May God’s blessings be upon them, for they showed us the straight path, revealed God’s law, and desired our felicity through it. They spoke to each according to their capacity: to the elite, they unveiled wisdom plainly, while to the common folk, they spoke in symbols—yet by both paths, they guided to God the Exalted, purging the world of disbelief and evil. May God’s blessings be upon them, especially upon our Prophet, the Seal of the Prophets and the Master of the Messengers, and upon his family and companions. And after them, may God’s blessings be upon the philosophers and the God-knowing sages, who are masters of reasoning, solvers of difficulties, and guides to the straight path. Blessings upon them all, and upon the souls of the pure and the leaders.

Know that there are many books out there, mostly in the Arabic language, and very few in Persian. But there is nothing exciting about those [Persian] works, such as _Surūd-i Akhtarān_ (Song of the Stars), Kitāb-i nām-i bār-i Khudāy (The Book of the Name of God), Marzbānnāma,_ and the like, except for _Dānishnāma,_ but its language is very difficult and full of allusions and riddles. The _Mujmal al-ḥikma_ is a compendium, but is similarly cryptic. Besides, there is a lot of redundancy in it. We have seen one or two translations of this book into Persian, following the same cryptic fashion also keeping the redundant stuff. It happened that this wretched was commanded to translate this book into Dari Persian, and to remove anything that is redundant, and decode anything that is in riddle, and to bring it from the level of mystery to clarity. I proceeded according to this command, hoping for the grace of obedience. This is the first epistle, serving as an introduction to the Book of Arithmetic.
